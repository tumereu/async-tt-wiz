# Async play

Playing without waiting for each other: the wait while the other player must act, assumed answers and the Assume
button, the information rule and its game settings, the unconfirmed mark, automation as button presses, branches and
lines of play, moves made on an out-of-date board, and playing on an assumed keep in the opening.

## How it works

The two players are rarely online at the same time, so the game never makes you wait longer than it must. While the
other player must act, a band under their hand says what they are doing, and the main button may offer Assume: your
device presumes their answer (usually a pass, sometimes their blocks or their opening keep) and you go on. Your
"Assume resolves" setting decides where your phone assumes by itself; anything your phone does for you counts exactly
as a press of a button. An assumption is a conditional pre-move, not a decision made for them: when their real answer
is the same it is confirmed, and when it differs the game branches, taking back what came after and going on from
their answer on a new line of play. While your play rests on answers they haven't confirmed, a violet dot marks the
main button and nothing you do may show you hidden cards or random results, unless the game's settings allow it. A move
made on a board the game has since moved past is made again on the newer board when the same decision still waits
there, otherwise it isn't taken. The starting player may play turn 1 on an assumed keep while the other player still
decides on their opening.

## Waiting for the other player

- **TRN-066** The wait: while the other player must act (online or away), a semi-transparent dark band right under their hand says why, in white text of up to 3 lines. It shows in landscape too, never takes a touch, and doesn't show while a play is shown (HIS-112). (r64)
- **TRN-150** The wait band sits in portrait under the other player's row, which holds their hand (ZON-001), and on the desktop table at the battlefields' top, from their seat header to the battlefields' right edge (WEB-016). It runs edge to edge, fading out toward both sides only over a narrow margin, with every line of its words on its solid middle, in portrait and landscape alike. (r64)
- **TRN-151** The wait's words: "<name> may respond to <spell>" (an ability as "<source>'s ability", STK-046), "<name> may act · <step>", "<name> is declaring blockers", "<name> is deciding on a mulligan", "<name> is choosing · <card>" (the card only when you can see it), or "Waiting for <name>". (r64)
- **TRN-152** When the other player is behind you (you went ahead on assumptions about them they haven't confirmed), "Waiting for <name>" says where they are instead: "Bob is at turn 5 · their combat" (a combat step as "combat", any other step by its name, "your" in your own turn). (r64)
- **TRN-004** While the other player must act, the main button (TRN-063) holds nothing where nothing of yours follows before their next real action: their own main phase with an empty Stack, their declaration of attackers, their choices and, before your own opening is done, their mulligan (the other things it holds then: TRN-153, TRN-161, TRN-154). (r76)
- **TRN-155** Where the main button holds nothing while the other player must act, its place shows a quiet status in plain words instead of a button (grey words, nothing to tap, so the pause doesn't read as lag): "Bob's answer…" (they may respond or act), "Bob is choosing…", "Bob is blocking…", "Bob's mulligan…", "Bob's move…" (their main phase or their attack). (r76)
- **TRN-156** No quiet status shows while your own events are on their way, or while an undo request or a result suggestion is answered in its dialog. (r76)

## Assumed answers

- **HIS-096** Assumed answers: at the other player's priority points the device assumes their pass (Assume resolves) or blocks (Assume blockers) so the game goes on, never at their own main phase. Their blocks are assumed only from the main button's Assume blockers screen (CMB-040). Conceptually an assumption is a conditional pre-move, not a decision made for them (I-D57). (r76)
- **HIS-175** A priority point belongs to the player who began it: whoever made the last real input since the step began (a cast, a land, an activation, an attack or block declaration, an answer to a decision; an assumed block is the assuming player's), or the active player when nobody did (a beginning-of-step trigger, an upkeep). Passes never count, nor whose turn it is or whose spell or ability is on top of the Stack: you attack and they block, the point after it is theirs. (r76)
- **HIS-176** At a point you began, the other player's pass is assumed at once where your "Assume resolves" setting assumes it or their own response mode would pass it. At a point they began only their mode decides, your setting ignored, so where their mode stops your phone waits for them (STK-036); their playback likewise stops where their own mode stops (HIS-095). (r76)
- **HIS-177** The other player's Quick mode is judged on their real hand: by choosing Quick they give that out, and no setting of yours uses their hidden cards. (r76)
- **HIS-178** Where nothing assumes their answer it is a wait with Assume on the main button (TRN-070), at their points too, so you can leave a spell up for a player you expect to respond or go on without them. Never their presence (online or away) decides it. Anything else of theirs is a real wait, their opening-hand actions included; TRN-071 applies, with the game's assume reveal settings (LOB-135). (r76)

## The "Assume resolves" setting

- **HIS-179** The "Assume resolves" setting, defined from the response modes (it changes with them), counts at your points. Never assumes nothing by itself: a wait wherever they get priority, as Control stops, also at your end step and right after your attack. (r76)
- **HIS-180** Assume resolves Normal assumes wherever Normal mode would pass, and their pause at your end step and right after you attack, before blocks (nothing of yours follows before their next real answer, so it never takes anything back). (r76)
- **HIS-181** Assume resolves When they have no mana assumes as Normal, and also at their pauses while what your table shows gives them no untapped mana source, no mana in their pool and no ability they could activate (the cards in their hand don't count). Always assumes every pass. (r76)
- **HIS-182** So at your points, against a player in Normal or Quick, Assume resolves Never and Normal behave the same; against one in Control, Never waits for them and Normal doesn't. (r76)

## The Assume button

- **TRN-153** While the other player must act, the main button holds "Assume resolves" (something on the Stack, STK-028) or "Assume pass" (an empty Stack: nothing to resolve, as after their blocks) where they hold priority anywhere else and your phone doesn't assume their pass by itself (HIS-096). (r76)
- **TRN-161** While the other player must act, the main button holds "Assume no blockers" / "Assume blockers" while they declare blockers (CMB-040; nothing where their blocks can't be assumed), and "Assume keep" while they decide on their opening once your own is done (TRN-027). (r76)
- **TRN-070** The wait's Assume (TRN-153, TRN-161), wherever the other player's answer may be assumed (HIS-096), is in the Assume violet with the Assume icon (TRN-063) and shows while they must act, whether they are online or away, with no timeout. (r76)
- **TRN-157** The wait's Assume shows also at points the other player began, where only their mode decides whether your phone waits (HIS-176). Unless "Assume resolves" is Never, their pause at your end step and right after you attack is never a wait (HIS-180). (r76)
- **TRN-158** Holding "Assume resolves" resolves the whole Stack (TRN-017); holding any other Assume is its tap. (r76)
- **TRN-154** While the other player must act, the main button holds a greyed Assume where assuming would show you hidden cards or a random result (TRN-071), and "Cancel request" while your own undo request is open (HIS-100). (r76)
- **STK-028** The other player sees the wait "Bob may respond to Storm Spark" (TRN-066) with "Assume resolves" on the main button where it may be assumed (a tap assumes Bob lets it resolve), and no Pass for Bob (TRN-004). (r54)

## Automation is button presses

- **HIS-109** Automation is button presses: your phone's own pass for you is exactly a press of the main button (Resolve, or a step move such as "To draw step"), and its assumption of the other player's answer exactly a press of Assume (TRN-070), which you may also press by hand at any of their points, theirs too. The presses a held Resolve or Assume makes (TRN-017) are your phone's own presses too; a tap of yours ends that chain. (r59)
- **HIS-187** So the player an assumption is about stays at that decision and may act there: passing confirms it, anything else branches (HIS-097). That includes a live move of theirs that reaches the server after the other phone's assumption (a second spell while holding priority in Control after you pressed Assume, or a response cast as their phone assumed your pass): their phone makes it there as their answer, without a note, never refused as "That wasn't taken". (r59)
- **HIS-211** A pass that is just what the other phone assumed confirms the assumption, with nothing on the passing player's table taken back (SYS-077). (r59)
- **HIS-188** A pass by hand looks like one made by itself: when the other player in Control holds priority after their own cast and then taps Resolve, your phone shows what it shows when a lower mode of theirs passed by itself: the same timeline events and texts (no pass entry, HIS-101), the same single "your move" notification, the same catch-up playback and its stops (HIS-104). (r59)
- **HIS-189** A tap of the main button (Resolve, a "To …" step move or Assume, TRN-063) that reaches your phone just after it made that same press by itself at the same point (e.g. a Resolve press racing the board where Quick passes for you over your own next ability on the Stack, which itself shows no Resolve, TRN-119) is that press: nothing more is made (never the next Stack item's resolution) and nothing is told (SYS-011). (r59)

## While your play rests on assumptions

- **TRN-068** The unconfirmed mark: while actions of yours rest on answers the other player hasn't confirmed yet, a small violet dot (no number) sits at the main button's corner; a tap or hold says "<name> hasn't seen your last moves yet; if <name> answers differently, they'll be taken back". Only you see it. (r50)
- **TRN-067** Information rule: while your line rests on an unconfirmed assumption, nothing you do or assume may show you a card you couldn't see (the other player's hand or library, face-down cards, random discards; never your own library) or a random result (coin, die, random choice), apart from what the game's settings allow (LOB-135). (r58)
- **TRN-159** An action the information rule forbids is refused before it shows: the card shakes and holding it says "Wait for the other player to catch up: this would show you hidden cards or a random result." The server checks the same for cards, with the game's settings. (r58)
- **TRN-071** Under the information rule your phone's own passes after an action of yours stop before one that would show you something (you keep priority there), and an assumption that would show you something isn't made. A reveal the game's settings allow (LOB-135) neither stops nor greys anything. (r74)
- **TRN-160** Where an assumption isn't made for the information rule, the wait reads "Waiting for <name> · going on would show you hidden cards or a random result" (TRN-066) and the main button holds the wait's Assume ("Assume resolves", "Assume pass") greyed: a tap shakes it and holding it says "Assuming this would show you hidden cards or a random result: wait for <name>." (r74)
- **LOB-135** Game settings, in the lobby's "Game settings" card (below Starting player) and in the game from Table actions → Game settings (TRN-010): "Allow assumes that reveal the opponent's library" (on by default) and "Allow assumes that reveal hands" (off by default), switches that relax the information rule (TRN-067) for both players. (r60)
- **LOB-152** With "Allow assumes that reveal the opponent's library", cards of the other player's library (a reveal from its top, a search's result shown) never hold back an assumption or your own actions on an unconfirmed assumption: every line of play reshuffles the unknown parts of the libraries (HIS-042). (r60)
- **LOB-153** With "Allow assumes that reveal hands", cards of the other player's hand (a reveal, a card shown or discarded at random) don't hold them back either. Face-down cards and random results (coins, dice, other random choices) stay held back. (r60)
- **LOB-154** Both players see the current values of the game settings; only the game's host (who created it) changes them, before and during the game, and the other player's switches are greyed under "Only <host> can change these.". A change counts at once for both phones and the server's check, outside undo and history. (r60)

## Branches and lines of play

- **HIS-097** Branch: when the other player's real answer differs from what was assumed, the game goes on from their answer and the line after it is discarded, never seen by them; your table goes back to the branch point with a note "<name> responded" ("blocked differently", "took a mulligan"). A new line starts (HIS-042). There is no other way to branch; at an assumed keep the other player's other opening move re-applies your line first (HIS-115). (r76)
- **HIS-185** When real actions of yours were taken back, the branch note names the first, in its event text's words (HIS-091): "<name> responded — your attack was taken back" ("your Storm Spark", "your blocks were", "your End turn"). (r76)
- **HIS-186** Your phone's own passes and assumptions (a press of Resolve or Assume is the same, HIS-109) are no action of yours, so a branch that took back only them says just "<name> responded", and the rewind animation (HIS-106) plays only when the board shown changes. (r76)
- **HIS-106** A branch goes back to the branch point step by step with timeline mode's look (HIS-008), each step followed by the Rewind pause (HIS-022), with the note. Meanwhile a tap on the table doesn't pause it, and Back and the menu (Timeline too) do nothing. (r60)
- **HIS-190** After its take-back a branch stops at the branch point as playback stops: your table is live there, looking like live play, with the centre text "<name> responded differently from here · Tap to continue" (HIS-158), whose tap plays the other player's new events back as catch-up playback with its stops (HIS-095). When nothing of theirs follows the branch point, or playback stops there anyway for a decision of yours, there is no extra stop. (r60)
- **HIS-191** Live (the game open, in sight, at the head) a branch's take-back plays at once. Coming back to it (the app in the background, the game not open, a notification, a cold start) opens the game exactly as you left it, and nothing moves until you tap: the centre text says "<name> responded differently · Tap to watch" (HIS-158), and the tap plays the take-back, up to its stop at the branch point. (r60)
- **HIS-042** Every line of play (the game's start, every branch, every rewind including free undo) starts by reshuffling the unknown parts of both libraries, shown nowhere; a card whose place someone knows (put on top or bottom by a known action, looked at or revealed where it lies, a kept-revealed top) keeps it. (r76)
- **HIS-192** Random results (shuffles, coins, dice, random choices) follow from the game's secret, the line and the player whose result it is (the library's owner for a shuffle and a line's reshuffle, the flipping or rolling player, the chooser of a random choice): the same input on a line gives the same result, a new line new ones, and one player's results never move another's (a mulligan of the other player's doesn't change what you draw). (r76)
- **HIS-043** There is no branch archiving and no "Make current" action anywhere; lines left by a branch are kept for problem reports and shown to nobody. (r48)

## Moves made on an out-of-date board

- **SYS-011** An action is made on the event shown to its player: the server makes it again there and refuses it when the rules don't allow it (SYS-057). (r60)
- **SYS-057** Every event's engine part is the engine's own inputs and reversible changes; the server makes it again from the inputs and refuses it when it isn't a legal input there. (r60)
- **SYS-130** Changing a player's response mode or Autotap is no event, and neither is part of the board. Every event records the response mode it was made under (STK-036); Autotap is no part of any event. (r60)
- **SYS-012** The main line changes only by a branch (HIS-097), a rewind (HIS-099) or a concession while behind (TRN-064). (r60)
- **SYS-131** When the game has moved past the event an action was made on, the server takes it only in these cases, never otherwise branching, and refuses anything else as out of date: the other player's table-only events in between (SYS-132), an assumption of your answer or the very move you assumed (SYS-077), End turn (SYS-076) and a concession, which branches (TRN-064). (r60)
- **SYS-132** An event made on an event the game has moved past is made after the events in between, on the newest board, when they are only the other player's table-only events (combat staging, early answers, result suggestions) and none touches what it touches. Anything outside the out-of-date cases (SYS-131) is refused and handled on your phone as SYS-135 to SYS-138 say. (r60)
- **SYS-077** When the server took the other player's event before your move, your phone makes your move again on it, without a note, when the same decision still waits for you there (SYS-135), each later move of yours where your phone's own steps after the one before leave it. When neither this nor SYS-133 applies, it tells you once (SYS-138). (r58)
- **SYS-133** When that event of theirs is their device's assumption of your answer at the very point you moved on (HIS-096), you are still at that decision: your move is your answer to it there (HIS-109), the same answer confirming the assumption and any other answer branching (HIS-097), also without a note. (r58)
- **SYS-134** When your move is that same answer, the very move their device assumed (nothing more in it), it is simply the assumption's confirmation, never refused and with no new event: your board, anything open on it (a panel, a choice) and your later moves made on it stay exactly as they are, with nothing taken back, played back or made again. The same when your device's assumption of their answer meets their own same answer. (r58)
- **SYS-076** End turn may be made anywhere in your own turn: tapped on a board your phone has moved on from, it ends the turn on the newer board while that is still the same turn of yours and nothing on the Stack is the other player's that you never saw (e.g. tapped right after To combat while your phone steps on by itself). (r52)
- **SYS-135** A move made on an event the game has moved past, like a tap on a board your phone has already moved past (its own steps, or the other player's event not yet shown), is made on the newer board when the same decision still waits for you there; an Undo also when only your own and table-only events came in between (HIS-107). (r60)
- **SYS-136** When such a move is the very press your phone made right there by itself (its pass for you, its assumption, HIS-109), it is that press and goes quietly, with nothing made again. (r60)
- **SYS-137** Otherwise (a new Stack object, another step, a chosen target gone, a hidden card shown, a quick second tap) such a move isn't taken, with the note of SYS-138. (r60)
- **SYS-138** When your phone can't make your action again on the newer board, it says once: "That wasn't taken: the game moved on first. The table shows where it is now." Your device's own passes and assumptions that lose such a race go quietly. (r60)
- **SYS-139** Any other refusal of your own action says nothing: what it was made on shakes (TBL-118: the card cast, played or activated; otherwise the main button), the board updates and you act again. (r60)
- **SYS-078** Notes on the table (a branch's "<name> responded — your later actions were taken back", SYS-012's "That wasn't taken: …") show one at a time near the top of the table for 6 s or until tapped; a note told while another one shows waits for it and shows next, never replacing it at once. (r53)

## Playing on an assumed keep in the opening

- **HIS-183** The other player's opening keep is assumed only from the main button's Assume keep (TRN-070), by the starting player once their own opening is done, never by your phone itself. An assumed keep that a re-apply makes again with your line (HIS-115) needs no new press. (r76)
- **HIS-184** Where the other player's last mulligan still owes cards to the bottom, an assumed keep puts a stand-in choice there (the last cards of their hand in hand order), hidden from you like their hand. (r76)
- **HIS-115** Re-apply: while you play on your assumed keep of the other player's opening (TRN-027), each opening move of theirs other than that keep (a mulligan, a bottom choice other than the stand-in, an action "any time you could mulligan") takes your line back, puts their move in its place and makes your line again on top of it at once, action by action (HIS-116), with no new line. You keep playing throughout, never waiting for their opening to end, and Undo works as anywhere else. (r76)
- **HIS-116** "Comes out the same": an action of yours made again on the other player's new opening move stands when Automatum accepts it, the game after it is the same as far as you could tell (what you can see, the open decision and what the action did that you could see; the other player's hand and library, their order and their counts don't count by themselves, an effect that counts them does) and it shows you the same cards. (r76)
- **HIS-212** When your line is made again, random results are worked out again from each player's own stream (HIS-192), and an event stands whole or not at all. (r76)
- **HIS-193** When everything comes out the same your table changes only where their move differs for you (their hand and library counts) and doesn't go back. After a mulligan a note "<name> took a mulligan · your turn stands" fades at the top (the branch note's chip, no rewind); nothing shows for another move. (r76)
- **HIS-194** From the first action of yours that doesn't come out the same, the rest is taken back as a branch (HIS-097, HIS-106): your table goes back to the last action that stands with "<name> took a mulligan — your Ember Augury was taken back", a new line starts there (HIS-042) and you get the branch notification (LOB-116). When nothing comes out the same (their move changed something you see, such as a card put onto the battlefield) the whole turn goes back. (r76)
- **HIS-195** An action of yours on its way while a re-apply happens is made again on the re-made line when the same decision waits there, else dropped with SYS-012's note. (r76)
- **HIS-196** The other player's undo inside their opening back past what your assumed keep rested on (their bottom choice; their mulligan, with your approval, HIS-087) is such a move too: your line is made again on the point it lands on, the first event made starting the undo's new line (HIS-042, so your later draws may come out differently), and what doesn't come out the same is taken back with "<name> responded — your Ember Echo was taken back". Their playback stays before your assumed keep. (r76)
- **HIS-117** The other player's lag: while you decide on your opening after the starting player assumed your keep, your game stays in your opening as any game you haven't caught up on: nothing of their turn shows anywhere (the table, life, timeline, event chip and texts, wait texts, the Stack, the game list row, notifications and reminders, the Undo dialog), and nothing says your keep was assumed. (r76)
- **HIS-197** While you decide on your opening, the starting player's keep tells you "<name> is ready" (LOB-114) and nothing more arrives while they play. Your Keep confirms their assumption and their turn reaches you as catch-up (HIS-094); what a re-apply took back never reaches you. Accepted tells: their presence and their chat. (r76)
