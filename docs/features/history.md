# History

The game timeline: timeline mode and playback, catching up on what happened (the paused catch-up, playback stops, assumed answers, branches when an answer differs), lines of play, undo and undo requests, event texts and icons, and how events are grouped by turn.

### Play mode

- **HIS-001** In play mode, live and in catch-up playback (HIS-094), the bottom bar has no event strip: it holds only the controls, and its room goes to the board. Nothing past your local head (HIS-032) shows anywhere. (r55)
- **HIS-004** Table actions' "Timeline" entry (TRN-010) is the only way into timeline mode; it opens paused at the newest event shown to you, and the timeline ends there. (r55)

### Timeline mode

- **HIS-006** Live play, catch-up playback (HIS-094) and timeline mode are three different things. Coming back with the other player's events past your local head starts the catch-up (HIS-094); with only your own events since, the game opens live. Timeline mode starts only when you enter it (HIS-004), also during playback, which then waits paused: leaving timeline mode returns to where you were (live, or the catch-up paused with its centre text). Opponent actions, hold-inspect and menu actions keep the table live. (r53)
- **HIS-007** In portrait the hand, piles and life sit directly above the bottom bar in play mode and above the timeline panel in timeline mode; the two cross-fade in about 200 ms. There the timeline panel is taller (its strip above Previous / Auto / Next), so entering timeline mode moves them up by the strip's height and leaving moves them back; in landscape your life, piles and hand sit in the bottom bar itself (TBL-009) and the timeline panel's strip and Previous / Auto / Next take the controls' fixed-width place at its right end, so nothing moves. (r60)
- **HIS-008** In timeline mode (and while a branch's take-back goes back, HIS-106) the top and bottom bars turn grey, the battlefield is dimmed, and the board centre shows a faint "Timeline mode" label with icon and, on its own line, the playback status "Playing" or "Paused" with its icon. Catch-up playback never looks like this (HIS-094). (r53)
- **HIS-009** When playback starts or stops (by hand or at the end), a large play or pause sign flashes in the board's top-right corner. (r54)
- **HIS-010** The timeline panel has the strip with a playback settings gear at its end, and below it Previous, the Auto button and Next. Previous/Next are plain borderless arrows, Next at the last event too. There is no game tree button. (r61)
- **HIS-005** Each event (your tap leaving your own main phase is one, HIS-101) is a 20 dp icon at a 24 dp pitch, coloured by who made it (your teal, the opponent's red, system grey). Where the turn or step changed between two events, a thin vertical line sits in the gap between them with a small label at its top right, "T3 · Combat" ("Opening" before the first turn), in the colour of the player whose turn it is; a new turn's line is stronger. When several changes fall between two events only the latest shows, and where labels would overlap the newer keeps its label (the lines all show). There is one line of play: no branch lines or markers. (r57)
- **HIS-011** Swiping the strip or tapping an event moves the review cursor to that exact event; holding an event shows its text without moving the cursor. (r33)
- **HIS-012** Previous and Next step one event along the main line, from the game's first event (HIS-111) up to your local head. The strip shows that line. (r58)
- **HIS-111** The timeline (timeline mode's strip, Previous and playback) and the Undo dialog's strip reach back to the game's first event ("The game began", with the flag) however long the game is. Older events load from the server as you go back: a small spinner at the strip's left end while they come, the board staying at the last point shown until the next one's events are in, and play never waits for them. Nothing limits how many events back you can look or undo (HIS-084, HIS-099). (r58)
- **HIS-013** The Auto button reads "Auto: off" (green play icon) or "Auto: on" (orange pause icon), or at the last event "Exit timeline" (HIS-015); tapping toggles automatic playback. Timeline mode is the read-only history viewer: it never stops for your decisions and nothing in it acts on the game. (r53)
- **HIS-015** Navigating to the last event by hand stays in timeline mode, and there (also as timeline mode opens, at the newest event) the Auto button reads "Exit timeline" with a close icon: one tap leaves timeline mode at once, to where you were (HIS-018); moving back from the last event makes it Auto again. Automatic playback reaching the end returns to play mode by itself, its button still "Auto: on" and its animation still playing. (r61)
- **HIS-016** In timeline mode double tap does nothing (no error), a one-finger drag on empty table pans, and card menus do not act on the game. (r45)
- **HIS-017** Hold-inspect works on every face-up card in timeline mode too, and there holding a card stops Auto, which stays off after the release. (Live and in catch-up playback a hold pauses what your view is waiting on, HIS-021.) (r61)
- **HIS-018** "Return to current" in the top bar, and Back, leave timeline mode to where you were: live play, or the catch-up paused with its centre text (HIS-094). (r53)
- **HIS-019** Leaving timeline mode never asks a question; the timeline has no branching action (a branch happens only when your real answer differs from an assumed one, HIS-097). (r48)
- **HIS-020** A pending win/loss/draw suggestion never blocks the timeline: Table actions still opens, with Timeline usable and its other entries greyed. It locks play only once the board shown to you (your local head) includes it (TRN-049). (r55)

### Playback

- **HIS-021** Playback advances one event at a time. An event that changes something you can see (a card moving, cast, played, resolving, dying or drawn; tapping, life, counters or damage; attacks and blocks; the other player's choices; a new turn) keeps its animation and then waits the playback delay: 0.33 s at Card move speed 1×, scaled by it (0.11 s at 3×, 1.32 s at 0.25×); an event with a play shown (HIS-112) waits that show (shorter for a repeat) instead of the delay. Attacks play back in the combat area (CMB-007). A finger held on the table (still, for the hold's time: a pan or a drag isn't a hold; on a card it also inspects it, and a pinned Inspect keeps the pause until closed, TBL-131) pauses whatever your view is waiting on: a play shown, a combat's hold (HIS-113), playback between events, live and in catch-up alike, however long it stays. It goes on when lifted, with the time left. Only your view waits, never the game: your phone makes none of its own steps while it waits, and the other player's phone goes on. Meanwhile a pause icon shows in the board's top-right corner, fading in and out quickly. It doesn't touch what isn't timed for your view: a card already moving, the turn banner (TRN-074), a branch's take-back (HIS-106), the catch-up's centre text or a stop; timeline mode's Auto keeps its own Pause (HIS-017). (r61)
- **HIS-104** In playback, passes, steps where nothing happened and declaring no attackers or no blockers go by at once (only the step indicator moves) unless your response mode is Control (TRN-059), where they keep the delay, except the other player's pass right after their own action (a resolve by hand after their cast, which a lower mode would have made inside that action's event, HIS-109); the other player's staging and early answers take 0.12 s. The History playback pause (HIS-028) paces only timeline mode's Auto. A combat's attackers and damage hold as HIS-113 says. (r61)
- **HIS-022** The "Rewind pause", 0.1 s by default, adjustable from 0.02 s to 2 s, paces a take-back after a branch or a rewind (HIS-097, HIS-099); there is no rewinding in timeline mode. (r49)
- **HIS-023** Starting Auto takes its first step right away; later steps keep the pause. (r31)
- **HIS-093** Catch-up playback stops at every point HIS-095 names, and at reveals (HIS-110), and waits there with no time limit. (r58)
- **HIS-094** Catching up: coming back with the other player's events past your local head shows no dialog: your table as you left it, looking exactly like live play (no dimming, grey bars, label or other sign that you are catching up; no event strip, as in live play, HIS-001), and in the board's centre a large text with a play icon, "<name> has made moves · Tap to watch" (when what waits begins with a take-back of what you had seen: "<name> responded differently · Tap to watch" for a branch, HIS-106, "<name> undid moves · Tap to watch" for their undo, HIS-108; at a branch's stop at the branch point "<name> responded differently from here · Tap to continue", HIS-106), with no count or preview. A tap on it, or anywhere on the table while it shows (the battlefields, the combat band, the cards and piles on them; your hand and the buttons keep their own taps, and holding a card still inspects it), starts the playback (a finger held on the table pauses it between events until lifted, HIS-021) (the text goes at once), which also looks like live play: the other player's actions animate as if they happened now (HIS-021, HIS-104), new events of theirs arriving meanwhile join it, and reaching the newest event you are live, with nothing announcing it. There is no Play/Pause button. The opening (mulligans) is never played back: the other player's opening declarations show at once, up to a decision of yours or the first turn, and the pause starts after them unless you already answered a decision there. (r60)
- **HIS-095** Catch-up playback shows the other player's events one at a time (HIS-021) and stops before an assumed answer about you where your current response mode stops (STK-036: Control at every point where you get priority, Normal at your pauses, Quick where you could respond; your decisions and assumed blocks always), the same mode as live play's (playback's quick actions show and change it as live play's do, TRN-059); events made earlier are judged by your mode now, not the one they recorded. Changing your mode at a stop leaves that stop until you answer it; the new mode applies from the next point on (STK-036). It also stops at the other player's assumption that took the place of a move of yours (HIS-109), and right after a reveal until you dismiss its panel (HIS-110). A tap on the table (a card, a pile or the empty table) while it runs pauses it: it goes on to the next point where you get priority (where Control would stop) and stops there, live, with the centre text (HIS-094) back, whose tap resumes it. Elsewhere, showing an assumed pass confirms it. (r58)
- **HIS-110** Reveal stop: when cards are revealed (from a hand or library, a card a search found, a top card, each card a "reveal until" turns over: any reveal, whoever's spell, ability or action made it), each player's table stops at that event, live or in catch-up playback, the revealing player's too, and a panel above your hand (STK-044) shows "<name> revealed · <source>", naming who revealed as the card says (the player whose hand or library the cards came from: "Bob revealed · Ember Exposure" when Alice's spell makes Bob reveal his hand; "You revealed · <source>" for your own; only "<name> revealed" when nothing made it), the revealed cards as cards at the Hand card size (TBL-034; wrapping onto more rows, the panel scrolling when they don't fit; holding one inspects it, TBL-104), the source's card at its right at the Panels size, and the eye; its OK is the main button (TRN-063) while it is open. Meanwhile game actions are refused (holding a greyed menu row says "Dismiss the reveal first: tap OK.") as while a choice is open (STK-032: what they were made on shakes, nothing glows, menu rows are greyed); looking, inspecting and Undo still work. Until you tap OK the other player's later events wait as at a playback stop and then play on as before; only your view waits: your device still makes its own steps, the game is never held for the other player, and the panel comes before any choice panel of yours (it shows after OK). OK closes the panel at once on your phone, whatever your device is still working out. Each player's OK is their own: dismissing it closes it only for them, and nothing else closes it but the reveal being taken back: not the other player's OK or reports, a chat message, an undo request, a suggestion, the game loading again or ending at the table; what you dismissed never opens again. Your own choice among cards you already see isn't a reveal for you: a card you chose in a search, cards you chose among, cards you already saw (your hand) open no panel of yours, while the other player still gets theirs. Several reveals open one panel each, in order; a reveal taken back before your OK (an undo, a branch, a rewind) closes its panel. Kept only on this phone: a game opened again opens none for what was shown before. (r62)
- **HIS-112** Plays shown: when the other player casts a spell, activates an ability or one of their triggers goes on the Stack, and when one of your own triggers does (never your own casts or activations), your table shows it large in the middle of the board for a moment and then it moves to the Stack, even when it resolves by itself right away, live and in catch-up playback alike: a spell as its card (the face cast; a face-down spell as a face-down card, HIS-062), an ability as its ability token (STK-046) enlarged, and under it a line naming it as the Stack line does (STK-057): "Bob cast Storm Spark · deals 3 damage to Ember Cub", or the source's name with the ability's kind ("Cycling trigger", STK-050) and its text with its targets named. It shows for the "Show plays and combat" time (LOB-082: 1.2 s by default; at 0 none shows), then its line goes and it flies to the Stack's top card in about 0.25 s (at Card move speed 1×, scaled by it, TBL-038), speeding up as it goes and taking the top card's place and size, fading out over the flight's second half; it is gone from the screen the moment it lands. The same ability from the same kind of source (the source's name and the ability's text, whoever controls it) shown again in one chain (each play showing within a second after the one before landed; a board wipe's "whenever a creature dies" triggers) shows 35% shorter each time, down to 0.15 s (never longer than the setting), its flight likewise down to 0.1 s at 1×; the first keeps the whole time, and a spell is never a repeat. A double tap on it ends it at once (a tap on the table right after one on it, the play gone from under the finger, does nothing); a single tap on it does nothing; holding it inspects it (TBL-104) and, as a finger held anywhere on the table does, pauses it until lifted (HIS-021). While it shows only your view waits: the other player's later events are held as at a playback stop, and your phone makes none of its own steps (its passes and assumptions) until it is done, so the board under it stays as it was; the game is never held for the other player, whose phone goes on. Several show one after another, in order, the next the moment the one before lands, and in order with the turn banner (TRN-074); a reveal panel (HIS-110) comes before them; one taken back (an undo, a branch, a rewind) ends; entering timeline mode ends the one showing. The Stack line and the wait's words (STK-057, TRN-066) give way while it shows. Kept only on this phone: a game opened again shows none of what was shown before. Its card has a content description naming it. (r58)
- **HIS-113** A combat stays readable: whoever's combat it is, live and in catch-up playback, your view holds (never the game) once when the attackers show and once on the damage (each combat damage step that deals any), each for the "Show plays and combat" time (LOB-082; at 0, Off, no hold): the later events are held as at a playback stop and your phone makes none of its own steps until it is done, while the other player's phone goes on. The damage's hold shows the board right after the damage is dealt: damage marked and life changed, what it did on the band (CMB-050), the creatures it destroys still on the battlefield; they die once the hold ends. Nothing shows for it but the board itself; a double tap on the table (the battlefields, the combat band, the cards and piles on them, not your hand) only skips it, with nothing else a double tap there does (no zoom to fit, TBL-012, no pile search), also if the hold ended between its two taps; a single tap there does nothing; a finger held there pauses it until lifted (HIS-021). When one board brings both, the attackers' step holds first (TBL-125). Declaring no attackers holds nothing. With plays shown (HIS-112) they come in order; entering timeline mode ends it; one taken back ends. (r63)
- **HIS-114** Cards leaving a public pile: when the other player's spell, ability or action moves cards out of a graveyard or exile (face up) into a hand or library you can't see, your table shows it as a reveal (HIS-110), live and in catch-up playback alike: the reveal panel titled "Bob returned Storm Spark from their graveyard to their hand" ("… from exile …", "Bob put Storm Spark from their graveyard into their library", "2 cards" for several; "your" for your own pile, and a third player's by name, "Carol's graveyard"), only the cards that left the pile (nothing else of the hand or library), the source's card at its right, its OK the main button (TRN-063), with the reveal's stops, refusals and OK of your own. Not for moves to the battlefield, the Stack or another public pile, into your own hand, nor for your own moves. (r62)
- **HIS-105** At a playback stop (HIS-095) the table is live at that point, exactly like live play: quick actions, the main button (Resolve with something on the Stack, STK-029), casting and activating, with nothing saying it was assumed. Answering the same confirms it and playback goes on by itself (an answer that starts the same and goes on, such as passing and then the discard it leads to, confirms it and the rest is your next action); anything else is a branch (HIS-097). (r53)
- **HIS-102** With everything before them shown (your live game), the other player's passes show at once on the live table, never as playback; what they lead to (a spell resolving) animates as usual. (r52)
- **HIS-096** Assumed answers: at the other player's priority points the device assumes their pass (Assume resolves) or blocks (Assume blockers) so the game goes on, never at their own main phase. A point belongs to the player who began it: whoever made the last real input since the step began (a cast, a land, an activation, an attack or block declaration, an answer to a decision; an assumed block is the assuming player's; passes never count, nor whose turn it is or whose spell or ability is on top of the Stack), or the active player when nobody did (a beginning-of-step trigger, an upkeep): you attack and they block, the point after it is theirs. At a point you began their pass is assumed at once where your "Assume resolves" setting assumes it or their own response mode would pass it; at a point they began only their mode decides, your setting ignored, so where their mode stops your phone waits for them (STK-036; their Quick judged on their real hand: by choosing Quick they give that out, and no setting of yours uses their hidden cards). Otherwise it is a wait with Assume on the main button (TRN-070), at their points too, so you can leave a spell up for a player you expect to respond or go on without them. The setting, defined from the response modes (it changes with them), counts at your points: Never assumes nothing by itself (a wait wherever they get priority, as Control stops, also at your end step and right after your attack); Normal (the default) assumes wherever Normal mode would pass, and their pause at your end step and right after you attack, before blocks (nothing of yours follows before their next real answer, so it never takes anything back); When they have no mana as Normal, and also at their pauses while what your table shows gives them no untapped mana source, no mana in their pool and no ability they could activate (the cards in their hand don't count); Always assumes every pass. So at your points, against a player in Normal or Quick, Never and Normal behave the same; against one in Control, Never waits for them and Normal doesn't. Never their presence (online or away) decides it; their playback stops where their own mode stops (HIS-095); their blocks are assumed only from the main button's Assume blockers screen (CMB-040). Anything else of theirs is a real wait, their opening keep or mulligan included (never assumed); TRN-071 applies, with the game's assume reveal settings (LOB-135). Conceptually an assumption is a conditional pre-move, not a decision made for them (I-D57). (r58)
- **HIS-097** Branch: when the other player's real answer differs from what was assumed, the game goes on from their answer and the line after it is discarded, never seen by them; your table goes back to the branch point with a note "<name> responded" ("blocked differently", "took a mulligan"). When real actions of yours were taken back the note names the first, in its event text's words (HIS-091): "<name> responded — your attack was taken back" ("your Storm Spark", "your blocks were", "your End turn"); your phone's own passes and assumptions (a press of Resolve or Assume is the same, HIS-109) are no action of yours, so a branch that took back only them says just "<name> responded", and the rewind animation (HIS-106) plays only when the board shown changes. A new line starts (HIS-042). There is no other way to branch. (r59)
- **HIS-109** Automation is button presses: your phone's own pass for you is exactly a press of the main button (Resolve, or a step move such as "To draw step"), and its assumption of the other player's answer exactly a press of Assume (TRN-070), which you may also press by hand at any of their points, theirs too (HIS-096). So the player an assumption is about stays at that decision and may act there: passing confirms it, anything else branches (HIS-097). That includes a live move of theirs that reaches the server after the other phone's assumption, such as a second spell while holding priority in Control after you pressed Assume, or a response cast as their phone assumed your pass: their phone makes it there as their answer, without a note, never refused as "That wasn't taken"; a pass that is just what the other phone assumed confirms it with nothing on their table taken back (SYS-077). Likewise a pass by hand looks like one made by itself: when the other player in Control holds priority after their own cast and then taps Resolve, your phone shows what it shows when a lower mode of theirs passed by itself: the same timeline events and texts (no pass entry, HIS-101), the same single "your move" notification, the same catch-up playback and its stops (HIS-104). And a tap of the main button (Resolve, Next, Pass or Assume) that reaches your phone just after it made that same press by itself at the same point (e.g. tapping Resolve as Quick passes for you over your own next ability on the Stack) is that press: nothing more is made (never the next Stack item's resolution) and nothing is told (SYS-011). The presses a held Resolve or Assume makes (TRN-017) are your phone's own presses too; a tap of yours ends that chain. (r59)
- **HIS-106** A branch (HIS-097) goes back to the branch point: your table goes back step by step with timeline mode's look (HIS-008), each step followed by the Rewind pause (HIS-022), with the note; meanwhile a tap on the table doesn't pause it, and Back and the menu (Timeline too) do nothing. Then it stops at the branch point as playback stops: your table is live there, looking like live play, with the centre text "<name> responded differently from here · Tap to continue" (HIS-094), and its tap plays the other player's new events back as catch-up playback with its stops (HIS-095). When nothing of theirs follows the branch point, or playback stops there anyway for a decision of yours, there is no extra stop. Live (the game open, in sight, at the head) the take-back plays at once. Coming back to it (the app in the background, the game not open, a notification, a cold start) opens the game exactly as you left it, and nothing moves until you tap: the centre text says "<name> responded differently · Tap to watch" (HIS-094), and the tap plays the take-back as above, up to its stop at the branch point. (r60)
- **HIS-028** Playback settings live in Settings under "Timeline playback" and in a dialog from the timeline's gear: "History playback pause", "Rewind pause" and Card move speed; Settings' group also has "Show plays and combat" (HIS-112, HIS-113), the gear's dialog doesn't. There are no auto-rewind switches. (r58)
- **HIS-030** Playback and live opponent actions animate each change (cards fly, rotate, flip, fade; also between piles). Take-backs animate backwards: an undo in one go on both tables (for the other player only as far as they had seen, HIS-108), a branch's take-back step by step (HIS-106). (r53)
- **HIS-031** Changes show a delta tag such as "+2" and a number flash for life, player counters, card P/T and counters (shown 1 s, then a 0.5 s fade; changes within 1.5 s add up), and a short highlight for face, control and step changes: your own and the opponent's, live and in playback (catch-up playback too), also when a take-back goes back; never on jumps, on opening the game or in timeline mode. They come from the events shown (life, P/T, step and turn, face, a change of control, a library's shuffle). (r60)

### Opening and returning to a game

- **HIS-032** The server keeps, per game and account, your local head: the newest event shown to you (live or in playback) or added by you; another device opens the game at it. Everything shown to you (timeline, chips, result dialog, game list times, notification texts) comes from it and your next stop. (r55)
- **HIS-033** Opening a game with the other player's events past your local head starts the catch-up paused with its centre text (HIS-094), with no count or preview; its tap then plays them back, stopping at your decision points (HIS-095). Playback never skips ahead (the opening shows at once, HIS-094). When everything since is your own, the game opens live; so does a game whose playback stands at a stop of yours (HIS-095: the next event is the other player's assumed answer where you decide): it opens at that stop, live, with no centre text, whatever of theirs follows it, unless a branch or the other player's undo took that stop back: then it opens as you left it with the centre text (HIS-106, HIS-108). (r59)
- **HIS-035** Leaving the app pauses playback at the shown point at once; coming back with the other player's events past your local head starts the catch-up paused with its centre text (HIS-094) instead of rushing through them, and coming back to a branch or the other player's undo of events you had seen waits as you left it for your tap (HIS-106, HIS-108). A return where only chat or other non-event data changed, or only your own events arrived, stays live, and so does a return to a stop of yours (HIS-095), also with the other player's events after it (nothing to watch before your decision), unless such a take-back took that stop back. (r59)

### Lines of play

- **HIS-042** Every line of play (the game's start, every branch, every rewind including free undo) starts by reshuffling the unknown parts of both libraries, shown nowhere; a card whose place someone knows (put on top or bottom by a known action, looked at or revealed where it lies, a kept-revealed top) keeps it. Random results (shuffles, coins, dice, random choices) follow from the game's secret and the line: the same input on a line gives the same result, a new line new ones. (r48)
- **HIS-043** There is no branch archiving and no "Make current" action anywhere; lines left by a branch are kept for problem reports and shown to nobody. (r48)

### Events and turns

- **HIS-058** End turn (TRN-057) is your device passing your priority until the turn ends: your passes fold into the events that reach them (HIS-092) and never pause playback, stop or notify; the other player's passes assumed on the way are events of their own, and their playback stops at those it stops at (HIS-095). (r53)
- **HIS-092** Each history event is one acting point: a player's input (a cast or land play, a pass, an answer, a declaration) with everything that then happens by itself folded in (automatic answers, forced passes, their device's passes by response mode, resolutions, mana, untapping and drawing, steps passed, triggers placed, state-based actions), up to the next point where someone can act. An assumed answer (HIS-096) always begins its own event and never shares it with a real input. (r48)

### Event texts

- **HIS-061** Every event has a short text: hold an icon on the timeline. Texts name who acted first ("You", the other player's name) and end with what it led to (HIS-091). (r55)
- **HIS-062** Texts never reveal what the viewer could not see at that event (hand, library, face-down, the other player's look); hidden cards read "a card" or "N cards". (r27)
- **HIS-091** Event texts name the actor: "You cast Storm Spark", "Bob played Mountain", "You activated Ember Herald's ability" (STK-046), "You attacked with 2", "Bob blocked with 1"; cycling reads "Bob cycled Shattered Reason", another keyword's ability by its keyword ("You activated Ember Blade's equip ability"), and a keyword's ability or trigger resolving by its kind (STK-050: "Shattered Reason's cycling trigger resolved"; other abilities "Ember Herald's ability resolved", HIS-101); table-only events read "Alice staged 2 attackers", "Bob arranged 1 block", "You answered early", "A result was suggested". An answer you assumed reads, for you only, as theirs with "(assumed)" ("Bob: no blocks (assumed)"); they read it as their own, so nothing tells them it was assumed. (r58)
- **HIS-101** Passes are implied: no "passed" line appears anywhere (timeline, event chip, Undo dialog, notifications), assumed or not, though passes stay in the game's record. A pass shows only as what it led to: "Storm Spark resolved" ("Ember Herald's ability resolved"), "Turn 3 → Bob" or the new step ("Combat"); one that led to none of these shows nowhere. On the timeline and in the Undo dialog a pass is an event only when something resolved, it began your own End turn ("You ended the turn"), or it is your tap of the main button leaving your own main phase with an empty Stack ("You went to combat", from your second main phase in Control "You went to the end step"): these last two are on the game's record with the event, what you pressed and on the press's first pass only, so they stay after a restart, on reopening the game and on another device, and they show only to you. Events from before the record held them have none. The turn and step changes passes led to show as the lines between events (HIS-005), so a turn where nothing happened shows only as such a line. (r61)

### Event icons

- **HIS-070** An event's icon is its input's: a cast = layers, a land = dashboard, an activation = a click, a pass = an arrow (a check when something resolved, skip-next for a new turn), attacks and blocks = swords, a keep = a check, a mulligan = shuffle, a concession = the outlined flag, results = the trophy, staging = the crosshair, an early answer = the note, the game's start = the flag, a new line after a rewind = the restore icon. (r49)

### Undo

- **HIS-083** The Undo button sits at the left end of the bottom bar: tap = Undo (HIS-084); hold = the Undo dialog to pick any earlier point (HIS-099), with a small haptic tick. There is no Redo. (r48)
- **HIS-084** Undo goes back to just before your latest own meaningful action, with your later assumptions and every pass after it (passes are never the target, HIS-101, except your tap leaving your own main phase; an End turn is one action): at once when free (HIS-087), else the Undo dialog opens with that point chosen (HIS-099). You land where you acted and your device passes nothing for you there until you act, also when the other player's table-only events (staging, early answers) arrive meanwhile; Undo again goes back past your action before. Tapped while your action is still on its way, it waits for it (HIS-107). With your cast or activation open (its panel or target bar, nothing sent yet) it only drops it, taking nothing back (STK-025). Your OK on a game's result is never the point it goes back to (TRN-065): it goes back past the result, with every OK on it. There is no limit on how many events go. (r61)
- **HIS-085** One acting point (a cast with its choices, a declaration with its payment) undoes as one. (r48)
- **HIS-107** An Undo tapped while your own action is still being worked out or sent (right after Attack or a cast, while your events wait for the server) is never refused: no shake and no "That wasn't taken"; it waits for the action, then takes back the action you last made when you tapped (never one you make, or that arrives, after the tap), and meanwhile your device makes nothing more for you. A move of yours waiting to be made again after the other player's event came first (SYS-077) is simply not made again, and any undo of yours ends such moves. When by then the undo isn't free (HIS-087), the Undo dialog opens with that point chosen (HIS-099). (r53)
- **HIS-108** An undo, yours or the other player's of events you had seen, shows its whole result at once and animates once, straight from the board before to the board after (a cast spell flies from the battlefield straight back to the hand), at the card move speed with no playback pause: no boards, step texts, main button or quick-action states in between, while "Undid: <action>" fades at the top (HIS-076). Live (the game open, in sight, at the head) the other player's undo shows at once. Coming back to it (the app in the background, the game not open, a notification, a cold start) opens the game exactly as you left it, and nothing moves until you tap: the centre text says "<name> undid moves · Tap to watch" (HIS-094), and the tap plays the undo as above, then their new events as catch-up playback with its stops. Their undo of only events you never saw is a plain catch-up. One that took back more than 200 events you had seen opens at its result in an app started anew. (r59)
- **HIS-086** Undo only deletes the events after the point: the game is exactly as it was there, and no timeline event ever says the game went back (the new line's reshuffle, HIS-042, happens with the next event). (r55)
- **HIS-087** Undo is free only when everything after the point is yours (real inputs, or assumptions not yet confirmed) or the other player's table-only events that stay (SYS-058), and none of it showed you something new: a card you couldn't see (your draw, look, scry, search, a reveal), a random result, or a wait for the other player's choice from a source you can't see. - **HIS-099** Holding Undo, or an Undo that isn't free, opens "Undo". On top a small view of the board at the chosen point as you see it (after a new choice it follows once the choice rests a quarter second, dimmed meanwhile); under it the timeline's strip of the points with their icons and turn and step lines, the chosen one boxed in the centre: dragging it left or right chooses an earlier or later point one event at a time (a tap on an event chooses it, holding one shows its text); then "Go back to just before" and the chosen event's text. - **HIS-100** The other player gets "<name> asks to undo" with the message, where the game goes back to as far as they have seen ("and actions you haven't seen yet are taken back" when past that), a small view of that board (their own newest one when the point is past what they've seen) and Deny / Accept, never how many events go.
