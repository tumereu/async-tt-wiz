# Priority and choices

Who may act when: priority, the response modes that decide where your device stops for you, the main button's
Resolve, the choices Automatum asks and the panels they open, and reveals.

## How it works

Priority follows the game's rules: the active player gets it first in each step, and a player passes it with the main
button. When both players pass in a row, the top of the Stack resolves. Your device passes for you where you could do
nothing anyway, and your response mode (Control, Normal or Quick) decides where else it stops to let you act. While a
spell or ability is on the Stack and you hold priority, the main button reads Resolve. When Automatum needs a decision
from a player (a "you may", cards to choose, an order of triggers, a card name), only that player answers it, in a
panel just above their hand; the main button is the panel's OK. When cards are revealed, each player's table stops
and shows them in a panel until that player taps OK.

## Priority

- **SYS-067** Priority follows the rules: each step gives it to the active player first; a player passes it with the main button (a step move such as "To draw step", or Resolve; TRN-063, TRN-057, STK-029), so the other player may act once the active player has passed; a caster passes after a cast (STK-026); after a mana ability or special action its player keeps it, in either turn; after something resolves the active player gets it again. Lands played count one per turn. (r47)
- **STK-026** Priority is passed by itself for a player who could only pass, judged on public information (no cards in hand, nothing else hidden they could use), by whichever device runs the step; never in their own main phases with an empty Stack. Your device also passes for you where your response mode doesn't stop (STK-036), and a caster passes after a cast. (r48)
- **STK-170** When both players pass in a row the top of the Stack resolves, and play goes on by itself to the next point where someone can act. (r48)
- **STK-053** In every response mode your own main phases are never passed for you (casting or playing a land there leaves you there with priority), and choices and declarations of attackers and blockers always wait for their player. (r52)
- **STK-037** At your stop or pause the table is yours to act on, with no time limit and no Hold: the main button (TRN-063), reading Resolve with something on the Stack (STK-029). (r53)
- **STK-029** While a spell or ability is on the Stack and you hold priority, the main button (TRN-063) reads "Resolve" (a play icon above the word): a tap passes your own priority; holding it resolves the whole Stack (TRN-107). Nothing sits under the Stack. While your cast or a choice is open, or a reveal panel (HIS-110), it holds their go-on action instead, or is greyed (TRN-063). Not while your phone is about to pass by itself: once a play shown is done, or right after your own cast, Assume or Resolve (TRN-119). (r76)

## Response modes

- **STK-036** Your response mode (TRN-059) sets where your device stops for you, in live play and in catch-up playback alike (HIS-095): Control at every point where you get priority (after your own cast or activation you keep it; in your own turn also at the beginning of combat, CMB-047, and your end step, which the main button in your second main phase taps on to, TRN-063). (r58)
- **STK-171** Normal stops at your stops (your own main phases with an empty Stack) and pauses (the other player's spell or ability on top of the Stack, their attack, blocks, their end step). (r58)
- **STK-172** Quick (the default) stops as Normal, but passes at pauses where you can't respond and answers one-answer questions (SYS-063), never the order of your triggers (STK-030) or cards picked up to an amount (STK-061). (r58)
- **SYS-063** Quick (STK-036) is part of your response mode and runs on your own device: it passes where you can't respond (never your own main phases, STK-053) and answers questions with one answer as your own inputs (never the order of your triggers, STK-030, or cards picked up to an amount, STK-061). (r58)
- **SYS-120** The other player's phone, which learns your mode, judges on your real hand where your Quick would pass, to assume your pass there (HIS-096; at points you began only your mode decides, so where it stops their phone waits for you); otherwise other devices judge what is automatic only on public information. (r58)
- **STK-173** A new response mode applies from the next point on: the stop you stand at when you change it, live or in playback, stays until you answer it (never passed by itself; where the other player's phone then assumes your pass, your table stops at that assumption). (r58)
- **STK-174** The response mode is a value of each game kept on the server, outside undo and history: the other player's phone learns it at once when you change it (never shown on their screen) and assumes your pass wherever your mode would pass; at points you began only your mode decides there, so it waits where your mode stops (HIS-096). (r58)
- **STK-175** Each event records the response mode it was made under (an assumption: the mode of the player it is about). (r58)
- **SYS-071** Control is one of the three response modes (STK-036), never an event: each game's mode is a value kept on the server, outside undo and history, which the other player's phone learns at once (never shown on their screen); set from the quick actions (TRN-059, in live play and catch-up playback alike, HIS-095) or, for new games, Settings (LOB-113). (r57)

## Automatum's panels

- **STK-044** In the phone layout (the desktop table: WEB-019) every Automatum panel (open choices, target bars, the reveal panel, HIS-110) sits where the cast panel does (a damage division is attached to the combat band instead, CMB-033): the table's full width (in landscape at most 420 dp from its left edge), white with a violet top edge, its bottom on the top of your hand row so your hand is never covered. No Hold button. A game's result is a dialog in the middle of the screen instead (TRN-065). (r58)
- **STK-176** Each Automatum panel has the eye, at the left of its bottom row or slim bar, which hides it to show the board (only the eye stays) and brings it back (cards picked by tapping stay picked, as in a search, STK-056). (r58)
- **STK-177** Panel titles say only the choice ("Scry 2", "Choose any target", "Pay {R}" with its symbols), except the cast panel's, which names its card (STK-023), and the reveal panel's, which names who revealed and the source (HIS-110), and the sentence the question asks about shows under them (STK-030). (r58)
- **STK-178** What the choice comes from is otherwise never named in text but sits at the panel's or bar's right, in the same place in each (a damage division's creature at its top left instead, CMB-033), as a small card: a spell or permanent as its card, an ability as its Stack ability token (STK-046, also when its source has left, as a land sacrificed to activate it; a copied ability's with its copy marker, never a card back); holding it inspects it (TBL-104). Choosing new targets for a copy names the spell or ability it copies. A panel with no source (a discard to hand size, the legend rule) has none. (r58)
- **STK-179** Every card in a panel (the source too) is the hand's 28×40 dp times the Panels card size (TBL-034), except the reveal panel's revealed cards, at the Hand card size (HIS-151). (r58)
- **STK-180** A card a tap or drop moves within a panel (scry, surveil, putting cards in order) slides to its new place while the others slide to close the gap, at the card move speed (TBL-038); holding a panel card to inspect it moves nothing in the panel. (r58)
- **STK-032** While Automatum's choice is open, casting and every action that changes what Automatum works with are refused on both devices: what it was made on shakes (TBL-118), on the other player's device their "is choosing" text flashes (HIS-075), and holding a greyed menu row or the main button says "Automatum is waiting for a choice. Answer it first." Looking, inspecting and Undo still work. (r53)

## Choices

- **STK-030** A choice Automatum meets while resolving or putting triggers on the Stack ("you may …", choosing cards, a trigger's targets and modes, the order of triggers, which replacement applies first, the legend rule, a payment) belongs to the player the rules name, who answers it in a panel above the hand (STK-044): a title saying only the choice ("Scry 2"), the source at the panel's right (STK-178), "About:" with the cards it concerns, a short grey instruction and the choice, cards drawn as cards. Nobody chooses for them (TRN-066). (r62)
- **STK-181** Without holding anything each question shows the sentence it asks about under its title: an ability's whole text (a keyword's trigger as its keyword's printed line; a trigger's targets picked on the table as the target bar shows them, STK-047, also when its source has left the battlefield or can't be seen, the ability's text being public), a resolving spell's sentence naming what is chosen. (r62)
- **STK-182** In a choice's sentence a "you may" is its own sentence with what follows it ("You may pay" over "You may forage. If you do, put two +1/+1 counters on it."), a cost to pay a "Cost" chip with its mana and tap symbols ("{2}", "{T}", "Forage"), and a cost of two ways asks which with what it is ("To forage, exile three cards from your graveyard or sacrifice a Food.", Exile / Sacrifice). (r62)
- **STK-183** Triggers to order and replacement effects to order are drag-to-reorder lists of full-width rows (a handle at the left, the place number, the card with its whole line; a trigger whose source has left the battlefield, a creature that died, shows that source and its line as it was there, CR 608.2h), opening in a suggested order (triggers the engine's, the top one resolving first; replacements the engine's), then OK; dragging a handle moves its row. (r62)
- **STK-184** Replacements are a preference list (CR 616.1): they apply in its order, skipping ones no longer applicable, and you are asked again only when a new one appears. (r62)
- **STK-185** When two or more of your triggers go on the Stack at the same time you are always asked their order, in every response mode (CR 603.3b); triggers that are exactly the same (one ability of one source triggering twice) are put on without asking. (r62)
- **STK-186** A number to choose has the same − value + with the numeric input as an X (STK-023); a player to choose is a button per player with their round avatar and name; the legend rule shows every permanent with the name to tap the one to keep, then Keep. A choice's OK (the legend rule's Keep) is the main button (TRN-114). (r62)
- **STK-187** A choice of permanents on the battlefield (an edict's sacrifice, "choose a creature", "choose a creature an opponent controls") is picked on the table instead (STK-047), as is a payment (STK-049). (r62)
- **STK-188** While the same choice stays open the panel stays, and what you have picked in it, whatever else arrives meanwhile (the other player's result suggestion or undo request). (r62)
- **STK-189** The cards a choice offers its own player show face up when they are theirs to see, also a card the same step put in their hand just before asking (connive's drawn card in its discard choice, problem report JN5UQN). (r62)
- **STK-056** A search (a fetch land's "Search your library for a basic land card, …", a trigger's "you may search your library for …") is such a choice: its title says what is searched, from "Search" up to the next comma ("Search your library for a basic land card"), its source at the right (STK-178); the cards it may find are picked by tapping, then OK. (r54)
- **STK-190** Under a search's cards, "Other cards" shows the searched zone's other cards (in a search of the other player's library, theirs), dimmed and sorted by name: tapping one does nothing, holding one inspects it. (r54)
- **STK-043** A choice between two places is one screen of two labelled areas with a count ("Top · 2", "Bottom · 1"): scry (Top / Bottom), surveil (Top / Graveyard), separating piles (Pile 1 / Pile 2, or Face-up / Face-down pile), discarding (Hand / Discard), bottoming (Keep / Bottom). Cards start in the first area; a tap moves a card to the end of the other area and back, a drag drops it where released. (r42)
- **STK-054** In a two-area choice (STK-043) cards whose order matters (scry's areas, surveil's Top) are numbered, 1 nearest the top; each area has an "All to …" icon button; OK is enabled once the count fits (exactly one for "discard a card") and sends choice and order in one step. Ordering alone ("put them back in any order") is the same screen with one area. (r42)
- **STK-061** Collecting evidence as something resolves is one such choice (STK-030): after "you may", the cards of your graveyard are picked by tapping in one panel, as many as you like, and those you picked are exiled together. It is asked even when only all your cards reach the amount, in every response mode. (r70)
- **STK-191** Collecting evidence's instruction says the amount ("Tap cards with total mana value 8 or more, then OK.", as casting with collect evidence and crew's does with total power); OK is greyed until their mana values reach it and stays enabled for more cards. (r70)
- **STK-060** When several of your permanents explore at once, you choose which goes next (CR 701.44d) in a panel of their cards; one that has already left the battlefield is shown by what it was (hidden only if it was face down), since it still explores. When all of them have left you aren't asked: the order changes nothing, and each still reveals in turn. (r70)
- **STK-041** Naming a card ("choose a card name", CR 201.4) is a search field autocompleting card names, never free text: the names containing what was typed, first those of your own library as your phone knows them (a card-stack icon), then those of cards you see this game (public zones and your own hand, an eye icon), then every card of the device's card records (from two letters on), each name once and each passing the effect's restriction ("a nonland card name"). (r57)
- **STK-192** In naming a card, a card with several names offers each by itself and never both together, judged by that part alone: either half of a split card, either side of a double-faced card, a flip card's, Adventure's, Omen's or prepare spell's other name. (r57)
- **STK-193** A tap on an offered card name chooses it (written in the field) and OK, greyed until a name is chosen or one offered is written, names it. The other player's hidden cards never show or change the order. (r57)
- **STK-194** Choosing a creature type is a list of every creature type of the rules with a field narrowing it; a tap chooses one. Neither it nor naming a card lists the other player's hidden cards or their types. (r57)
- **STK-062** A copy of the card with a chosen name ("create a copy of the card with the chosen name", CR 707.12) is made at once from the card records of the phone of the player who makes it, the card built as a deck's cards are; nothing is asked. Without card records the game waits there.

## Reveals

- **HIS-110** Reveal stop: when cards are revealed (from a hand or library, a card a search found, a top card, each card a "reveal until" turns over: any reveal, whoever's spell, ability or action made it), each player's table stops at that event, live or in catch-up playback, the revealing player's too, and a panel above your hand (STK-044) shows them. (r62)
- **HIS-150** The reveal panel is titled "<name> revealed · <source>", naming who revealed as the card says: the player whose hand or library the cards came from ("Bob revealed · Ember Exposure" when Alice's spell makes Bob reveal his hand; "You revealed · <source>" for your own; only "<name> revealed" when nothing made it). (r62)
- **HIS-151** The reveal panel shows the revealed cards as cards at the Hand card size (TBL-034), wrapping onto more rows and scrolling when they don't fit (holding one inspects it, TBL-104), the source's card at its right at the Panels size, and the eye. Its OK is the main button (TRN-063) while it is open. (r62)
- **HIS-155** Your own choice among cards you already see isn't a reveal for you: a card you chose in a search, cards you chose among, cards you already saw (your hand) open no panel of yours, while the other player still gets theirs. (r62)
- **HIS-153** Until you tap OK the other player's later events wait as at a playback stop and then play on as before. Only your view waits: unlike a play shown, your device still makes its own steps, and the game is never held for the other player. The panel comes before any choice panel of yours (that shows after OK). OK closes the panel at once on your phone, whatever your device is still working out. (r62)
- **HIS-152** While a reveal panel is open, game actions are refused as while a choice is open (STK-032: what they were made on shakes, nothing glows, menu rows are greyed); holding a greyed menu row says "Dismiss the reveal first: tap OK.". Looking, inspecting and Undo still work. (r62)
- **HIS-154** Each player's OK is their own: dismissing it closes it only for them. Nothing else closes it but the reveal being taken back: not the other player's OK or reports, a chat message, an undo request, a suggestion, the game loading again or ending at the table. What you dismissed never opens again. (r62)
- **HIS-156** Several reveals open one panel each, in order; a reveal taken back before your OK (an undo, a branch, a rewind) closes its panel. Reveal panels are kept only on this phone: a game opened again opens none for what was shown before. (r62)
- **HIS-114** Cards leaving a public pile: when the other player's spell, ability or action moves cards out of a graveyard or exile (face up) into a hand or library you can't see, your table shows it as a reveal (HIS-110), live and in catch-up playback alike, with only the cards that left the pile (nothing else of the hand or library) and the reveal's stops, refusals and OK of your own. Not for moves to the battlefield, the Stack or another public pile, into your own hand, nor for your own moves. (r62)
- **HIS-157** The panel for cards leaving a public pile is titled "Bob returned Storm Spark from their graveyard to their hand" ("… from exile …", "Bob put Storm Spark from their graveyard into their library", "2 cards" for several; "your" for your own pile, and a third player's by name, "Carol's graveyard"), with the source's card at its right and its OK the main button (TRN-063). (r62)
