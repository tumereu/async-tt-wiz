# Cross-cutting rules

Rules that every feature follows: what the product is, who decides game state, how sync, errors and performance behave, version compatibility, the player-access policy and shared UI conventions.

### Product scope and principles

- **SYS-001** A two-player cooperative tabletop for Android, Magic: The Gathering first, played by the rules: Automatum works out costs, targets, priority and resolution in every game (SYS-055), the server decides the game state, and each player makes only their own decisions, live or asynchronously (SYS-036). The app shows what happened and what each player may do now. (r49)
- **SYS-002** Change only what was requested: a player's action does what the rules make of it and nothing more, and a card's name never decides anything. (r49)
- **SYS-003** Finished edits save and sync automatically. There is no Send button (chat messages excepted), no required explanation and no routine confirmation. (r1)
- **SYS-004** Each history event is one acting point (HIS-092): one player action with everything that then happens by itself; it undoes as one (HIS-085). (r49)
- **SYS-005** Personal UI state stays on the device and is never shared: camera, open menus, timeline review position, playback and card-size settings. Kept on the server for you alone: your local head (HIS-032) and your response mode, Assume resolves, playback's Full control and Autotap (TRN-059). (r53)
- **SYS-006** Undo is only a convenience. No feature works by undoing something (withdrawing a result suggestion, for example, is its own event), so a refused undo never blocks a feature. (r18)

### Server authority and sync

- **SYS-007** The server decides all shared game, lobby and history state, Undo and undo requests included. The app applies your own actions locally right away as a prediction; an undo request's answer is never local. (r48)
- **SYS-008** The app sends actions in order and keeps several in flight without waiting for replies; the server takes them in arrival order. A refused action also drops the player's later actions made on top of it: the board shows where the game is and the player acts again (SYS-012). (r49)
- **SYS-009** When the server corrects a prediction, only the real difference animates. Accepted moves and still-valid local moves stay where they are, with no visible rewind and replay. (r1)
- **SYS-010** A player action that needs several engine inputs (a cast with its choices and payment) goes to the server as one submission, taken or refused whole, and becomes one event (HIS-092). (r49)
- **SYS-011** An action is made on the event shown to its player: the server makes it again there and refuses it when the rules don't allow it (SYS-057) or the game moved on (SYS-012), never branching. A tap on a board your phone has already moved past (its own steps, or the other player's event not yet shown) is made on the newer board when the same decision still waits for you there (an Undo also when only your own and table-only events came in between, HIS-107); otherwise (a new Stack object, another step, a chosen target gone, a hidden card shown, a quick second tap) it isn't taken, with SYS-012's note. (r53)
- **SYS-076** End turn may be made anywhere in your own turn: tapped on a board your phone has moved on from, it ends the turn on the newer board while that is still the same turn of yours and nothing on the Stack is the other player's that you never saw (e.g. tapped right after To combat while your phone steps on by itself). (r52)
- **SYS-077** When the server took the other player's event before your move, your phone makes your move again on it, without a note, when the same decision still waits for you there (SYS-011); otherwise it tells you once (SYS-012). (r52)
- **SYS-012** The main line changes only by a branch (HIS-097), a rewind (HIS-099) or a concession while behind (TRN-064). An action made on a point the other player has since moved past only by arranging their cards or answering early is made after those; otherwise it isn't taken: "That wasn't taken: the game moved on first." Any other refusal of your own action says "That wasn't taken: the game didn't accept it."; the board updates and you act again. Your device's own passes and assumptions that lose such a race go quietly. (r50)
- **SYS-078** Notes on the table (a branch's "<name> responded — your later actions were taken back", SYS-012's "That wasn't taken: …") show one at a time near the top of the table for 6 s or until tapped; a note told while another one shows waits for it and shows next, never replacing it at once. (r53)
- **SYS-013** Playing and viewing a game need a connection; there is no offline queue of actions and no offline mode. An action made while the connection is briefly lost waits up to 3 s and is sent once, as soon as the connection is back. (r1)
- **SYS-014** After a reconnect, actions that were already sent are recovered automatically: no duplicates and no manual resend. If the app process dies, it restarts from the server's state, and actions it had not sent yet may be lost. (r1)
- **SYS-015** Recovery from missed updates is automatic. There is no manual "download history" or resync button. (r15)
- **SYS-016** Losing the connection shows nothing by itself. Only when an action has waited 3 s for the server does "Reconnecting…" / "Game actions unavailable." appear, with a "Back to games list" button; from then on actions are refused until the connection is back. The dialog can be dismissed; you stay on the table and actions are still refused. (r1)
- **SYS-017** Opening a game shows "Loading table…" until the table loads or really fails. It never flashes "Table unavailable" first. A game this device can't load says "This game can't be opened on this device. Send a problem report from the menu." instead of reconnecting over and over. (r50)
- **SYS-018** Lobby buttons stay enabled while the app reconnects. A tap waits for the connection and then goes through. (r21)
- **SYS-019** Coming back from the background never bounces or jumps the layout, and the interruption itself shows no error. (r7)

### Connection failures and errors

- **SYS-020** Automatic refreshes of data already on screen fail silently and keep the shown data. After 30 s of continuous failures, a calm "Can't reach the server · retrying" note appears; taps pass through it, and it disappears on the next success. (r22)
- **SYS-021** A first load (nothing on screen yet) keeps its loading indicator and retries quietly for about 10 s before it shows Retry. (r22)
- **SYS-022** Requests the player makes (taps, saves, Retry) report their failures at once. (r22)
- **SYS-023** Background checks never show loaders or spinners that push content around. Player edits such as settings hide the wait: no views are disabled while saving. (r17)
- **SYS-024** Real errors appear as a floating message, never as inline red text that shifts the layout (fixed form slots excepted). A first load shows a full-area placeholder, the layout keeps the same geometry when its state changes, and list order stays stable. (r15)

### Performance

- **SYS-025** Every action, Undo included, shows its result locally before the server answers: under 20 ms from input to applied state, with 80 ms as the hard ceiling. Waiting on the server for a visible result is a bug (an undo request's answer comes from the other player). (r48)
- **SYS-026** Games reach 10,000+ actions. Normal play, sync, navigation, undo and saving never replay or rebuild from the start of the game: a state comes from the nearest checkpoint (at most 63 events back) and the events after it. (r48)
- **SYS-027** Every event stores exactly how to apply and revert it, checked against the current values. Timeline stepping, undo and opponent animations move step by step from the current position. (r6)
- **SYS-028** Background lobby refreshes never redraw the open game table. (r30)
- **SYS-075** The games list is kept current from lightweight summaries: the first read and each return to the app read the whole list, and between them only the games changed since the last read are fetched (ended or declined games drop out). While a game is open nothing else of the list is read, and only the open game is loaded as a game on the device; the others are summaries until opened. (r50)
- **SYS-029** Every single-tap menu opens immediately, without waiting out the double-tap window. (r8)
- **SYS-030** Test and debug builds record every action that took over 20 ms or had to wait for the server, plus stutters and reconnects, and upload them as reports. Production builds do the same only after the player turned reports on (LOB-104). (r35)

### Versions and compatibility

- **SYS-031** Games carry no rules version: events are stored in the engine's own format, which the one current app and server read (SYS-033); a format change that old games can't be read in removes them, only with the owner's permission. Event texts and icons are made by the app (HIS-091, HIS-070). (r49)
- **SYS-032** If a game has events from newer rules than the app knows, the app shows "Update the app to continue this game" and the update screen. If the server is the older side, it shows "Server update pending" and retries every 30 s. (r16)
- **SYS-033** Only one compatible set of apps and server is kept working: the server serves only the current app protocol and rules, so a server change may require the newest app, and older apps then only show the update screen (SYS-032). (r35)
- **SYS-034** Unknown event kinds or icons from a newer version never crash the app; they show a fallback. (r31)
- **SYS-035** If local data was written by a newer app, the app says "Local data needs a newer app. Update the app without uninstalling." It never erases local data when storage fails to open. (r1)
- **SYS-052** If the server can't read one saved game, pod, pod preset or card (e.g. after an update changed its format), or one saved game fails its damage check at startup, only that item is affected: it is left out of its list (a deck whose cover can't be read shows none), opening it fails like a temporary server problem, and an unreadable pending action is refused. The server still starts, everything else carries on, nothing is deleted or rewritten, and the item is logged. (r35)
- **SYS-053** The server refuses any action the rules don't allow, with a clear message saying why; it never ignores such an action silently. The app doesn't offer actions the server would refuse. (r46)

### Automatum

- **SYS-055** Every game is played with Automatum: there is no Automatum switch, and every play of every game, pod challenges included, has Automatum from its first state (both opening hands drawn, TRN-062); cards show the values Automatum works out (TBL-112, TBL-113). Games played before this version were removed when it was installed. (r45)
- **SYS-057** Every event's engine part is the engine's own inputs and reversible changes; the server makes it again from the inputs and refuses it when it isn't a legal input there, or when its parent isn't the head (stale) and more than the other player's table-only events came after it (SYS-012). A player's response mode and Autotap are no part of any event or of the board. (r48)
- **SYS-058** Undo's concern rule: undoing goes back past the other player's later table-only events (a card's place, staging, an early answer, a suggestion) that touched nothing the undone events changed, and those stay; any later input of theirs needs their approval (HIS-087, HIS-099). There is no redo. (r49)
- **SYS-063** Auto-skip (STK-036) is part of your private response mode and runs only on your own device: it passes where you can't respond (never your own main phases, STK-053) and answers questions with one answer as your own inputs. Other devices judge what is automatic only on public information. (r48)
- **SYS-071** Full control is one of the three response modes (STK-036), private to you and never an event; set from the quick actions (TRN-059) or, for new games, Settings (LOB-113). Catch-up playback has its own Full control quick action (TRN-059, HIS-095), also private. (r53)
- **SYS-064** A card whose card data Automatum can't read plays with its printed values (name, cost, colour indicator, the types it knows, P/T, loyalty, defense) instead of keeping Automatum from running; its rules text is a step Automatum can't do, skipped and said in the timeline (STK-030), and a deck, cube or set holding it counts as unsupported (LOB-115). (r46)
- **SYS-065** A spell Automatum casts face down (morph and the like) gets a face-down Stack entry (the opponent sees a back) and becomes a face-down permanent; nothing the opponent's table shows (texts, panels, inspects) names it, so they see only its back. (r49)
- **SYS-066** An emblem Automatum makes (CR 114) is kept in the game with whose it is and its text, for its chip by the life square (TRN-053). (r36)
- **SYS-067** Priority follows the rules: each step gives it to the active player first; a player passes it with the step arrow or Resolve (TRN-057, STK-029), so the other player may act once the active player has passed; a caster passes after a cast (STK-026); after a mana ability or special action its player keeps it, in either turn; after something resolves the active player gets it again. Lands played count one per turn. (r47)
- **SYS-068** A copy of a card that Automatum makes (a Saga that copies cards in exile and lets you cast the copies) is a token card in its owner's Exile that looks like the copied card. Cast, it stays there with its Stack entry pointing at it; like every token (TBL-103) it stops existing when it goes anywhere but the Battlefield, and a copy that isn't cast stops existing at once. (r38)
- **SYS-069** Every change made by hand is refused ("The game changes only by the rules."): tapping or untapping, moving cards, P/T, damage, tokens, the Stack, library, pile and panel actions and the combat area. What has no game effect still works: inspecting, result suggestions, moving a card within its own Battlefield (ZON-077), your combat staging (CMB-028, CMB-034) and your opening's Mulligan and Keep (TRN-062). Manual gestures shake, without a message (TBL-118). (r48)
- **SYS-072** A token Automatum makes shows the best matching token picture in the card records: of the tokens its maker card is linked to, the one with its name sharing most of its type, colours, P/T and rules text (then from the maker's set, then the newest); else a token of its name with all of those (the maker's set first, then the newest); else the plain token frame. A token that is a copy of a card (offspring, embalm, eternalize, myriad, encore, a "token that's a copy" of any card) shows that card's own picture instead, the face of its name among the game's cards. Both players see the same picture, also after undo and in branches (a device opening the game later may pick it by the token alone); it is downloaded ahead. (r49)
- **SYS-073** The server takes gameplay only as engine inputs (each player's own, the assumed answers HIS-096 allows, engine-automatic answers, random answers checked against the line's stream, HIS-042) and table changes (a card's place on its own Battlefield, result suggestions and their answers, confirming the result, your own early answers). Undo, undo requests and their answers, chat and conceding are separate requests; conceding (TRN-064) needs nobody's agreement and is always taken. (r48)
- **SYS-074** A creature spell cast for its mutate cost that merges with a creature (on top or under, its player choosing) stays one creature with Automatum: the mutating card moves from the hand to lie on the same Battlefield as a plain card, part of that creature, which has the top card's name, cost, type and power/toughness and every card's abilities; when the creature leaves the battlefield, every card of it goes to the new zone. (r46)

### Player access policy

- **SYS-036** Gameplay is the rules': each player makes their own decisions (their casts, passes, answers, declarations); the other player's answers may only be assumed where HIS-096 allows, and are confirmed or branched by their real answer. Hidden information stays hidden. (r48)
- **SYS-037** The only allowed differences between players: quick actions and defaults fit the acting player, and hidden information the opponent owns is not shown. (r48)
- **SYS-038** These rules still hold: cards go to their owner's hand, library, graveyard and exile, only game members act, you undo only back to your own actions (past the other player's only by an undo request, HIS-099), and personal proposals belong to their author (withdrawing a result suggestion). (r48)
- **SYS-039** The app hides hidden information on screen only, a deliberate trust-the-client choice. Inspection never shows a hidden side. (r52)
- **SYS-040** Menu actions are always shown and are disabled when they don't apply. An action is replaced only by its state twin (Mute ↔ Unmute, Hide ↔ Show). Each menu context has one fixed layout with no hidden buttons and no empty slots. (r28)
- **SYS-041** The battlefield feels infinite: no visible edge inside the pan area, and drags may place cards in any column, including left of the first. Only how far you can pan away from placed cards is limited. Auto-placement starts at the first free slot from column 0. (r31)

### Shared UI rules

- **SYS-042** Every context menu on the game table is one floating popup: square, opaque, bordered buttons with a light shadow on the board, with no sheet, title or Close button. Tapping outside or pressing Back dismisses it; only buttons and chips count as inside (gaps are outside), and outside taps also pass through to the table. The phase dialog (TRN-008) is a dialog instead. Menus outside a game (lobby, pods) are ordinary drop-down menus. (r11)
- **SYS-043** Only one popup can be open at a time; tapping with several fingers never opens more. (r19)
- **SYS-044** Every custom button shows press feedback. (r4)
- **SYS-045** Holding a short-label button shows its full name in a tooltip above the button (below it near the top edge), never under the finger. (r3)
- **SYS-046** Value controls everywhere use "− [value] +" with one square value field. (r13)
- **SYS-047** Input dialogs focus their field, select its text and show the keyboard. The keyboard's Enter key does what the screen's main button does. (r24)
- **SYS-048** Dialogs on regular screens share one style: a title, a close button and Cancel/main action buttons, never a lone left button. Gameplay, inspect and special popups are excluded. (r21)
- **SYS-049** Every hold to inspect behaves like the in-game one (TBL-104): it opens at once, closes on release, and early finger movement goes to the scroll or swipe underneath. (r30)
- **SYS-050** System Back first closes open menus, dialogs, panels and modes. It never undoes game actions. On the bare table, leaving needs a second Back within 2 s ("Press back again to leave"). Top-level menu destinations reset the back stack. (r30)
- **SYS-051** Shared card type order: Creatures, Planeswalkers, Battles, Instants, Sorceries, Artifacts, Enchantments, Lands, Other. A card joins the first type its front type line names. Every grouping of cards by type uses it: deck preparation (LOB-047), saved decks and cubes (LOB-056), the card list editor (LOB-105), Prepare cube (DRF-141) and the graveyard/exile view (ZON-037). (r35)
- **SYS-054** Each card list section has one colour everywhere it appears (section headers, the destination buttons, the search result squares and swipe tints): Main green and Side blue, as the preparation headers already are (LOB-047), and Maybe yellow, a darker amber where yellow text would be hard to read on a light background. Amber is used for Maybe text, outlines and chips; yellow only for the translucent swipe tint. (r35)
