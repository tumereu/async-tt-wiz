# Table

The battlefield, how cards look on it, how you touch them, their menus and inspecting cards. Automatum keeps the game: nothing on the table is changed by hand.

### Battlefield layout

- **TBL-001** Both battlefields and the combat band between them form one surface that pans and zooms together; hands, piles, life and counters stay fixed-size around it. (r2)
- **TBL-002** Battlefield cards are 28×40 dp at normal size. Each group (a card alone, a stack, a card with what is attached to it) takes a 42 dp slot in its row: a land stack 8 dp more for each land after the first, a tight stack 3 dp more for each of its second and third cards, an opened group a slot per card (TBL-130). Tapped cards rotate in place and groups never overlap, in any mix of tapped cards and also mid-rotation. (r53)
- **TBL-003** Each battlefield has two rows: the back row by the player's own edge and the creature row by the middle, next to the combat band (CMB-007). A row is card height × card size × zoom plus about 10% margin above and below, and room for the cards peeking out above another (TBL-053; 8 dp × card size × zoom for each, at least one), so cards never stick out over zone edges. (r53)
- **TBL-004** Each battlefield lays itself out from the game state (MTG Arena style); nobody places cards by hand. Creatures lie in the row nearest the middle, from the left; the back row, by the player's own edge, holds lands from the left and the other permanents (artifacts, enchantments, planeswalkers, battles, others) from its right end, the first to arrive rightmost. The right end is the battlefield's width on the screen at 1×, further right when the row needs more room. A permanent of several types lies by its first type in the shared order (SYS-051), so every creature lies in the creature row; face-down permanents lie in the creature row. The other player's battlefield mirrors the rows: creatures nearest the middle on both sides. (r53)
- **TBL-129** Within a row, groups keep the order they arrived in: a card moves only when its group or row changes (it becomes a creature, gets a counter, has an Aura attached, joins or leaves a stack), and the row closes the gap a group leaves; nothing re-sorts by itself. Joining or leaving a stack, opening or closing a group and a row closing a gap animate at the card move speed (TBL-038). (r53)
- **TBL-120** Each player's mana pool shows, while it isn't empty, as a small white pill of mana symbols (as drawn in card text, in WUBRG order, then colourless; more than 8 mana shows each kind once with its count, "×4") at the left of their battlefield on the line between their land row and the next row, live and in the timeline. (r47)
- **TBL-005** The battlefield feels endless: no field edge is visible anywhere you can pan, and its background covers the whole pan area on both sides. (r31)
- **TBL-009** In landscape each player's life square, piles and emblems sit in a bar row and the battlefields take the whole width: the opponent's in the top bar after the turn header, before their hand; yours at the left end of the bottom bar, left of the quick actions and the step button. There the piles are at most 32 dp tall, each with its own 48 dp touch target; the Stack is pinned at the right edge as in portrait (STK-007). In portrait the piles and life sit above and below the battlefield. (r57)

### Pan and zoom

- **TBL-010** Pinch zooms the battlefield between 0.65× and 2×; a two-finger drag pans it. (r2)
- **TBL-011** A one-finger drag that starts on empty battlefield pans it, as does a double-tap with the finger kept down; there is no selection rectangle. (r45)
- **TBL-012** A double tap on empty battlefield without dragging zooms to fit the whole table: both battlefields' rows and every card on them. (r53)
- **TBL-013** You can pan up to 3 card slots past the outermost cards on the left and right. (r6)
- **TBL-014** Panning past the limit stretches like a rubber band and springs back on release without overshooting. (r3)
- **TBL-015** Each direction is limited on its own: content wider than the screen can't show past its ends, and narrower content can't be pushed partly off-screen. (r3)
- **TBL-016** A pan stays in the area where it started (battlefield, hand, side panel or Stack); pinching a scrolling area never zooms the battlefield. (r10)
- **TBL-017** Camera and zoom are personal; they never change where cards are for the other player. (r2)
- **TBL-121** In landscape the table opens zoomed out (never below 0.65×) so both battlefields' rows fit between the hands, their left ends still at the left edge; your first pan or zoom takes over from there. (r53)

### How cards look

- **TBL-018** Real cards are rectangles with a border in the owner's colour; tokens have cut corners in the owner's colour with the same size; Stack and combat copies have a dashed border. (r1)
- **TBL-019** Normal cards show the art crop in the art box, with the app drawing the name and P/T; custom-image cards show the whole image with no name and P/T on a small background in the corner; cards without art show a type icon. (r4)
- **TBL-020** Card names on the table shrink and clip to the card, never ending in "…". (r13)
- **TBL-021** P/T sits in the bottom-right corner; planeswalkers show loyalty there, battles their defense, and Sagas start at 1. A creature's corner shows the P/T Automatum works out instead (TBL-112). (r37)
- **TBL-022** P/T that differs from the printed value is drawn in brown (#A0522D); a creature whose P/T Automatum works out uses TBL-112's colours instead. (r49)
- **TBL-023** A card with an until-end-of-turn P/T change shows its P/T in teal with an hourglass ⌛ in front; a creature whose P/T Automatum works out keeps TBL-112's colours with the ⌛. (r49)
- **TBL-024** Damage shows as a red-outlined badge left of P/T; it fills red when damage reaches the card's toughness (a hint only). (r16)
- **TBL-027** A card that came onto the battlefield this turn shows its name in blue (#1A56DB), also after changing sides and for new tokens and clones; combat copies follow their card; full-image cards show no mark. (r29)
- **TBL-028** Face-down battlefield cards show the card back with a "?" name on a white chip, blue when the card arrived this turn. (r30)
- **TBL-029** Face-down battlefield cards' P/T and damage are visible to both players; they start as 2/2, and turning one face up resets P/T to printed. (r45)
- **TBL-030** Hidden cards (opponent's hand, face-down outside the battlefield) show no P/T. (r45)
- **TBL-031** Every tap on a card shows a short press border, so both taps of a double tap are visible; it rotates with the card, also mid-animation. (r45)
- **TBL-112** A battlefield creature (and its combat copies) shows the P/T Automatum works out from its printed values, counters and effects: green when up in one number and down in neither, red the other way round, brown when one is up and one down, the usual ink when unchanged, with ⌛ in front while an until-end-of-turn effect changes it. Loyalty, defense, Sagas and other non-creature corners keep their own values. While the board holds data Automatum can't read, cards show their own values. (r46)
- **TBL-113** What Automatum changed on a battlefield card besides P/T shows as one violet-text chip under its title (first 32 characters): a changed type line, changed colours, keywords it gained ("Flying") or lost ("no Flying"), and its counters ("+1/+1 ×2"). (r45)
- **TBL-033** Changes to a card's P/T flash a small change tag such as "+2" (changes close together add up; about 1 s, then a 0.5 s fade); owner and face changes briefly highlight the card. Both players' changes, live and in playback, never on jumps or catch-up. (r45)

### Card sizes

- **TBL-034** Settings has a Card size slider (it also scales battlefield cards, separately from zoom) and a "Customize per area" switch that shows separate Hand, Piles, Stack and Panels sliders; saved on the device. (r13)
- **TBL-035** Settings has a Card text size slider and a "Customize per text" switch with Title and "P/T & counters" sliders. (r46)
- **TBL-036** Card and text size sliders run from 0.5× to 3×; tapping the value lets you type it within those limits, and a live sample card shows the result. (r14)

### Animations

- **TBL-037** Tapping is a plain 90° clockwise turn about the card centre in 90 ms; untapping turns it back the same way. (r5)
- **TBL-038** Moving cards fly at a steady speed (1.2 dp/ms by default, easing out, at most 350 ms, so short moves arrive early); a "Card move speed" setting scales it from 0.25× to 3×. (r14)
- **TBL-039** Turning, moving, flipping and fading each take their own natural time; a scene lasts as long as its longest part. (r15)
- **TBL-040** Every card move animates, yours and the opponent's, live and in playback, including moves between piles (library, graveyard, exile) on either side; hand cards slide aside when cards join or leave. (r51)
- **TBL-125** When one board brings several steps (your action and the passes, resolutions and triggers that follow by itself), each step that moves, taps, makes or turns something plays in event order, one after another, at the card move speed with no playback pause; a token made by a resolving Stack entry flies out of it. Until your action's outcome is in, its own board shows and the panels (whose move, the waiting bar) don't change. (r51)

### Gestures

- **TBL-041** A single tap on a card acts at once (opens its menu, raises a hand card, activates a battlefield card's ability or opens its ability chips, TBL-122), never waiting for a possible double tap. (r52)
- **TBL-042** A double tap is two taps within 400 ms and 32 dp of each other; it still works when the first tap already opened a menu. (r5)
- **TBL-043** The second tap of a double tap on a battlefield card does nothing more than its first tap did (TBL-122): no shake, no message, nothing tapped by hand (SYS-069); nor does a combat copy's (CMB-003). (r56)
- **TBL-122** One tap activates: tapping your battlefield card with exactly one ability usable now (mana abilities and special actions too) activates it as its menu row would (TBL-126): mana goes to your pool at once and you keep priority; costs, targets or choices go through the cast flow (STK-023). With several usable abilities the tap opens their chips (TBL-124); with none, or while a cast or choice is open or the board is still being worked out, the menu (TBL-115). While attackers or blockers are declared a tap only stages combat (CMB-047). A tap on a group of several cards is the group's (TBL-130). (r53)
- **TBL-124** With several abilities you can activate now, tapping the card (a card alone, the one card a group's tap means, or a member of an opened group, TBL-130) opens a row of chips above it (below it with no room above), like a hand card's (ZON-080): one white, violet-outlined chip per ability reading as on the card, cost first and then the effect ("{2}{G}, {T}: Draw a card", "{T}: Add {G}"), the cost in mana symbols; a long effect wraps onto more rows, never cut. The chips take the fewest rows they can, each row centred: in card order when that already takes the fewest, otherwise the fewest-row arrangement closest to card order (always the same for the same chips). Mana and tap symbols ({G}, {T}, {X}, hybrid, Phyrexian, snow, energy) are drawn as symbols wherever text shows them: chips, menus, panels, event texts, inspect tiles and hold texts; {T} is a turning arrow, as printed, and {Q} the same arrow mirrored on a purple badge; the letters in the badges are optically centred. Tapping a chip activates it; holding one shows the card's name and the ability's whole text in a card-like popup. A tap elsewhere or on the card again closes them. (r57)
- **TBL-130** A stack (TBL-053) and a card with what is attached to it are each a group, and a tap on a closed group is one tap for the group. When it can mean only one thing it does that at once: exactly one member can do what a tap would do now (activate an ability, one-tap mana, stage an attacker, pick a blocker) or is a legal pick while targets are chosen; the identical cards of a stack count as one, the tap acting on an untapped one first (a tap on a land stack taps one land). When it could mean several things (an equipped creature whose creature and Equipment both have usable abilities, several different members that are legal targets), the first tap opens the group in place: its members side by side, the usable or pickable ones glowing, the rest of its row moving along; the next tap is the tapped member's own, and using a member closes the group (its ability chips or menu keep it open). A tap anywhere else closes it. With nothing in it usable now, the tap is its top card's. An attached card's strip is never a target of its own. (r53)
- **TBL-044** Only battlefield cards can be tapped; a card leaving the battlefield untaps, and double taps never turn hand cards or cards elsewhere. (r12)
- **TBL-047** Holding a card for 350 ms inspects it (TBL-104) without changing anything; moving the finger first starts a drag or a pan instead (a battlefield card is never placed by hand, ZON-077), and an open preview never turns into a move. (r54)
- **TBL-048** Holding empty hand space shows nothing, and holding a library never reveals its cards. (r29)

### Dragging, stacks and groups

- **TBL-051** Dragging a card does what the drag means to Automatum (ZON-077); one card is dragged at a time. (r45)
- **TBL-052** A drag shows a lifted card and highlights where it will land; a cancelled or invalid drop puts the card back and records nothing. (r45)
- **TBL-053** Identical permanents lie in one stack: the same card and face, controller and owner, and the same counters, damage, P/T and marks as worked out (TBL-112, TBL-113), entered-this-turn mark (TBL-027) and in combat or not. Tapped and untapped ones stay in one stack, a tapped one turned inside it. Face-down permanents, cards in combat and cards with something attached never stack. A stack has no size limit. Auras and Equipment lie behind the card they are attached to (on its battlefield), peeking out 8 dp above it, and so do a merged creature's other cards (SYS-074). (r53)
- **TBL-054** The lands of a stack fan out, each 8 dp right of the one before, so their count shows at a glance; other stacks (tokens above all) lie tight, their second and third cards showing 3 dp each, with a "×N" count badge on the top card, the last to arrive. (r53)

### Card menus

- **TBL-064** Card menus (battlefield, combat and panel cards) put their buttons in a grid of 5 columns of 48 dp cells, each an icon plus one short label; fewer buttons just make fewer rows. Labels wrap only between words: where a word or the label's two lines don't fit the cell (large system text), the label is drawn smaller instead of breaking or being cut; the quick-actions cells do the same. Holding a button shows its full name (SYS-045). (r16)
- **TBL-067** The menu opens on the side of the card with the most room, preferring above your own cards and below the opponent's. Card menus and the other table popups (piles, life, the Stack list) keep off both hands wherever they fit elsewhere: a menu of your Library, say, opens just above your hand instead of over it. (r47)
- **TBL-068** A tap outside the menu closes it and still goes through to the table (double taps and drags work); only buttons and chips count as inside, gaps do not. Back also closes it. (r11)
- **TBL-069** A press that starts within 280 ms of the menu opening does nothing, so a fast second tap cannot hit a button by accident. (r8)
- **TBL-071** Each kind of card has one fixed one-page menu (TBL-116): battlefield cards and Graveyard or Exile panel cards; a hand card (ZON-079) and a combat copy (CMB-003) have no menu. Stack entries have no card menu: tapping the Stack opens the Stack list (STK-018). (r57)
- **TBL-115** A card's menu (TBL-071) starts with one full-width row with a violet icon per thing Automatum offers for it: its casts ("Cast", or "Cast · " and the way, such as a flashback or an Adventure), a land's "Play", then each activated ability by its printed text ("{T}: Add {G}."), mana abilities and special actions included. Rows allowed now are enabled, the rest greyed (TBL-123); "Automatum is working out what this card can do…" shows greyed while it still does. (r57)
- **TBL-123** A greyed menu row shakes when tapped; holding it shows why: "Bob holds priority now.", "Only in your main phase while the Stack is empty.", "A loyalty ability: once each turn, in your main phase while the Stack is empty.", "Already used as often as allowed this turn.", "Storm Spark can't be cast now." or "Grovewatch Druid: Automatum doesn't allow that now (a cost it can't pay, or nothing to target)."; while a cast or an Automatum choice is open, its own message (STK-025, STK-032). The other player's cards list their abilities greyed ("Only its controller can do that."). (r44)
- **TBL-126** A menu row is used like a cast: the cast panel when there is a real choice (STK-023; Autotap pays), with the ability's text and "Activate", then one step. An ability that goes on the Stack is then passed on by its player as after a cast (STK-026); a mana ability or special action without choices goes out at once and its player keeps priority (in either player's turn), unless it set off a trigger, which is passed on as after a cast. (r44)
- **TBL-116** A combat copy has no menu (there is no Combat menu): its taps select it and take it out of combat (CMB-003). Battlefield and panel cards have nothing below the rows: no Inspect or Other side (holding a card inspects it, TBL-104), no Declare and no "Attack →" (CMB-048). A card whose menu would hold nothing opens no menu: the tap does nothing. The menu has one page. (r57)
- **TBL-117** Cards Automatum lets you use now (cast, play, or activate an ability other than a mana ability, special actions included) glow softly in cyan from behind, exactly as it allows at that moment: never the other player's cards, nor a permanent whose only use now is mana. Hand, battlefield and panel cards glow; nothing glows in the timeline, the opening or while a cast or choice is open. A closed group of several (TBL-130) glows as a whole when any member does (or is a legal target), in a colour saying what a tap will do: cyan (or red-orange while attackers are declared, a legal target's violet frame while targets are picked) when it acts at once, white with a thin grey edge when it opens the group. Glows fade in or out in 0.22 s and stay while the next board is worked out, so they never blink. While you declare attackers, your creatures that may attack glow red-orange instead (CMB-028). While a finger is down on a glowing card (cyan, violet target or blocker, red-orange, a group's white; on the battlefield, in the hand or in a panel) or on a combat target chip (CMB-013), its glow grows a little and brightens, so it feels pressable; it goes back on release. (r55)
- **TBL-118** A gesture Automatum doesn't allow says nothing: the card, pile or life square it was made on gives a small, brief sideways shake (0.16 s, three swings of at most 3 dp; every shake in the app is this one, greyed controls too). A refusal inside an open cast panel is said in the panel (STK-024); failures that aren't Automatum's refusals (offline, still working out) keep their message. (r52)
- **TBL-128** What shakes (TBL-118): a card that can't be cast or played now (ZON-079), tapping a card by hand, a manual drag (ZON-077; a card dropped back where it was doesn't), a Library with nothing to offer (ZON-078), a tap on a non-target while targets are picked (STK-047), actions refused while your cast is open (STK-025) or while the other player chooses (STK-032), and a cast dropped because its card can't be cast or paid for now or the table changed. (r52)
- **TBL-119** There are no virtual-tabletop controls, because Automatum keeps the game: no notes, pings, text marks on Stack entries, links, colour counters on cards or players, statuses, dice or coins, tokens or combat areas made by hand, Temp piles, selection or interaction modes. Chat, holding to inspect (TBL-104), the timeline and settings stay. (r52)

### Card state rules

- **TBL-095** Damage and until-end-of-turn P/T clear from every card when the turn passes and when a card changes zone; moving between battlefields keeps them. (r46)
- **TBL-097** The owner is whose deck the card came from; the controller is whichever battlefield it is on. (r45)

### Tokens

- **TBL-101** A new token joins the stack of identical permanents (TBL-053), whatever its size, or arrives as a group of its own in its row (TBL-004, TBL-129). (r53)
- **TBL-102** Tokens and copies lie in the row of their type like every permanent: creature tokens with the creatures, land tokens with the lands, the others in the back row from the right (TBL-004). (r53)
- **TBL-103** A token moved to a graveyard, exile or library stops existing. (r28)

### Inspecting cards

- **TBL-104** Inspect opens only by holding a card (TBL-047) or a Stack ability (STK-050), wherever cards show: the table, hands, the Stack and its list, piles and panels, choice screens, the cast panel, draft screens, deck building and preparation, search and card lists; no menu has Inspect. It shows the card images over the dimmed screen, each as large as fits (in portrait one under another, in landscape side by side), and closes on release. In a game, under each battlefield card (beside it in landscape) a dark panel lists only what is changed on it or added to it, one line each: its counters ("+1/+1 counter ×2"; a planeswalker's loyalty and a battle's defense are its corner, not lines), damage ("1 damage"), each continuous effect without its source ("+3/+3 until end of turn", "Flying", "Loses flying", "Becomes blue", "Controlled by you"), "Attached to <card>" and "Entered this turn"; an unchanged card has no panel. A thin white line separates the cards when several show; a card's faces stay together. (r55)
- **TBL-131** Pinned Inspect: in a game, dragging sideways either way (right too, for a card at the table's side) while holding a card on the table, in a hand or pile or in an Automatum panel shrinks the cards to a little over half the width, moving them to the left edge, while their changes (TBL-104) slide in at their right and a round close button comes in at the top right; let go past half way it stays open (the close button or Back closes it), short of it it closes as usual. There each change is a dark block of its own in slightly smaller text, and a card without changes has a faded "No changes" centred at its right. A tap on a change, or holding it, shows its source under it ("From Ember Blade"). Shrinking draws the picture already shown, scaled, never loading it again. Once the finger has scrolled up or down, sideways movement never starts it. (r56)
- **TBL-132** Rule explainers: holding something shown as a chip that names a keyword or keyword action (a cost chip such as "Forage" in a question, a combat keyword badge such as "flying", the attacker's "Exert" chip) shows a dark popup with the keyword's name in bold and what it means in a sentence or two of plain words, from Automatum's own glossary (never the rules' or a card's reminder text); in pinned Inspect (TBL-131) a change line naming one ("Flying", "+3/+3, trample until end of turn") shows it under the line on a tap or hold, with the source. Every keyword and keyword action Automatum knows has an explanation. (r57)
- **TBL-127** While inspecting, moving the finger up or down scrolls the images at least as far as the finger moves, sped up so that a drag over 90% of the screen height crosses the whole view; movement past either end is not stored, so turning back scrolls at once. It scrolls the same when the card is held in an Automatum panel (its source at the right included). (r55)
- **TBL-105** The hold preview works on every face-up card, live and in timeline mode, and holding pauses timeline playback. (r8)
- **TBL-106** Inspect shows every face of a card you see face up (or hold in your own region): one picture per side of a double-faced card (transform, modal double-faced, meld, battle, double-faced token), the face shown on the table first; a card whose parts share one printed image (split, adventure, flip, aftermath, Room) shows it once, a flipped flip card upside down. A face whose picture isn't downloaded shows the placeholder card with its name. (r52)
- **TBL-108** Split cards, Rooms, Planes and Battles turn sideways when inspected; sideways cards are no taller than upright ones (at most 448 dp). Aftermath and flip cards show upright; Inspect has no Rotate button. (r52)
- **TBL-109** Inspecting your own face-down card shows its front dimmed with a "Face down" label; inspecting never shows a hidden side to the opponent, who sees a "Covered card" tile. (r52)
- **TBL-111** Holding a closed group (TBL-130) inspects all its members in the same scrolling view (one under another in portrait, side by side in landscape): a stack's top card first, a card with attachments before what is attached to it; each shows only what the table shows of it (a face-down card stays hidden from the opponent), with its own change lines (TBL-104) and a thin line between members. In an opened group, holding a member inspects that card alone. (r55)
