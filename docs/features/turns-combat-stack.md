# Turns, combat and Stack

Turn and step tracking, whose move it is, the top and bottom game bars, life, game start and result suggestions (`TRN`), the combat area (`CMB`) and the Stack (`STK`).

### Turn structure

- **TRN-001** A turn has 12 positions: Untap, Upkeep, Draw, Main 1, Beginning of combat, Declare attackers, Declare blockers, Combat damage, End of combat, Main 2, End step, Cleanup. The turn moves through Automatum (TRN-057), which adds a First-strike damage step when a combat needs one (TRN-056). (r37)
- **TRN-056** A combat in which a creature with first strike or double strike is fighting has two combat damage steps (CR 510.4): First-strike damage (a bolt icon, short name "1st strike"), then Combat damage. The step button shows it like any step; the phase dialog shows its square between Blockers and Damage from the moment that combat reaches it until the combat ends. (r46)
- **TRN-057** The step arrow moves the turn on for you only (the phase dialog only shows where the turn is, TRN-009): Next (a tap) passes your priority, or declares what the combat lanes hold when you declare attackers or blockers; End turn (holding it) makes your device pass your priority until the turn ends, attacking with nothing. The other player's points are assumed (HIS-096), engine-automatic or waited for, never passed for them. Each step's own actions happen by themselves; going back to an earlier step or changing the step any other way is refused: "The turn moves on through Automatum: use Next step or End turn." (r48)
- **TRN-060** When the game comes back to the same position with only passes nobody could avoid in between (CR 104.4b), the player holding priority is asked a "Loop" choice: "The game is in a loop of actions nobody can stop, which makes it a draw (CR 104.4b). End it as a draw, or continue?" with Continue and Draw. Draw makes the game a draw (TRN-065); Continue goes on. Abilities that keep triggering without anyone getting priority stop after 200 rounds with the same choice, "Possible loop: …". (r48)
- **TRN-002** Turn numbers count both players' turns, so turn 1 is only the starting player's first turn. Next step from Cleanup, or End turn, passes the turn to the other player's Untap. (r33)
- **TRN-052** A turn change Automatum makes names whose turn begins, so an extra turn goes to the same player again (turn number up by one, at Untap); the pass that begins it reads "… · Turn N → <name>" (HIS-091). (r49)
- **TRN-003** Passing the turn clears every card's damage and until-end-of-turn P/T at once. (r46)
- **TRN-004** The step arrow moves the game on only for you: it shows while you may act (your move, or a decision stop in playback) and is hidden while the other player must act, in either player's turn (I-D56 §14). (r48)

### Top bar

- **TRN-005** The top bar's first line reads "Your turn · N" or "<name>'s turn · N" in the turn player's colour; only the name shortens on a narrow screen. (r11)
- **TRN-006** Under it, the move pill: a solid green "Your move" when it is your move (TRN-058: the game really waits for you), otherwise an outlined "Waiting · <name>". Tapping it does nothing: the move isn't passed by hand. (r50)
- **TRN-007** The step button in the top bar is a square in the current phase's colour, showing the step's icon and its short name, the same names as in the phase dialog (TRN-008) (no border colour, no turn number). It flashes on a step change and opens the phase dialog. (r35)
- **TRN-008** The phase dialog is a real dialog, not a floating popup. Its title is the step with its phase (e.g. "Combat · Declare attackers"), then the turn player ("Your turn" / "<name>'s turn"); phase labels sit above each row of step squares, and the current step is outlined in the turn player's colour. Step squares use short names that always fit on one line on a phone: Untap, Upkeep, Draw · Main 1 · Begin, Attackers, Blockers, Damage, End · Main 2 · End, Cleanup (and with Automatum "1st strike" between Blockers and Damage while a combat has that step, TRN-056). (r37)
- **TRN-009** The phase dialog only shows where the turn is: its step squares do nothing and it has no buttons; the game moves on only with the step arrow (TRN-063). (r47)
- **TRN-010** The top bar's Table actions button opens the Table actions dialog: Timeline, Suggest game end (or Accept result while the opponent's suggestion is waiting) and Concede (TRN-064), each an icon and its name. Nothing in it changes the board by hand: no combat area, tokens, dice or coins (TBL-119). (r45)
- **TRN-011** Opening a game shows "Loading table…" until the table loads; "Table unavailable" only when it really fails. (r13)

### Whose turn and whose move

- **TRN-012** Whose TURN shows on the battlefields: only the turn player's battlefield is tinted (your own green-ish, the opponent's red-ish, the land band a little darker); the other battlefield is neutral grey. (r11)
- **TRN-013** Whose MOVE shows on the bars: the top and bottom bars take the light colour of the player whose move it is (TRN-058), never part of an event. In timeline mode both bars are grey. (r50)
- **TRN-014** The move follows whom the game really waits for (TRN-058); no event carries it. (r50)
- **TRN-058** The move is the player the game really waits for, the same record "Your move" notifications use (LOB-073): "Your move" when the game waits for you, "<opponent>'s move" when it waits for them. While one player plays on assumptions about the other and nothing waits for the other, the acting player has the move for both, also while the other player's playback stops at an assumption they may answer (they still can). An open undo request (HIS-099) or result suggestion (TRN-047) is the move of the player who must answer it; a result is the move of whoever hasn't been shown it yet (TRN-065). The game list (LOB-022, LOB-024), the move pill (TRN-006) and the bar colours (TRN-013) all show this one move, never from an event; when the game waits for nobody (both have seen a result) the list says "Move not available". (r50)

### Bottom bar and quick actions

- **TRN-015** The bottom bar shows your toggles on either player's turn (TRN-059) and the step arrow (TRN-063) only while you may act; while the other player must act its place stays empty, in your own turn too; during your opening your quick actions are Mulligan and Keep (TRN-062). (r48)
- **TRN-016** Quick actions are equal-width buttons in the popup style (icon plus small black label, 48dp tall) filling the width left of the step arrow, with press feedback. A greyed quick action stays in place. (r14)
- **TRN-017** The step arrow sits in the bottom-right corner, arrow-shaped, about 72dp wide, with the next step's icon above its short name, the same names as in the phase dialog (TRN-008) ("End turn" when the next step passes the turn). Tap = Next step; hold = End turn, with a small haptic tick. It says what a tap does instead (TRN-063). (r42)
- **TRN-063** The step arrow is white with a grey outline and says under an icon what a tap does, in MTG Arena's words: "To combat", "End turn", "Next", "Pass", "Attack (2)" / "No attack", "Block (1)" / "No blocks", "Resolve" with something on the Stack (a tap passes your priority). It acts only for you (TRN-057): there is no "Pass" for the other player, and it is hidden while they must act (TRN-015). It is greyed when it can't act (your cast open, the opening, another choice open, a declaration Automatum doesn't allow); then a tap shakes it and holding it says why. Otherwise hold is End turn. (r48)
- **TRN-023** Each quick action is one Undo step, however many changes it makes. (r15)
- **TRN-059** The quick-actions row is your own private settings for this game, on either player's turn, left of the step arrow: the response mode (a tap goes on to the next of Full control, Normal and Auto-skip; its name shows), "Always assume resolves" and "Autotap mana", equal-width cells with an icon and their words at a readable size; on, filled violet with a check; off, white with an empty ring; holding one says what it does. Only you see or change them (kept on the server for you and this game, I-D56 §7). There are no other quick actions then, except your opening's (Mulligan and Keep, TRN-062) and the combat staging ones (Rest → and Clear, CMB-028, CMB-034). (r48)

### Game start and mulligans

- **TRN-026** The game setup's Starting player box offers Random or either player, plus an "Alternate" switch in its top-right corner, on by default: after each play ends, the player who did NOT start it is set to start the next. You can still pick by hand. (r28)
- **TRN-027** A game begins with both players in the status "Mulliganing" (the engine's opening, both at the same time). While yours is, your quick actions are "Mulligan" and "Keep" (TRN-062); keeping makes you Ready. The starting player may play their first turn on an assumed keep of the other player's (HIS-096): at once while the other player is away, else from "Assume keep" in the step arrow's place (TRN-066). While both openings are open each player's own mulligan is their move (listed and notified as theirs, LOB-022, LOB-072). (r50)
- **TRN-028** The starting player's turn-1 controls wait until the other player kept or their keep is assumed (TRN-027). (r48)
- **TRN-029** There is no Turn 0: both players mulligan during turn 1, while their opening status is set. (r25)
- **TRN-062** The opening follows the game's rules (opening hand 7, the first mulligan free, London mulligans, CR 103.5), run by the engine with both players declaring at the same time. While your status is "Mulliganing" your quick actions are "Mulligan" (your hand goes back, your library is shuffled and seven are drawn again) and "Keep" (with cards owed to the bottom the Keep / Bottom screen opens first). (r48)

### Life

- **TRN-030** Each player starts at 20 life. Tapping a life square opens a floating Life panel with that player's life, which only the rules change (I-D52), and for the other player their presence line (TRN-043); the life row sits on the panel edge facing the life square, also in landscape, where the panel opens beside the square. (r45)
- **TRN-034** Portrait: each player's row shows the life square and packed piles; the room beside them stays free. (r45)
- **TRN-035** Landscape: each player's life square and piles sit in the sidebar, with a free row under them (above them on the opponent's side). (r45)

### Emblems

- **TRN-053** With Automatum, a player's emblems (a planeswalker's ultimate, say) show as violet chips, each "Emblem: " and the emblem's text, cut after two lines. In portrait they stack, oldest first, in the free room right of the player's piles in their life row, clear of the hands; in landscape (or when that room is too narrow) by the life square: under the opponent's square, above your own, the oldest nearest. They are drawn only, so taps reach the table beneath. (r47)
- **TRN-069** A player's dungeon in the command zone (LOB-117) shows as a small card with its picture in a fourth place after their Library, Graveyard and Exile (in landscape, where the sidebar has no room for it, in the free row by the piles, under the Library), and the room its venture marker is on as a violet chip "Room: <room>", first among the emblem chips (TRN-053). Tapping the dungeon opens Inspect; holding it shows it large. It can't be dragged. Choosing a dungeon to venture into shows the dungeons as cards; a room's ability on the Stack shows its dungeon. (r50)

### Opponent presence

- **TRN-042** The opponent's life square has a small dot in its corner: green when they have this game open and touched it in the last 2 minutes, yellow when open but idle, grey when away. It is hidden while you are disconnected. (r29)
- **TRN-043** The opponent's Life panel has an info line with the same dot: "Online", "Idle 7 min", "Last seen 12 min ago", "Last seen 3 h ago", "Last seen Oct 27" or "Not seen yet". (r29)

### Result suggestions

- **TRN-046** Suggest game end opens a chooser My win / Draw game, then a confirm dialog ("Suggest my win?" or "Suggest draw?") with an optional one-line "Reason", at most 200 characters (placeholder "Infinite combo"). (r45)
- **TRN-047** A waiting suggestion shows as a panel on the table, not a dialog: "<name> proposed their win." (or loss, or "a draw"; "You proposed your win." for the author). The opponent gets Decline and Accept (an agreed result), the author Withdraw; made at the newest event. Answering it is the opponent's move (TRN-058). (r50)
- **TRN-064** Concede (a flag icon) in Table actions asks "Concede?" ("You lose this game at once.", Cancel / Concede, red); Concede ends the game at once as your loss (CR 104.3a), also while a suggestion waits and while you are behind (also when the other player's latest action hasn't reached you yet): then the game first branches at your local head, so the result never depends on what you haven't seen (I-D56 §11). (r48)
- **TRN-065** When the rules end a game (a player at 0 life or otherwise losing by the rules, a draw, the loop draw of TRN-060), each player sees the result once it is shown to them, in a panel above the hand: "You won", "You lost" or "Draw" with OK; while the other player hasn't caught up it reads "… · waiting for <name> to catch up" with OK greyed. A result you haven't seen yet (also a result agreed while you were away) is your move: you get a "Your move" notification (LOB-072) and the game is listed as yours until you reach it. Once both have seen it either player may press OK, and the result is final and recorded (LOB-046); the other player is notified (LOB-111). Until then the result can't be declined, but Undo (HIS-084) still takes the step back. (r49)
- **TRN-066** The wait (I-D56 §14): while the other player must act, faint words over the battlefield (like timeline mode's, never taking a touch or covering the board) say why: "<name> may respond to <spell>", "<name> may act · <step>", "<name> is declaring blockers", "<name> is deciding on a mulligan", "<name> is choosing · <card>" (the card only when you can see it, else just "<name> is choosing"), "Waiting for <name>"; their presence shows on their life square (the dot). Where the answer may be assumed (HIS-096), Assume resolves, Assume blockers or Assume keep stands in the step arrow's place, with no timeout. While an undo request is open the words say so and the requester has Cancel request there. (r50)
- **TRN-067** Information rule (I-D56 §5): while your line rests on an unconfirmed assumption, nothing you do or assume may show you a card you couldn't see (the other player's hand or library, face-down cards, random discards; your own library never counts), nor a random result (a coin flip, die roll or random choice: whether one would show you a card can't be told before it happens). Such an action is refused: the card shakes and holding it says "Wait for the other player to catch up: this would show you hidden cards or a random result."; such an assumption isn't made and the wait reads "Waiting for <name> · going on would show you hidden cards or a random result" (TRN-066). The server checks the same for cards. (r48)
- **TRN-068** The unconfirmed mark (I-D56 §14): while actions of yours rest on answers the other player hasn't confirmed yet, a small violet dot sits at the step arrow's corner (no number); a tap or hold says "<name> hasn't seen your last moves yet; if <name> answers differently, they'll be taken back". Only you see it. (r50)
- **TRN-048** Decline and Withdraw are their own events, not undos. (r18)
- **TRN-049** While a suggestion waits, every other game action is blocked for a player once the board shown to them includes it; chat, timeline browsing, hold-preview and Inspect still work. (r48)
- **TRN-050** A suggestion left behind on a discarded line (an earlier real answer of the other player's branched, HIS-097) no longer locks anything. (r48)
- **TRN-051** The suggestion panel appears in playback once its event is reached; its buttons work only at the live end, with playback paused. (r18)

### Combat area

- **CMB-001** Combat uses a temporary combat area between the two battlefields. Automatum opens it when attackers are staged and closes it itself (SYS-069); nobody opens or closes it by hand. (r45)
- **CMB-002** Attackers and blockers are combat copies of real cards: the real card stays where it is, and anything done to a copy's card applies to the real card. A card has at most one copy per combat area. (r1)
- **CMB-003** Combat copies are always upright and can't be tapped. A first tap selects one (CMB-032); tapping the selected copy opens its one-page Combat menu: Inspect, Attack →, Remove from combat (TBL-116). Double-tap does nothing; hold inspects. (r45)
- **CMB-004** Remove from combat takes the copy out of the declaration Automatum waits for (CMB-028); the real card is unchanged. (r45)
- **CMB-006** When the real card leaves the battlefield, all its combat copies disappear; surviving blockers of a removed attacker stay in place. Closing the area removes all copies but leaves real cards and their state unchanged. (r1)
- **CMB-007** Rows for the attacking player: the target headers on top, then blockers, then, after a gap for the block arrows (CMB-031), attackers. The defender sees it mirrored: attackers on top, their blockers below, the headers at the bottom. The combat area is a white rounded panel sized to its content, floating over the middle of the table: opening it never moves or resizes the battlefields, which show around it (the narrow band between them shows each battlefield's own colour, the upper half the upper battlefield's, the lower half the lower one's). The panel covers the battlefield cards under it, and only the panel takes combat drops: a card dropped beside it lands on the battlefield. A round fold button (a "fold" icon; holding it says "Hide the combat area") just right of the panel's top-right corner folds it away to see the battlefield under it (each viewer's own choice); "Combat ▴" in the middle of the table brings it back, and a new combat area shows again. Its cards are never smaller than 44dp wide on screen, whatever the card size or the table's zoom: the panel is screen-level, so zooming out (or landscape's opening view, TBL-121) never shrinks them below that. (r47)
- **CMB-008** Pairs are packed groups without dividers between them (only lane separators), centred as a group and re-centred when added; cards sit centred in their slot (one attacker over two blockers is centred), with about two card-border widths of margin. (r40)
- **CMB-009** Dragging a battlefield card into the combat area makes its copy there. Dropping left or right of all groups makes a new pair at that end; near a divider, a new pair there. While dragging, a faint shorter divider shows where it lands; reordering works the same both ways. Dragging a copy between rows changes its role, except a defending player's blocker dropped in an attacker row, which blocks that attacker (CMB-031). (r40)
- **CMB-027** A card dragged into the combat area (from the battlefield or from another combat slot) that covers at least half the width of a card in the row it is dropped in goes beside that card, on the side the dragged card's centre is closer to: it joins that card's pair, or over a card of its own pair it changes places within the pair. With less overlap the gaps of CMB-009 apply. This wins over divider drops and reordering pairs, in both rows, except a card of the defending player dropped in an attacker row, which blocks that pair's attacker (CMB-031, CMB-034). (r40)

### Attack targets

- **CMB-012** While combat is open, every attackable target always has its own lane in the same attacker/blocker rows: the defending player first, then each face-up planeswalker and battle in battlefield order, including your own Sieges (protected by the opponent). An empty lane is one card slot wide. (r27)
- **CMB-013** Lane headers sit in their own row, never covered, as a pill tinted with the colour of the target's side: the player lane shows the defending player's round avatar and name, card lanes tiny art and name; a hidden target shows only "→ Face-down card". At the header's right end the target's life, loyalty or defense (none for a hidden target); loyalty and defense are the card's loyalty and defense counters. A planeswalker or battle that left the battlefield keeps its lane as "→ <name> (gone)" without a value: its attackers stay attacking but attack nothing and deal no combat damage (CR 506.4c). Names are always shown in full at the title size of the area's cards; lanes widen to fit, with room for the damage preview's "16 → 11" (CMB-029), and a too-wide area pans with the table. (r40)
- **CMB-014** Lane separators are 2dp, fairly dark, scaling with card size, run through the header row, and keep about 2dp of space around cards. (r40)
- **CMB-015** Dropping an attacker into another lane, also an empty one, retargets its whole pair (blockers stay attached); dropping into the blocker row makes it a blocker. (r31)
- **CMB-016** "Attack →" opens one row per target, each with a small image (avatar or art) and the full name on an opaque background; the current target is marked and greyed. The icon is a crosshair. On a staged attacker's own card menu, Attack → retargets its copy. (r46)

### Blocks and arrows

- **CMB-031** Every block is an arrow from the blocker to the attacker it blocks, drawn in the gap between the blocker and attacker rows. A blocker sits in the pair of the first attacker it blocks; a blocker blocking several attackers is one card with an arrow to each (no second copy), and an attacker's blockers include those arrows (an attacker blocked only by an arrow counts as blocked). The timeline reads "<name> blocked with N" (HIS-091). (r49)
- **CMB-032** Tapping a combat copy selects it: a violet frame, the copies it fights (the attackers it blocks, or an attacker's blockers) framed in amber and its arrows amber. Tapping the selected copy again opens its Combat menu (CMB-003); tapping the empty table clears the selection. The selection is the viewer's own and lasts only for the combat step it was made in. While blockers are being declared, tapping one of the defending player's creatures picks it as the blocker to place instead (CMB-034). (r40)

### Combat with Automatum

- **CMB-028** While attackers are awaited the lanes are the attacker's staging, shared as they make it: each change is an event of its own that the other player's table shows (their lanes, as they play back), and the declaration ends it in its own event (I-D54 §8); behind the newest event (a decision stop in playback) it stays on the attacker's device and goes out only as the declaration. One target lane is active, framed in violet (at first the defending player's; tapping another lane's header makes it active). Tapping one of your creatures stages it as an attacker of the active target (its copy appears in that lane), tapping it again unstages it; a creature that can't attack that target isn't staged and Automatum's reason shows. Dragging works the same way. The quick actions are "Rest → <target>" and "Clear"; the step arrow reads "Attack (N)" ("No attack" with nothing staged) and declares what the lanes hold as one step. Declared lanes show Automatum's combat: a pair per attacker, blockers by their arrows (CMB-031). (r49)
- **CMB-034** While blockers are awaited, tapping one of your creatures selects it as the blocker to place (CMB-032) and the attackers it may block glow violet; tapping an attacker then blocks it (an arrow), tapping an attacker it already blocks removes that block. Dragging does the same. The staging is the defender's, shared as they make it like the attacker's (CMB-028: the attacker's table shows the arrows while they arrange them) and ended by the declaration; behind the head (playing back assumed blocks, HIS-095) it stays on their device and goes out only as the declaration. The quick action is "Clear"; the step arrow reads "Block (N)" or "No blocks" and declares. The attacker's assumed blocks are a separate screen of their own (CMB-040). (r49)
- **CMB-029** In the declare blockers step (also while a division of combat damage is open, and in the first-strike damage step) both players see on the combat area what combat damage would do if nobody does anything more, with the blocks being arranged and the suggested divisions: a red "−3" badge over each copy that would take damage, "☠" beside it when it would die; there is no Pass for the other player of the turn. (r48)
- **CMB-033** A division of combat damage Automatum asks for (the attacker's first, then the defender's, CR 510.1) opens as a panel docked above the hand (STK-044) for the player who divides, one at a time with "1 of 2" when that step has more: the creature's name, P/T and trample, "N damage to divide among what it fights", a switch "In order" / "Free", a row per creature with its card drawn small, the damage it gets and "lethal ☠", a hint line, the eye and OK; it opens filled in with the suggestion. A division whose in-order result can't depend on the order is made with the suggestion by the device running the step, for every player, without asking (I-D56 §6). Nobody divides for the other player. (r48)
- **CMB-035** A real Battlefield card in combat carries a small chip over its top edge: violet "→ <target>" while staged in the declare attackers step; grey "attacking" or "blocking" otherwise, only on cards wide enough for it (larger card sizes). Attackers' copies show violet badges for the keywords that matter in combat (flying, reach, menace, trample, first strike, double strike, deathtouch, lifelink, vigilance), as Automatum works them out, on their side away from the arrows. A card's chips and badges are stacked one per line, none wider than the card (their text made smaller to fit), so neighbours' never overlap. (r40)
- **CMB-036** While attackers are staged, a staged attacker with enlist has an "Enlist" chip (a person-plus icon) right under its copy, nearest the card, before its badges; the attacking player taps it to pick what it enlists: their creatures that may be enlisted (untapped, not staged, without summoning sickness) glow violet on the Battlefield, and tapping one enlists it (one per enlist ability). The chip then reads "+N" (N the enlisted creatures' power), filled violet, and the enlisted card carries a violet "enlisted" chip; tapping the filled chip takes the enlistment back. Tapping anything else, or the empty table, stops picking. Attacking with the step arrow taps the enlisted creature and adds its power until end of turn (CR 702.154a). A creature staged as an attacker is never enlisted. Holding the chip says what enlist does. (r47)
- **CMB-037** A staged attacker's optional costs to attack ("You may exert this creature as it attacks", CR 508.1g) are toggle chips under its copy ("Exert" with an empty or checked box, filled violet when on); holding one shows the ability's text. A cost the rules make a creature pay to attack or block ("can't attack unless … pays {2}", "… sacrifice a land", "… pay 2 life", CR 508.1h, 509.1d) shows as an amber chip under the staged attacker's or drafted blocker's copy ("{2}" with mana symbols, "2 life", "Sacrifice"); holding it shows the ability that asks it. Every cost is paid as the step arrow declares: with Autotap mana on (LOB-113) the mana is paid by itself as casting pays it; otherwise, and for any other choice of the payment (which land to sacrifice), Automatum's choice opens for that player (STK-030). A cost that can't be paid greys the step arrow with Automatum's reason (TRN-063). The other player sees the chosen costs' chips and the costs, without changing them. (r47)
- **CMB-038** A staged attacker with banding has a "Band" chip (a link icon): tapping it starts forming its band; tapping other staged attackers then puts them into its band or takes them out (instead of unstaging them), until the chip or the empty table is tapped again. A band of two or more is joined by a violet bar on its copies' arrow side, each copy with a filled "Band" chip; the step arrow attacks with it as an attacking band (CR 702.22c), greyed with Automatum's reason when the band isn't allowed (more than one creature without banding, different targets). A creature drafted to block one member blocks the whole band once blockers are declared: an arrow to each (CMB-031). Unstaging an attacker, or Clear, drops its choices (CMB-036–CMB-038), and they last only for the declare attackers step they were made in; the choices travel with the shared staging (CMB-028), so the defender sees them too, and the strip reads "<name> staged N attackers". (r49)
- **CMB-039** A battle is attacked through its lane (CMB-012) as Automatum allows: its header shows its defense counters (CMB-013), its protector is the defending player of the creatures attacking it and the one who blocks them, and combat damage to it removes that many defense counters (CR 310.6, 310.9). (r47)
- **CMB-040** Assume blockers (I-D56 §4): when you attack while the defender is away, a screen above your hand opens: "Assume Bob's blockers" with "No blocks" and a hint that the game goes back if they block differently when they come back. The blocks may be edited first: beside each of your attackers (a small card) the defender's untapped creatures as small cards; tapping one makes it block that attacker (a violet ring) or not, holding one inspects it, and the screen counts them ("1 block"). OK assumes them (one tap with "No blocks"; Automatum's reason shows if it refuses them), Back leaves it a wait with "Assume blockers" in the step arrow's place (TRN-066). While they are online that Assume blockers opens the same screen. Nothing of it is shared; blocks with a cost to block are never assumed. (r49)

### Putting cards on the Stack

- **STK-001** Every way of putting a card on the Stack (from battlefield, hand, graveyard, exile or library) adds a Stack entry pointing at the real card; the real card stays where it is. Several entries may come from the same card. (r17)
- **STK-002** A hand card's entry is shown face up to both players while it is on the Stack. (r2)
- **STK-003** Stack entries are always upright and can't be tapped. A real card is never in the Stack itself; only entries pointing at it are (STK-001). (r35)
- **STK-006** Adding an entry shows a ghost card flying from the card to the Stack; removing one flies back. An entry leaving to the graveyard, battlefield or exile flies to that destination. Undo reverses the flight. (r14)

### Stack display

- **STK-007** Portrait: the Stack is a fixed-size HUD pinned at the right edge, vertically mid-screen. Landscape: it sits centred in the side area. (r17)
- **STK-008** The Stack area has a ~3dp very dark purple border and a soft shadow (floating look), a fixed 12dp inset, and its count on the top-left corner of the border. Only the top entry is shown, at single-card size. (r14)

### Stack list

- **STK-035** Automatum resolves the Stack: tapping it opens the Stack list (STK-018), and there are no Stack bubbles (no Put to battlefield, Put to graveyard, Put to exile, Remove from Stack or Mark text), text marks or buttons on the list's entries (TBL-119). (r45)
- **STK-018** Tapping or double-tapping the Stack (its card or count) opens "Stack · N" with every entry, top first ("Top", "2", …), each with art and name; it is only for looking. The list is only as tall as its entries (it scrolls when they don't fit). (r47)
- **STK-021** The Stack order can't be changed: there is no reordering. Holding an entry in the Stack list inspects it above the list. (r45)

### Casting

- **STK-022** Automatum puts an ability whose source isn't on the table (a delayed trigger of a spell copy, an emblem's ability, an ability of a token that's gone) on the Stack as an entry of its own: a card showing the source's name and the ability's text, like an entry whose card has left. (r49)
- **STK-023** Casting a hand card (a second tap on the raised card or its chosen way chip, a double-tap or a drag, ZON-079, ZON-080) opens the cast panel when the cast has a real choice: a white panel the full width of the table, docked exactly above your hand (over the Stack too) with a violet top edge: the card small as on the battlefield (a tap opens Inspect; holding it shows it large until released) beside its name (an ability's text for an ability, TBL-115), no rules text, then one section per choice in rules order: modes (STK-040), X (− value +) and, only for a cast begun from a card menu (TBL-115), how to cast it when Automatum offers several ways (faces, both halves of a fuse card as "Reel // Rend (fused)", which also names the spell, alternative costs, as chips) and optional costs ("Pay the Kicker cost?" No/Yes, then a switch); a hand card's way and optional costs are chosen with its cost chips before (ZON-080) and never asked; each changeable later, which keeps the choices after it that still fit. Targets are picked on the table: the panel shrinks to a slim bar docked just above your hand (STK-044; "Storm Spark — choose any target", with Done (None while nothing is picked) for several targets and Cancel, each with its icon) while every legal target glows violet, players by their life squares; a Graveyard or Exile pile holding a legal target glows too, and tapping it opens its card panel with its cards one by one (not grouped), the legal ones glowing, to pick from; the panel closes again once the targets are chosen. A tap on anything else shakes it, without a message (TBL-118). Then the cast goes out, paid by Autotap (STK-024); with Autotap off the panel returns with the mana section. When Automatum leaves nothing to choose but the payment (a land, a spell with no choices) it goes out at once without a panel. Changed choices show without a spinner. The cast goes out as one step (shown at once, STK-042): the card stays where it was with a Stack entry as in STK-001 (a hand card's no longer shows in the hand, STK-045), its sources tapped, and then the caster passes priority by itself (STK-026); a choice the cast causes afterwards (the order or targets of the triggers it sets off) is Automatum's open choice (STK-030). A spell or ability on the Stack that is a legal target glows as its Stack entry only, never as the spell's card in a hand or pile. When a Stack entry below the top is a legal target, the Stack glows too: tapping it opens the target picker in the target bar's place, docked above your hand like every Automatum panel (STK-044): "Choose a target · N" with the Stack's entries top first drawn as cards numbered from the top (holding one inspects it), the legal ones ringed in violet and the others dimmed, and the eye and Back (to picking on the table) in its bottom row; a tap on a legal entry picks it and closes it. (r44)
- **STK-040** Choosing modes (in the cast panel or an open choice, STK-030): when one is chosen a tap on a mode answers; otherwise taps choose and unchoose modes and OK sends the choice once the count fits. A mode that may be chosen more than once (a Season's pawprint modes) is added again by each tap and shows "×2" and so on, with a "−" icon beside it taking one back and Clear taking back all; each pawprint mode shows its cost ("{P}{P}") and "Pawprints: N of 5" shows the total, red and OK greyed while it is over the limit. Each mode chip has a mark in front, filled once chosen. (r42)
- **STK-024** With "Autotap mana sources" on (LOB-113, the default) a cast's payment is never asked: Automatum pays with its suggestion, mana already in your pool first, then the cheapest sources, and the cast panel has no mana selection. To pay differently, Undo the cast, tap the sources you want by hand (TBL-043: their mana goes to your pool) and cast again: the pool pays first and Autotap taps only the rest. A payment Autotap can't make (an X too large) is refused: the panel stays as it was and says so inside it in red ("Not enough mana to pay {5}{R}."), not over the table. With Autotap off the panel's mana section ("Mana · {4}{R}") shows each mana source Automatum may use as its card drawn as on the battlefield with the mana it makes in its corner (mana in the pool and life for a Phyrexian symbol as chips), those it suggests tapped (a card turned sideways; a chip with a sideways card mark and violet fill) and the rest untapped; tapping one taps or untaps it, and the suggestion follows the choices above it. The tapped chips count as a set: which colour each makes is worked out from all of them, so they pay whenever they can, whatever order they were tapped in. "Paid" or "Not paid yet" shows under it; Cast is enabled only once the tapping pays. The panel's bottom row has the eye at its left and Cancel and Cast (Activate, Play), each with its icon, at its right; the eye fades the rest of the panel away and brings it back while it stays where it is (holding it says what it does); holding cards to inspect them works throughout. While Automatum works out a step the panel takes no taps. (r43)
- **STK-042** A cast that goes out shows on your device at once: its Stack entry appears and its mana sources turn tapped (with their animations) while Automatum still works out what follows the cast (the passes, the spell resolving), with no spinner; then the cast's step takes the place of what was shown and goes to the server. Meanwhile other game actions wait as during an open cast (STK-025). If the cast can't go out after all (it can't be worked out, the table changed meanwhile, it can't be queued) or the server refuses it, the table goes back as it was, as with any refused action. (r42)
- **STK-025** An open cast is the caster's alone: nothing reaches the other player until Cast, and Cancel or Back drops it without a trace. While it is open other game actions are refused without a message: the card or pile they were made on shakes (the card being cast when there is none, TBL-118) and the step arrow is greyed (holding it says "Finish or cancel the cast first.", TRN-063); when the board changes under it (the other player acted, an Undo) it is worked out again on the new board, keeping the choices that still fit (your own mana tapping too, while its sources are still there), or dropped, its card shaking. While you can't act (reviewing the timeline, offline) the cast keeps to the live board and Cast sends nothing: "You can't cast right now (reviewing the timeline, or offline). The cast stays open." (r43)

### Automatum: priority and resolution

- **STK-026** Priority is passed by itself for a player who could only pass, judged on public information (no cards in hand and nothing else hidden they could use): by whichever device runs the step. Your own device also passes for you where your response mode doesn't stop (STK-036), folded into the event that reached it (HIS-092). The other player's other points are assumed (HIS-096) or waited for (TRN-066), never passed for them. When both players have passed in a row the top of the Stack resolves, and whatever follows happens by itself up to the next point where someone can act. (r48)
- **STK-036** Your response mode (TRN-059, private, I-D56 §2) says where your own device and your playback stop for you: Full control at every point where you get priority (after casting a spell or activating an ability you keep priority); Normal at your stops (your own main phases with an empty Stack) and pauses (the other player's spell or ability on top of the Stack, their attack, blocks, their end step); Auto-skip (the default for new games) as Normal, but your device passes where you can't respond and answers your questions that have only one answer. Everywhere else your device passes for you (STK-026). Choices and declarations of attackers and blockers always wait for their player. (r48)
- **STK-037** At your stop or pause the table is yours to act on, with no time limit and no Hold: the respond panel (STK-027) on the Stack, else the step arrow (TRN-063). (r48)
- **STK-027** The player holding priority with something on the Stack sees a panel docked above the hand (STK-044): the card on top of the Stack drawn as a card beside "Alice cast Storm Spark" ("You cast …", "… activated …", "… triggered"), an ability's own line, "Target:" with its targets, "N more below it on the Stack" when there are, the eye and Respond. Letting it resolve is the step arrow's "Resolve" (STK-029). Respond shrinks the panel to a bar above the hand while the player casts a response the usual way. There is no Hold: you decide with no time limit. (r48)
- **STK-044** Every Automatum panel sits in one place, like the cast panel (STK-023): the respond panel, open choices, divisions of combat damage, the Assume blockers screen (CMB-040), the decision-stop line (HIS-095), the result line (TRN-065), the target bars and the Stack target picker are the table's full width (in landscape at most 420 dp from its left edge) with their bottom on the top of your hand row, so your hand is never covered; white with a violet top edge. A panel's bottom row has the eye at its left and its buttons at its right; there is no Hold button. (r48)
- **STK-045** A hand card whose spell is on the Stack isn't drawn in its hand, nor counted in it, while the spell is there: it shows only as its Stack entry, for both players (it stays in the hand underneath, STK-001, and shows again if the spell goes back to the hand). (r43)
- **STK-028** The other player sees the wait (TRN-066) "Bob may respond to Storm Spark" over the battlefield with Assume resolves in the step arrow's place where it may be assumed, and no Pass for Bob; they have no step arrow then (TRN-015). (r50)
- **STK-029** While a spell or ability is on the Stack the step arrow reads "Resolve" (TRN-063): a tap passes your own priority (STK-027). (r48)
- **STK-030** A choice Automatum meets while resolving or putting triggers on the Stack ("you may …", choosing cards, a trigger's targets and modes, the order of triggers, which replacement applies first, the legend rule, a payment) is the open choice of the player it belongs to by the rules, who answers it in a panel docked above the hand (STK-044): the source's card beside the title saying what the choice is ("Scry 2 · Ember Divination"), "About:" with the cards it is about, a short grey instruction and the choice, every card drawn as a card. The other player sees the wait "<name> is choosing · <card>" (TRN-066) with no button: nobody chooses for them. (r48)
- **STK-043** A choice between two places is one screen of two labelled areas with a count ("Top · 2", "Bottom · 1"): scry (Top / Bottom), surveil (Top / Graveyard), separating cards into piles (Pile 1 / Pile 2, or Face-up / Face-down pile), discarding (Hand / Discard) and putting cards on the bottom of a library (Keep / Bottom). The cards start in the first area; a tap moves a card at once to the end of the other area (and back), and dragging a card drops it where it is let go, in either area. Where their order matters (both of scry's areas, surveil's Top) the cards carry numbers, 1 nearest the top. Each area has an "All to …" icon button. OK is enabled once the number of cards there fits (exactly one for "discard a card") and sends the choice and the orders in one step. Putting cards in order alone ("put them back in any order") is the same screen with one area: a tap moves a card to its end. (r42)
- **STK-041** Naming a card ("choose a card name", in the cast panel or an open choice) is a text field: any name may be written, the device's card records suggest names containing what was typed (tapping one fills the field), and OK names it. Choosing a creature type is a list of every creature type of the rules with a field that narrows it; a tap chooses one. Neither lists the game's own cards or their types, so nothing about the other player's hidden cards shows. (r42)
- **STK-032** While Automatum's choice is open, casting and every action that changes what Automatum works with are refused on both devices ("Automatum is waiting for a choice. Answer it first."); positions on the table still change, and looking, holding to inspect and Undo still work. (r46)
- **STK-033** A division of combat damage Automatum asks for (the attacker's first, then the defender's; only when it can go more than one way) is its open choice (STK-030), made in the division panel (CMB-033): rows per creature or player that may get damage, filled in with Automatum's suggestion (lethal damage to each in turn, the rest to the player with trample). OK deals the combat damage and Automatum goes on. A face-down creature the dividing player may not see shows as "A hidden card". (r43)
- **STK-034** Early answers (I-D33) are only your own and only at the newest event: a division of combat damage of yours that comes later in the same damage step may be given early in its rows (In order or Free, CMB-033). It is an event of its own that both tables show, kept in the game for that creature's division in that turn, and the device running the step applies it when the division opens if it still fits. (r49)
