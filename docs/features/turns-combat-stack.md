# Turns, combat and Stack

Turn and step tracking, whose move it is, the top and bottom game bars, life, game start and result suggestions (`TRN`), the combat area (`CMB`) and the Stack (`STK`).

### Turn structure

- **TRN-001** A turn has 12 positions: Untap, Upkeep, Draw, Main 1, Beginning of combat, Declare attackers, Declare blockers, Combat damage, End of combat, Main 2, End step, Cleanup. Automatum moves the turn through them (TRN-057) and adds a First-strike damage step when a combat needs one (TRN-056). (r37)
- **TRN-056** A combat in which a first or double striker fights has two combat damage steps (CR 510.4): First-strike damage (a bolt icon, short name "1st strike"), then Combat damage. The step button shows it like any step; the phase dialog shows its square between Blockers and Damage from when the combat reaches it until the combat ends. (r46)
- **TRN-057** The step arrow moves the turn on for you only: a tap passes your priority (or declares what the combat lanes hold); holding it is End turn, your device passing your priority until the turn ends, attacking with nothing. The other player's points are assumed (HIS-096), automatic or waited for, never passed for them. Each step's own actions happen by themselves; any other step change is refused: "The turn moves on through Automatum: use Next step or End turn." (r48)
- **TRN-060** When the game comes back to the same position with only passes nobody could avoid in between (CR 104.4b; holding only cards that can't be played then, such as lands while something is on the Stack, doesn't count as being able to act), the player holding priority is asked "The game is in a loop of actions nobody can stop, which makes it a draw (CR 104.4b). End it as a draw, or continue?" with Continue and Draw; Draw makes the game a draw (TRN-065). Abilities that keep triggering with nobody getting priority stop after 200 rounds with the same choice, "Possible loop: …". (r48)
- **TRN-002** Turn numbers count both players' turns, so turn 1 is only the starting player's first turn. Next step from Cleanup, or End turn, passes the turn to the other player's Untap. (r33)
- **TRN-052** A turn change Automatum makes names whose turn begins, so an extra turn goes to the same player again (turn number up by one, at Untap); the event that begins it reads "Turn N → <name>" ("Turn N → you" for that player, HIS-091). (r53)
- **TRN-003** Passing the turn clears every card's damage and until-end-of-turn P/T at once. (r46)
- **TRN-004** The step arrow shows only while you may act (your move, or a decision stop in playback) and is hidden while the other player must act, in either player's turn. (r48)

### Top bar

- **TRN-005** The top bar's first line reads "Your turn · N" or "<name>'s turn · N" in the turn player's colour; only the name shortens on a narrow screen. (r11)
- **TRN-006** Under it, the move pill: a solid green "Your move" when it is your move (TRN-058), otherwise an outlined "Waiting · <name>", also while catching up (HIS-094). Tapping it does nothing: the move isn't passed by hand. (r53)
- **TRN-007** The step button in the top bar is a square in the current phase's colour with the step's icon and its short name, as in the phase dialog (TRN-008), with no border colour or turn number. It flashes on a step change and opens the phase dialog. (r35)
- **TRN-008** The phase dialog is a real dialog titled with the step and its phase ("Combat · Declare attackers"), then "Your turn" / "<name>'s turn"; phase labels sit above rows of step squares, the current one outlined in the turn player's colour. Square names fit on one line on a phone: Untap, Upkeep, Draw · Main 1 · Begin, Attackers, Blockers, Damage, End · Main 2 · End, Cleanup ("1st strike" while a combat has it, TRN-056). (r37)
- **TRN-009** The phase dialog only shows where the turn is: its step squares do nothing and it has no buttons; the game moves on only with the step arrow (TRN-063). (r47)
- **TRN-010** The top bar's Table actions button opens a dialog with Timeline, Suggest game end (or Accept result while the opponent's suggestion waits), Concede (TRN-064) and Send problem report (Settings' "Report a problem" with its optional description, sent without leaving the game), each an icon and its name. Nothing in it changes the board by hand (TBL-119). (r50)

### Whose turn and whose move

- **TRN-012** Whose TURN shows on the battlefields: only the turn player's battlefield is tinted (your own green-ish, the opponent's red-ish, the land band a little darker); the other battlefield is neutral grey. (r11)
- **TRN-013** Whose MOVE shows on the bars: the top and bottom bars take the light colour of the player whose move it is (TRN-058), never part of an event. In timeline mode both bars are grey. (r50)
- **TRN-058** The move is the player the game really waits for (the record "Your move" notifications use, LOB-073). While one player plays on assumptions about the other, the acting player has it, even while the other's playback stops at an assumption they may answer. An open undo request (HIS-100) or result suggestion (TRN-047) is the answerer's move; a result is the move of whoever hasn't seen it. When nobody is waited for, the game list says "Move not available". (r50)

### Bottom bar and quick actions

- **TRN-015** The bottom bar shows your toggles (TRN-059) on either player's turn and the step arrow (TRN-063) only while you may act; while the other player must act its place stays empty, in your own turn too. During your opening your quick actions are Mulligan and Keep (TRN-062). (r48)
- **TRN-016** Quick actions are equal-width buttons in the popup style (icon plus small black label, 48dp tall) sharing the width left of the main button (TRN-017), which is twice as wide as one, with press feedback. A greyed quick action stays in place. (r53)
- **TRN-017** The step arrow, the main button, sits in the bottom-right corner, arrow-shaped, twice as wide as a quick action (TRN-016), with an icon above the words of what a tap does (TRN-063). Tap = what it says; hold = End turn, with a small haptic tick (holding Resolve or a wait's Assume only says what it does). (r53)
- **TRN-063** The step arrow (the main button) holds whatever goes on now. It is white with a grey outline and says under an icon what a tap does, with room for two lines (wrapped between words; drawn smaller rather than cut where two lines don't fit): "To combat", "End turn" (not stopping at your own main phases, TRN-057), "Next", "Pass", "Attack (2)" / "No attack", "Block (1)" / "No blocks", "Resolve" with something on the Stack (STK-029), and "OK" while your division of combat damage is open (CMB-033); while the other player must act it holds the wait's Assume (TRN-070). Nothing sits under the Stack. It is greyed when it can't act (your cast or another choice open, the opening, a declaration Automatum doesn't allow, a division not yet complete): a tap shakes it and holding it says why. (r54)
- **TRN-023** Each quick action is one Undo step, however many changes it makes. (r15)
- **TRN-059** The quick-actions row holds your own settings for this game, on either player's turn. In live play: the response mode (a tap goes on to the next of Full control, Normal and Auto-skip; its name shows), "Always assume resolves" and "Autotap mana". In catch-up playback (HIS-094): "Full control" (on: playback stops wherever you get priority; off, the default: where Auto-skip would, HIS-095) and "Autotap mana". Equal-width cells with an icon and words, on filled violet with a check, off white with an empty ring; holding one says what it does. Only you see or change them. Other quick actions: your opening's (TRN-062) and combat staging's (CMB-048, CMB-034). (r53)

### Game start and mulligans

- **TRN-027** A game begins with both players "Mulliganing" at the same time (TRN-062); keeping makes you Ready. Declarations made at nearly the same moment both stand (the later one is made again on top of the other). The starting player plays turn 1 only after the other player's own keep: their keep is never assumed, whether they are away or online (HIS-096). While both openings are open, each player's own mulligan is their move (LOB-022, LOB-072). (r53)
- **TRN-028** The starting player's turn-1 controls wait until the other player kept (TRN-027). (r53)
- **TRN-029** There is no Turn 0: both players mulligan during turn 1, while their opening status is set. (r25)
- **TRN-062** The opening follows the game's rules (opening hand 7, the first mulligan free, London mulligans, CR 103.5), both players declaring at once. While you are "Mulliganing" your quick actions are "Mulligan" (your hand goes back, your library is shuffled and seven are drawn) and "Keep" (with cards owed to the bottom, the Keep / Bottom screen opens first, STK-043); nothing else asks about it or covers the board. (r51)

### Life

- **TRN-030** Each player starts at 20 life. Tapping a life square opens a floating Life panel with that player's life (only the rules change it) and, for the other player, their presence line (TRN-043); its life row sits on the panel edge facing the square (in landscape the panel opens beside it). (r45)
- **TRN-034** Portrait: each player's row shows the life square and packed piles; the room beside them stays free. (r45)
- **TRN-035** Landscape: each player's life square and piles sit in the sidebar, with a free row under them (above them on the opponent's side). (r45)

### Emblems

- **TRN-053** A player's emblems show as violet chips, "Emblem: " and the emblem's text, cut after two lines. In portrait they stack, oldest first, in the free room right of the player's piles, clear of the hands; in landscape (or when that room is too narrow) by the life square: under the opponent's, above your own, the oldest nearest. They are drawn only, so taps reach the table beneath. (r47)
- **TRN-069** A player's dungeon (LOB-117) shows as a small card in a fourth place after Library, Graveyard and Exile (in landscape under the Library, in the free row), and the room its marker is on as a violet chip "Room: <room>", first among the emblem chips. Tapping it does nothing, holding inspects it, and it can't be dragged. Dungeons to choose from show as cards; a room's ability on the Stack shows its dungeon. (r50)

### Opponent presence

- **TRN-042** The opponent's life square has a small dot in its corner: green when they have this game open and touched it in the last 2 minutes, yellow when open but idle, grey when away. It is hidden while you are disconnected. (r29)
- **TRN-043** The opponent's Life panel has an info line with the same dot: "Online", "Idle 7 min", "Last seen 12 min ago", "Last seen 3 h ago", "Last seen Oct 27" or "Not seen yet". (r29)

### Waiting, assumptions and results

- **TRN-046** Suggest game end opens a chooser My win / Draw game, then a confirm dialog ("Suggest my win?" or "Suggest draw?") with an optional one-line "Reason", at most 200 characters (placeholder "Infinite combo"); both players see the reason in quotes in the suggestion panel (TRN-047). (r45)
- **TRN-047** A waiting suggestion shows as a panel on the table, not a dialog: "<name> proposed their win." (or loss, or "a draw"; "You proposed your win." for the author). The opponent gets Decline and Accept (an agreed result), the author Withdraw; made at the newest event. (r50)
- **TRN-064** Concede (a flag icon) in Table actions asks "Concede?" ("You lose this game at once.", Cancel / Concede in red); Concede ends the game at once as your loss (CR 104.3a), also while a suggestion waits. If the other player's latest actions haven't reached you yet, the game first branches at your local head, so the result never depends on what you haven't seen. (r48)
- **TRN-065** When the rules end a game (a loss by the rules, a draw, the loop draw of TRN-060), each player sees the result once it is shown to them, in a panel above the hand: "You won", "You lost" or "Draw" with OK, or "… · waiting for <name> to catch up" with OK greyed. Once both have seen it either may press OK: the result is final and recorded (LOB-046) and the other player notified (LOB-111). Until then it can't be declined, but Undo (HIS-084) still takes the step back. (r49)
- **TRN-066** The wait: while the other player must act (online or away), faint words over the battlefield (never taking a touch or covering the board) say why: "<name> may respond to <spell>" (an ability as "<source>'s ability", STK-046), "<name> may act · <step>", "<name> is declaring blockers", "<name> is deciding on a mulligan", "<name> is choosing · <card>" (the card only when you can see it), "Waiting for <name>". (r53)
- **TRN-070** Where the other player's answer may be assumed (HIS-096), the main button holds "Assume resolves" (something on the Stack, STK-028), "Assume no response" (nothing on the Stack, as at the other player's end step), or "Assume no blockers" / "Assume blockers" (CMB-040) while they must act, whether they are online or away, with no timeout; holding it says what it does. There is no Assume keep: the other player's opening is always waited for (TRN-027). (r54)
- **TRN-067** Information rule: while your line rests on an unconfirmed assumption, nothing you do or assume may show you a card you couldn't see (the other player's hand or library, face-down cards, random discards; never your own library) or a random result (coin, die, random choice). Such an action is refused before it shows: the card shakes and holding it says "Wait for the other player to catch up: this would show you hidden cards or a random result." The server checks the same for cards. (r50)
- **TRN-071** Under the information rule (TRN-067), your phone's own passes after an action of yours stop before one that would show you something (you keep priority there), and such an assumption isn't made: the wait reads "Waiting for <name> · going on would show you hidden cards or a random result" (TRN-066). (r50)
- **TRN-068** The unconfirmed mark: while actions of yours rest on answers the other player hasn't confirmed yet, a small violet dot (no number) sits at the step arrow's corner; a tap or hold says "<name> hasn't seen your last moves yet; if <name> answers differently, they'll be taken back". Only you see it. (r50)
- **TRN-048** Decline and Withdraw are their own events, not undos. (r18)
- **TRN-049** While a suggestion waits, every other game action is blocked for a player once the board shown to them includes it; chat, timeline browsing and holding to inspect still work. (r48)
- **TRN-050** A suggestion left behind on a discarded line (an earlier real answer of the other player's branched, HIS-097) no longer locks anything. (r48)
- **TRN-051** The suggestion panel appears in playback once its event is reached; its buttons work only at the live end, with playback paused. (r18)

### Combat area

- **CMB-001** Combat uses a temporary combat area: a band between the two battlefields (CMB-007). Automatum opens it when attackers are staged and closes it itself (SYS-069). While you declare attackers with none staged, the band is a thin red-orange strip that pulses; tapping it opens the band with its empty lanes (your own view only: nothing is staged or shared), and so does staging the first attacker. (r53)
- **CMB-002** Attackers and blockers are combat copies of real cards: the real card stays where it is, and anything done to a copy's card applies to the real card. A card has at most one copy per combat area. (r1)
- **CMB-003** Combat copies are always upright and can't be tapped. A first tap selects one (CMB-032); tapping the selected copy opens its one-page Combat menu, Remove from combat (TBL-116), with no "Attack →" (CMB-048). Double-tap does nothing; hold inspects. (r52)
- **CMB-004** Remove from combat takes the copy out of the declaration Automatum waits for (CMB-028); the real card is unchanged. (r45)
- **CMB-006** When the real card leaves the battlefield, all its combat copies disappear; surviving blockers of a removed attacker stay in place. Closing the area removes all copies but leaves real cards and their state unchanged. (r1)
- **CMB-007** Rows for the attacking player: target headers on top, then blockers, then (after a gap for the block arrows, CMB-031) attackers; the defender sees it mirrored. The open combat area is a band between the battlefields that pushes them apart (the band keeps each battlefield's colour), with its lanes on a white rounded panel sized to its content in the band's middle; the band is part of the table's surface and pans and zooms with the battlefields (TBL-001). Nothing of it floats over the battlefields. Only the panel takes combat drops. (r53)
- **CMB-042** Combat cards are the battlefield cards' size at the same zoom, but never narrower than 44dp at zoom 1 (whatever the card size), so names and badges stay readable; they zoom with the band (CMB-007), so zooming out (or landscape's opening view, TBL-121) shrinks them with the battlefields. (r53)
- **CMB-008** Pairs are packed groups without dividers between them (only lane separators), centred as a group and re-centred when added; cards sit centred in their slot (one attacker over two blockers is centred), with about two card-border widths of margin. (r40)
- **CMB-009** Dragging a battlefield card into the combat area makes its copy there: dropped left or right of all groups it makes a new pair at that end, near a divider a new pair there (a faint shorter divider shows where while dragging); reordering works the same both ways. Dragging a copy between rows changes its role, except a defender's blocker dropped in an attacker row, which blocks that attacker (CMB-031). (r40)
- **CMB-027** A card dragged into the combat area (from the battlefield or another combat slot) that covers at least half the width of a card in the row it is dropped in goes beside that card, on the nearer side: joining its pair, or swapping places within its own pair; with less overlap CMB-009's gaps apply. This wins over divider drops and reordering, except a defender's card dropped in an attacker row, which blocks that pair's attacker (CMB-031). (r40)

### Attack targets

- **CMB-012** While combat is open, every attackable target always has its own lane in the same attacker/blocker rows: the defending player first, then each face-up planeswalker and battle in battlefield order, including your own Sieges (protected by the opponent). An empty lane is one card slot wide. (r27)
- **CMB-013** Lane headers sit in their own row, never covered: a pill tinted in the target side's colour with the defending player's round avatar and name, or a card's tiny art and name ("→ Face-down card" for a hidden target), and at its right end the target's life, loyalty or defense counters (none for a hidden target). Names show in full at the area's card title size; lanes widen to fit, with room for the damage preview's "16 → 11" (CMB-029); a too-wide area pans with the table. (r40)
- **CMB-046** A planeswalker or battle that left the battlefield keeps its lane as "→ <name> (gone)" without a value: its attackers stay attacking but attack nothing and deal no combat damage (CR 506.4c). (r40)
- **CMB-014** Lane separators are 2dp, fairly dark, scaling with card size, run through the header row, and keep about 2dp of space around cards. (r40)
- **CMB-015** Dropping an attacker into another lane, also an empty one, retargets its whole pair (blockers stay attached); dropping into the blocker row makes it a blocker. (r31)

### Blocks and arrows

- **CMB-031** Every block is an arrow from the blocker to the attacker it blocks, drawn in the gap between the blocker and attacker rows. A blocker sits in the pair of the first attacker it blocks; one blocking several attackers is one card with an arrow to each (no second copy), and an attacker blocked only by an arrow counts as blocked. (r49)
- **CMB-032** Tapping a combat copy selects it: a violet frame, with the copies it fights framed in amber and its arrows amber. Tapping it again opens its Combat menu (CMB-003); tapping the empty table clears the selection. The selection is the viewer's own and lasts only for the combat step it was made in. While blockers are declared, tapping a defender's creature picks it as the blocker to place instead (CMB-034). (r40)

### Declaring attackers and blockers

- **CMB-028** While attackers are awaited, one target lane is active, framed in violet (first the defending player's; tapping a lane's header makes it active). Tapping one of your creatures stages it as an attacker of the active target (its copy appears in that lane), tapping it again unstages it; one that can't attack that target isn't staged and Automatum's reason shows. Dragging works the same, also to retarget (CMB-015). While attackers are awaited, your creatures that may attack glow red-orange from behind (the cyan glow stays "can activate", TBL-117). (r53)
- **CMB-048** While attackers are declared the quick actions are "Rest → <target>" and "Clear", and the step arrow reads "Attack (N)" ("No attack" with nothing staged) and declares what the lanes hold as one step. Only the lanes pick targets: no card menu has "Attack →". (r52)
- **CMB-047** While attackers are awaited nobody holds priority: a tap on your creature only stages or unstages it, never activating anything (TBL-122), and card menus show their ability rows greyed ("Nobody holds priority now."); a mana ability an attack cost needs is paid as the step arrow declares (CMB-044). To act before attacks, Full control stops at the beginning of combat (STK-036). (r52)
- **CMB-043** Combat staging is shared as it is made: each change is an event of its own that the other player's table shows (as they play back), and the declaration ends it in its own event. Behind the newest event (a decision stop in playback) the staging stays on its player's device and goes out only as the declaration. (r52)
- **CMB-034** While blockers are awaited, tapping one of your creatures selects it as the blocker to place (CMB-032) and the attackers it may block glow violet; tapping an attacker then blocks it (an arrow), tapping one it already blocks removes that block; dragging does the same. The staging is shared (CMB-043). The quick action is "Clear"; the step arrow reads "Block (N)" or "No blocks" and declares. (r49)
- **CMB-029** In the declare blockers step (also while a combat damage division is open, and in the first-strike damage step) both players see on the combat area what combat damage would do if nobody does anything more, with the blocks being arranged and the suggested divisions: a red "−3" badge over each copy that would take damage, "☠" beside it when it would die. (r48)
- **CMB-033** The damage division panel (STK-033) opens attached to the combat band (under it, or above it when there is no room below; never above the hand) and shows the dividing player one division at a time ("1 of 2" when the step has more): the title "Divide N damage" with the creature dealing it at the panel's right (STK-044), "N damage to divide among what it fights" (" · trample" with trample), an "In order" / "Free" switch, a row per creature (its card drawn small, the damage it gets, "lethal ☠"), a hint line and the eye; it opens filled in with the suggestion, and the main button's OK deals the damage (TRN-063). An early division (STK-034) opens attached to the band the same way. (r53)
- **CMB-035** A real Battlefield card in combat carries a chip over its top edge: violet "→ <target>" while staged, grey "attacking" or "blocking" otherwise (only on cards wide enough). Attackers' copies show violet badges for the combat keywords Automatum works out (flying, reach, menace, trample, first strike, double strike, deathtouch, lifelink, vigilance) on their side away from the arrows. Chips and badges stack one per line, never wider than the card (text shrinks to fit). (r40)
- **CMB-036** A staged attacker with enlist has an "Enlist" chip (a person-plus icon) under its copy: tapping it makes the creatures that may be enlisted (untapped, not staged, without summoning sickness) glow violet, and tapping one enlists it (one per enlist ability). The chip then reads "+N" (their power), filled violet, the enlisted card gets a violet "enlisted" chip, and tapping the chip again takes it back; any other tap stops picking. Attacking taps the enlisted creature and adds its power until end of turn (CR 702.154a). Holding the chip says what enlist does. (r47)
- **CMB-037** A staged attacker's optional costs to attack ("You may exert this creature as it attacks", CR 508.1g) are toggle chips under its copy ("Exert" with an empty or checked box, filled violet when on); holding one shows the ability's text. (r47)
- **CMB-044** A cost the rules make a creature pay to attack or block (CR 508.1h, 509.1d) shows as an amber chip under its copy ("{2}", "2 life", "Sacrifice"); holding it shows the ability. All attack and block costs (CMB-037 too) are paid as the step arrow declares: mana by Autotap when it's on, otherwise (and for any other payment choice) through Automatum's choice (STK-030); an unpayable cost greys the step arrow (TRN-063). The other player sees the chips but can't change them. (r47)
- **CMB-038** A staged attacker with banding has a "Band" chip (a link icon): tap it, then tap other staged attackers to put them in its band or take them out (instead of unstaging them), until the chip or the empty table is tapped again. A band of two or more is joined by a violet bar, each copy with a filled "Band" chip, and attacks as a band (CR 702.22c); the step arrow greys with Automatum's reason when it isn't allowed. A blocker drafted onto one member blocks the whole band once declared. (r49)
- **CMB-045** Attack choices (CMB-036–CMB-038) are dropped when their attacker is unstaged or on Clear, last only for the declare attackers step they were made in, and travel with the shared staging (CMB-043), so the defender sees them. (r49)
- **CMB-039** A battle is attacked through its lane (CMB-012) as Automatum allows: its header shows its defense counters (CMB-013), its protector is the defending player of the creatures attacking it and the one who blocks them, and combat damage to it removes that many defense counters (CR 310.6, 310.9). (r47)
- **CMB-040** Assume blockers: when you attack, the defender's blocks are a wait (TRN-070), whether they are away or online, and meanwhile your table is at once in assume mode on the combat band's lanes: no panel and no tap to enter it. You place the defender's creatures as blockers by tapping or dragging, exactly as the defender would (CMB-034), with "Clear" as the quick action. The main button reads "Assume no blockers" while none is placed and "Assume blockers" once one is; only pressing it assumes the blocks shown (Automatum's reason shows if it refuses them); nothing is ever assumed by itself. Nothing of it is shared; blocks with a cost to block are never assumed. (r53)
- **CMB-049** The blocks placed to assume (CMB-040) show on the lanes as the defender's would, with their arrows and the damage preview (CMB-029); holding a card inspects it. (r53)

### Putting cards on the Stack

- **STK-001** Every way of putting a card on the Stack (from battlefield, hand, graveyard, exile or library) adds a Stack entry pointing at the real card; the real card stays where it is. Several entries may come from the same card. (r17)
- **STK-002** A hand card's entry is shown face up to both players while it is on the Stack. (r2)
- **STK-003** Stack entries are always upright and can't be tapped. (r35)
- **STK-006** Adding an entry shows a ghost card flying from the card to the Stack; removing one flies back, or to the graveyard, battlefield or exile it leaves to. A hand card's spell (STK-045) flies from the hand to the Stack and then from its entry to its place, drawn once all the way. Undo reverses the flight. (r51)

### Stack display

- **STK-007** Portrait: the Stack is a fixed-size HUD pinned at the right edge, vertically mid-screen. Landscape: it sits centred in the side area. (r17)
- **STK-008** The Stack area has a ~3dp very dark purple border and a soft shadow (floating look), a fixed 12dp inset, and its count on the top-left corner of the border. Only the top entry is shown, at single-card size. (r14)
- **STK-046** An ability on the Stack never looks like a card: it is an ability token, smaller than a card, dark with the controller's outline, the source's art filling it (its type glyph while the art is missing) and a small violet chip at its bottom: "T" for a triggered ability, "A" for an activated one; no name or text (Inspect shows them, STK-050). The same token stands for it in the Stack list, the target picker (STK-048) and at a panel's right (STK-044). Spells, spell copies and face-down entries stay drawn as cards. (r54)
- **STK-050** Holding an ability on the Stack inspects it (TBL-104): first a dark rounded panel with the controller's outline, as wide as an inspected card and as tall as its content (not card-shaped): the source's art, then under it the kind chip and "Triggered ability" (or "Activated ability"), the source's name, the ability's whole text and what was chosen for it ("Mode: …", "Targets: Bob, Meadow Sentinel"; a card the viewer can't see reads "a hidden card"); then the source card's picture(s). (r54)

### Stack list

- **STK-035** Automatum resolves the Stack: tapping it opens the Stack list (STK-018), and there are no Stack bubbles, text marks or buttons on the list's entries (TBL-119). (r45)
- **STK-018** Tapping or double-tapping the Stack (its card or count) opens "Stack · N" with every entry, top first ("Top", "2", …), each with art and name (an ability as its token, "<source>'s ability" and its text); it is only for looking, and only as tall as its entries (it scrolls when they don't fit). (r47)
- **STK-021** The Stack order can't be changed: there is no reordering. Holding an entry in the Stack list inspects it above the list. (r45)

### Casting

- **STK-022** Automatum puts an ability whose source isn't on the table (a delayed trigger of a spell copy, an emblem's ability, an ability of a token that's gone) on the Stack as an entry of its own, drawn as an ability token (STK-046). (r52)
- **STK-023** Casting (ZON-079, ZON-080, TBL-126) opens the cast panel only for a real choice; with only the payment to choose, the cast goes out at once. The panel is white, the table's full width, docked above your hand (over the Stack too) with a violet top edge: a title saying only what is done ("Cast a spell"; "Activate an ability" with the ability's text under it; "Play a land") and the card at the panel's right (an ability as its token, STK-044), no rules text, then a section per choice in rules order: modes (STK-040), X (− value +: − and + stop at its least and at the most your mana pays now, with no OK: Cast answers it and goes on, STK-052), the way to cast (STK-051). Choices stay changeable, keeping later ones that still fit, without a spinner. (r54)
- **STK-051** In a cast begun from a card menu, when Automatum offers several ways to cast, the panel shows them as chips (faces, both halves of a fuse card as "Reel // Rend (fused)", which also names the spell, alternative costs), and optional costs ask "Pay the Kicker cost?" No/Yes, then show a switch. A hand card's way and optional costs are chosen with its chips before (ZON-080, ZON-082) and never asked. (r44)
- **STK-047** Targets are picked on the table: the cast panel becomes a slim bar above your hand ("Choose any target"; Done, or None while nothing is picked, for several targets; no Cancel, Undo and Back drop the cast, STK-025; the card at the bar's right, STK-044) while every legal target glows violet (players by their life squares, spells and abilities only as Stack entries). A Graveyard or Exile pile holding a target glows; tapping it opens its cards one by one, the legal ones glowing, until the targets are chosen. Other taps shake (TBL-118). (r54)
- **STK-048** When a Stack entry below the top is a legal target, the Stack glows; tapping it opens the target picker in the target bar's place: "Choose a target · N" with what it is chosen for at the panel's right (STK-044), the entries top first as cards (abilities as tokens), numbered from the top, the legal ones ringed violet and the rest dimmed, and the eye and Back (to the table) in its bottom row. Holding an entry inspects it; tapping a legal one picks it. (r53)
- **STK-040** Choosing modes: with one to choose a tap on a mode answers; otherwise taps choose and unchoose and OK sends once the count fits (in the cast panel Cast does, with no OK, STK-052). A mode that may be chosen again (a Season's pawprint modes) is added per tap ("×2"), with "−" taking one back and Clear taking all; pawprint modes show their cost ("{P}{P}") and "Pawprints: N of 5" the total, red with OK greyed while over the limit. Each mode chip's mark in front fills once chosen. (r54)
- **STK-024** With "Autotap mana sources" on (LOB-113, the default) payment is never asked: Automatum pays with its suggestion, pool mana first, then the cheapest sources, and the cast panel has no mana section. To pay differently, Undo the cast, tap the sources you want (their mana goes to your pool, TBL-122) and cast again. A payment Autotap can't make (an X too large) is refused in red inside the panel ("Not enough mana to pay {5}{R}."), which stays as it was. (r43)
- **STK-049** With Autotap off the cast panel has a mana section ("Mana · {4}{R}"): each source Automatum may use as its card with the mana it makes in its corner (pool mana and life for a Phyrexian symbol as chips), the suggested ones tapped; a tap taps or untaps one, and the suggestion follows the choices above it. The tapped sources pay as a set, whatever order they were tapped in. "Paid" or "Not paid yet" shows under it; Cast is enabled once they pay. (r43)
- **STK-052** The cast panel's bottom row has the eye at its left (STK-044) and Cast (Activate, Play) with its icon at its right; there is no Cancel (Undo and Back drop the cast, STK-025). A choice built up in the panel (X, several modes, a division, cards to pick) has no OK of its own: Cast answers it and goes on (to the targets, or out), and its hint says "then Cast"; Cast is greyed only while that choice isn't complete or the cast can't be paid. Holding cards to inspect them works throughout; while Automatum works out a step the panel takes no taps. (r54)
- **STK-042** A cast shows on your device at once: its Stack entry appears and its sources tap (animated) while Automatum works out what follows (the passes, the spell resolving), with no spinner; then that outcome replaces it and goes to the server. Other game actions wait meanwhile (STK-025). If the cast can't go out after all or the server refuses it, the table goes back as with any refused action. (r42)
- **STK-025** An open cast is the caster's alone: nothing reaches the other player until Cast; Back or Undo drops it without a trace (there is no Cancel) (Undo's point is the cast's start: you are just before you began it, where Undo would take you after it was made, HIS-084). Meanwhile other game actions are refused without a message: what they were made on shakes (TBL-118) and the step arrow is greyed ("Finish the cast first, or drop it with Undo."). While you can't act (timeline, offline) Cast sends nothing: "You can't cast right now (reviewing the timeline, or offline). The cast stays open." (r54)
- **STK-055** When the board changes under an open cast (the other player acted, an Undo), the cast is worked out again on the new board, keeping the choices that still fit (your mana tapping too, while its sources are there), or dropped, its card shaking. (r43)

### Priority and resolution

- **STK-026** Priority is passed by itself for a player who could only pass, judged on public information (no cards in hand, nothing else hidden they could use), by whichever device runs the step; never in their own main phases with an empty Stack. Your device also passes for you where your response mode doesn't stop (STK-036), and a caster passes after a cast. When both players pass in a row the top of the Stack resolves, and play goes on by itself to the next point where someone can act. (r48)
- **STK-036** Your response mode (TRN-059) sets where your device stops for you in live play (catch-up playback stops by HIS-095): Full control at every point where you get priority (after your own cast or activation you keep it; in your own turn also at the beginning of combat, CMB-047); Normal at your stops (your own main phases with an empty Stack) and pauses (the other player's spell or ability on top of the Stack, their attack, blocks, their end step); Auto-skip (the default) as Normal, but passing at pauses where you can't respond and answering one-answer questions (SYS-063). (r53)
- **STK-053** In every response mode your own main phases are never passed for you (casting or playing a land there leaves you there with priority), and choices and declarations of attackers and blockers always wait for their player. (r52)
- **STK-037** At your stop or pause the table is yours to act on, with no time limit and no Hold: the main button (TRN-063), reading Resolve with something on the Stack (STK-029). (r53)
- **STK-027** The player holding priority with something on the Stack sees its top entry on the Stack (an ability as its token) and Resolve on the main button (STK-029); no panel covers the board or says who cast it. Responding is casting or activating the usual way, with no time limit. (r53)
- **STK-044** Every Automatum panel (open choices, the result line, target bars, the Stack target picker) sits where the cast panel does (a damage division is attached to the combat band instead, CMB-033): the table's full width (in landscape at most 420 dp from its left edge), white with a violet top edge, its bottom on the top of your hand row so your hand is never covered. Each has the eye, at the left of its bottom row or slim bar, which hides it to show the board (only the eye stays) and brings it back. No Hold button. Titles say only the choice ("Scry 2", "Choose any target"); what the choice comes from is never named in text but sits at the panel's or bar's right, in the same place in each, as a small card: a spell or permanent as its card, an ability as its Stack ability token (STK-046); holding it inspects it (TBL-104). A panel with no source (a discard to hand size, the legend rule, the result line) has none. (r53)
- **STK-045** A hand card whose spell is on the Stack isn't drawn in its hand, nor counted in it, while the spell is there: it shows only as its Stack entry, for both players (it stays in the hand underneath, STK-001, and shows again if the spell goes back to the hand). (r43)
- **STK-028** The other player sees the wait "Bob may respond to Storm Spark" (TRN-066) with "Assume resolves" on the main button where it may be assumed (a tap assumes Bob lets it resolve), and no Pass for Bob (TRN-015). (r54)
- **STK-029** While a spell or ability is on the Stack and you hold priority, the main button (TRN-063) reads "Resolve" (a play icon above the word): a tap passes your own priority; holding it says what it does. Nothing sits under the Stack. While your cast or a choice is open it is greyed, as the main button is then (TRN-063). (r53)
- **STK-030** A choice Automatum meets while resolving or putting triggers on the Stack ("you may …", choosing cards, a trigger's targets and modes, the order of triggers, which replacement applies first, the legend rule, a payment) belongs to the player the rules name, who answers it in a panel above the hand (STK-044): a title saying only the choice ("Scry 2"), the source at the panel's right (STK-044), "About:" with the cards it concerns, a short grey instruction and the choice, cards drawn as cards. Nobody chooses for them (TRN-066). (r53)
- **STK-043** A choice between two places is one screen of two labelled areas with a count ("Top · 2", "Bottom · 1"): scry (Top / Bottom), surveil (Top / Graveyard), separating piles (Pile 1 / Pile 2, or Face-up / Face-down pile), discarding (Hand / Discard), bottoming (Keep / Bottom). Cards start in the first area; a tap moves a card to the end of the other area and back, a drag drops it where released. (r42)
- **STK-054** In a two-area choice (STK-043) cards whose order matters (scry's areas, surveil's Top) are numbered, 1 nearest the top; each area has an "All to …" icon button; OK is enabled once the count fits (exactly one for "discard a card") and sends choice and order in one step. Ordering alone ("put them back in any order") is the same screen with one area. (r42)
- **STK-041** Naming a card ("choose a card name") is a text field: any name may be written, the device's card records suggest names containing what was typed (a tap fills the field), and OK names it. Choosing a creature type is a list of every creature type of the rules with a field narrowing it; a tap chooses one. Neither lists the game's own cards or their types, so nothing about the other player's hidden cards shows. (r42)
- **STK-032** While Automatum's choice is open, casting and every action that changes what Automatum works with are refused on both devices: what it was made on shakes (TBL-118), on the other player's device their "is choosing" text flashes (HIS-075), and holding a greyed menu row or the step arrow says "Automatum is waiting for a choice. Answer it first." Positions on the table still change, and looking, inspecting and Undo still work. (r52)
- **STK-033** A combat damage division (the attacker's first, then the defender's, CR 510.1) is the dividing player's open choice (STK-030) only when it can go more than one way; one whose in-order result can't depend on the order is made with the suggestion without asking. The suggestion is lethal damage to each in turn, the rest to the player with trample; The main button's OK deals the damage (CMB-033). A face-down creature the divider can't see shows as "A hidden card". Nobody divides for another player. (r53)
- **STK-034** Early answers are only your own and only at the newest event: a combat damage division of yours later in the same damage step may be given early in its rows (In order or Free, CMB-033). It is an event of its own that both tables show, kept for that creature's division that turn, and applied when the division opens if it still fits. (r49)
