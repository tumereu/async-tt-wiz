# Turns, combat and Stack

Turn and step tracking, whose move it is, the top and bottom game bars, life, game start and result suggestions (`TRN`), the combat area (`CMB`) and the Stack (`STK`).

### Turn structure

- **TRN-001** A turn has 12 positions: Untap, Upkeep, Draw, Main 1, Beginning of combat, Declare attackers, Declare blockers, Combat damage, End of combat, Main 2, End step, Cleanup. Automatum moves the turn through them (TRN-057) and adds a First-strike damage step when a combat needs one (TRN-056). (r37)
- **TRN-056** A combat in which a first or double striker fights has two combat damage steps (CR 510.4): First-strike damage (a bolt icon, short name "1st strike"), then Combat damage. The step button shows it like any step; the phase dialog shows its square between Blockers and Damage from when the combat reaches it until the combat ends. (r46)
- **TRN-057** The main button moves the turn on for you only: a tap passes your priority (or declares what the combat lanes hold); holding it is End turn, your device passing your priority until the turn ends, attacking with nothing. The other player's points are assumed (HIS-096), automatic or waited for, never passed for them. Each step's own actions happen by themselves; nothing else changes the step. (r60)
- **TRN-060** When the game comes back to the same position with only passes nobody could avoid in between (CR 104.4b; holding only cards that can't be played then, such as lands while something is on the Stack, doesn't count as being able to act), the player holding priority is asked "The game is in a loop of actions nobody can stop, which makes it a draw (CR 104.4b). End it as a draw, or continue?" with Continue and Draw; Draw makes the game a draw (TRN-065). Abilities that keep triggering with nobody getting priority stop after 200 rounds with the same choice, "Possible loop: …". (r48)
- **TRN-002** Turn numbers count both players' turns, so turn 1 is only the starting player's first turn. Next step from Cleanup, or End turn, passes the turn to the other player's Untap. (r33)
- **TRN-052** A turn change Automatum makes names whose turn begins, so an extra turn goes to the same player again (turn number up by one, at Untap); the event that begins it reads "Turn N → <name>" ("Turn N → you" for that player, HIS-091). (r53)
- **TRN-004** While the other player must act, the main button (TRN-063) holds: nothing where nothing of yours follows before their next real action (their own main phase with an empty Stack, their declaration of attackers, their choices and their mulligan); "Assume resolves" (something on the Stack) or "Assume no response" (an empty Stack) where they hold priority anywhere else and your phone doesn't assume their pass by itself (HIS-096, TRN-070); "Assume no blockers" / "Assume blockers" while they declare blockers (CMB-040; nothing where their blocks can't be assumed); a greyed Assume where assuming would show you hidden cards or a random result (TRN-071); and "Cancel request" while your own undo request is open (HIS-100). (r60)

### Top bar

- **TRN-005** The top bar's first line reads "Your turn · N" or "<name>'s turn · N" in the turn player's colour; only the name shortens on a narrow screen. (r11)
- **TRN-006** Under it, the move pill: a solid green "Your move" when it is your move (TRN-058), otherwise an outlined "Waiting · <name>", also while catching up (HIS-094). Tapping it does nothing: the move isn't passed by hand. (r53)
- **TRN-007** The step button in the top bar is a square in the current phase's colour with the step's icon and its short name, as in the phase dialog (TRN-008), with no border colour or turn number. It flashes on a step change and opens the phase dialog. (r35)
- **TRN-008** The phase dialog is a real dialog titled with the step and its phase ("Combat · Declare attackers"), then "Your turn" / "<name>'s turn"; phase labels sit above rows of step squares, the current one outlined in the turn player's colour. Square names fit on one line on a phone: Untap, Upkeep, Draw · Main 1 · Begin, Attackers, Blockers, Damage, End · Main 2 · End, Cleanup ("1st strike" while a combat has it, TRN-056). (r37)
- **TRN-009** The phase dialog only shows where the turn is: its step squares do nothing and it has no buttons; the game moves on only with the main button (TRN-063). (r47)
- **TRN-010** The top bar's Table actions button opens a dialog with Timeline (the only way into timeline mode, HIS-004), Suggest game end (or Accept result while the opponent's suggestion waits), Concede (TRN-064), Send problem report (Settings' "Report a problem" with its optional description, sent without leaving the game) and Game settings (LOB-135), each an icon and its name. Nothing in it changes the board by hand (TBL-119). (r58)

### Whose turn and whose move

- **TRN-012** Whose TURN shows on the battlefields: only the turn player's battlefield is tinted (your own green-ish, the opponent's red-ish, the land band a little darker); the other battlefield is neutral grey. (r11)
- **TRN-013** Whose MOVE shows on the bars: the top and bottom bars take the light colour of the player whose move it is (TRN-058), never part of an event. In timeline mode both bars are grey. (r50)
- **TRN-074** Turn banner: at a turn change "Your turn" or "<name>'s turn" shows at the screen's centre in the turn player's colour and fades out in about 1.2 s, taking no touches, holding nothing and waiting for nothing but a play shown before it (HIS-112), live and in catch-up playback. It and the plays shown never overlap: a play that went on the Stack before the turn change shows first, one after it waits for the banner. Never in timeline mode, a take-back or on opening a game. (r61)
- **TRN-075** Your move cue: when the move passes to you from the other player (you may act again and their real input came since, or playback stops at their assumption about you, TRN-063), your phone gives one haptic tick and the main button one pulse in your colour, once per passing, not repeating. Never for your own chain of steps, in playback only when you actually stop at a decision (not a stop your own pause made), not when you've just acted yourself (their answer within 1.5 s of your own action), not in timeline mode or on opening a game; while a play, a combat or a reveal holds your view it comes when that is done. (r61)
- **TRN-058** The move is the player the game really waits for (the record "Your move" notifications use, LOB-073). While one player plays on assumptions about the other, the acting player has it, even while the other's playback stops at an assumption they may answer. An open undo request (HIS-100) or result suggestion (TRN-047) is the answerer's move; a result is the move of whoever hasn't seen it. When nobody is waited for, the game list says "Move not available". The move passing to you has a cue (TRN-075). (r61)

### Bottom bar and quick actions

- **TRN-015** The bottom bar shows your toggles (TRN-059) on either player's turn and the main button with what it holds now (TRN-063, TRN-004); where it holds nothing its place stays empty, in your own turn too. During your opening your quick actions are Mulligan and Keep (TRN-062). (r60)
- **TRN-016** Quick actions are equal-width buttons in the popup style (icon plus small black label, 48dp tall) sharing the width left of the main button (TRN-017) equally, with press feedback. A greyed quick action stays in place. (r56)
- **TRN-017** The main button sits in the bottom-right corner, arrow-shaped, 112 dp wide whatever quick actions show beside it (TRN-016), with an icon above the words of what a tap does (TRN-063). Tap = what it says; hold = End turn, with a small haptic tick, in every response mode, except: holding Resolve (STK-029) or a Stack wait's "Assume resolves" (TRN-070) resolves the whole Stack: your phone presses Resolve at each of your priority points and Assume at each of the other player's (where the information rule allows it, TRN-071), whatever your response mode, until the Stack is empty, a decision of yours, a reveal stop (HIS-110), a greyed Assume, your own action, or the other player's real input arriving; that input is never passed over: if it reaches the server before the chain's next press, that press isn't made and you get priority with it on top (where your response mode decides, as always). Holding an Assume with nothing on the Stack ("Assume no response", "Assume no blockers", "Assume blockers") is its tap; holding a greyed one says why. (r59)
- **TRN-063** The main button holds whatever goes on now. It is white with a grey outline and says under an icon what a tap does, with room for two lines (wrapped between words; drawn smaller rather than cut where two lines don't fit): "To combat", "End turn" (not stopping at your own main phases, TRN-057; in your own second main phase only in Normal and Quick, which don't stop at your end step), "End step" (in Control, in your own second main phase: a tap goes on to your end step, where Control stops; at your end step it reads "End turn"), "Next", "Pass", "Attack (2)" / "No attack", "Block (1)" / "No blocks", "Resolve" with something on the Stack (STK-029), and "OK" while your division of combat damage is open (CMB-033); while the other player must act it holds the wait's Assume (TRN-070). Nothing sits under the Stack. It is greyed when it can't act (your cast or another choice open, the other player's reveal panel open until its OK (HIS-110), the opening, a declaration Automatum doesn't allow, a division not yet complete): a tap shakes it and holding it says why. When the result of your own action (a tap or hold of the main button, or a card tap that makes an action) hasn't shown 0.15 s after the press, a soft violet band sweeps across the main button with a thin violet line along its bottom until it shows, at least 0.3 s; a quicker result looks as before, and playback never shows it. When the move passes to you it pulses once in your colour (TRN-075). (r61)
- **TRN-023** Your opening's Mulligan and Keep (TRN-062) are each one Undo step, however many changes they make; the settings in the quick actions (TRN-059) are no events at all (SYS-071). (r60)
- **TRN-059** The quick-actions row holds your own settings for this game, on either player's turn. In live play: the response mode (a tap goes on to the next of Control, Normal and Quick; its name shows), "Assume resolves" (a tap goes on to the next of Never, Normal, When they have no mana and Always, HIS-096; it reads "Assume: Never", "Assume: Normal", "Assume: No mana" or "Assume: Always") and "Autotap mana". The response mode and Assume resolves cells always fill by how much control you keep: white words and icon on deep blue for Control / Assume: Never, violet for Normal / Assume: Normal, amber for Quick / Assume: No mana, red-orange for Assume: Always. In catch-up playback (HIS-094): the same response mode cell as live play's (its name, colour and tap; playback stops where it stops, HIS-095) and "Autotap mana". Equal-width cells with an icon and words; a toggle on is filled violet with a check, off white with an empty ring; holding one says what it does. Only you see or change them (the other player's phone learns your response mode, STK-036). Other quick actions: your opening's (TRN-062) and combat staging's (CMB-048, CMB-034). (r57)

### Game start and mulligans

- **TRN-027** A game begins with both players "Mulliganing" at the same time (TRN-062); keeping makes you Ready. Declarations made at nearly the same moment both stand (the later one is made again on top of the other). The starting player plays turn 1 only after the other player's own keep: their keep is never assumed, whether they are away or online (HIS-096). While both openings are open, each player's own mulligan is their move (LOB-022, LOB-072). (r53)
- **TRN-029** There is no Turn 0: both players mulligan during turn 1, while their opening status is set. (r25)
- **TRN-062** The opening follows the game's rules (opening hand 7, the first mulligan free, London mulligans, CR 103.5), both players declaring at once. While you are "Mulliganing" your quick actions are "Mulligan" (your hand goes back, your library is shuffled and seven are drawn) and "Keep" (with cards owed to the bottom, the Keep / Bottom screen opens first, STK-043); nothing else asks about it or covers the board. (r51)

### Life

- **TRN-030** Each player starts at 20 life. Tapping a life square opens a floating Life panel with that player's life (only the rules change it) and, for the other player, their presence line (TRN-043), for you the quick messages and Write (LOB-139); its life row sits on the panel edge facing the square (the panel opens under the opponent's square and above your own, in landscape too). (r59)
- **TRN-034** Portrait: each player's row shows the life square and packed piles; the room beside them stays free. (r45)
- **TRN-035** Landscape: each player's life square and piles sit in their bar row (TBL-009), the opponent's in the top bar and yours in the bottom bar, their emblems right after them. (r57)

### Emblems

- **TRN-053** A player's emblems show as small round chips (24 dp) with a violet edge in a row by the player's life square, oldest first: each with the emblem's art from the card records (the emblem printing with the same rules text), or an emblem icon when there is none; beyond three, the fourth chip reads "+N" for the rest. In portrait the row sits at the right of the free room right of the player's piles, clear of the hands (when that room is too narrow, by the life square: under the opponent's, above your own, after the room chip, TRN-069); in landscape right after the player's piles in their bar row (TBL-009), after the room chip, clear of the hands and of your quick actions (your row gives way to your hand in the bottom bar, TBL-009): where the row doesn't fit, its chips shrink (to 20 dp at least) and then fewer show, the last reading "+N". A tap on one does nothing; holding any of them inspects all that player's emblems (TBL-104) until the finger lifts: one dark panel each, outlined in its owner's colour, with the emblem's art (or the icon), "Emblem" and its full text. (r60)
- **TRN-072** What the rules keep for a player and show nowhere else shows as small text chips (18 dp, violet edge) in a row by the player's life square: Monarch, Initiative, City's blessing, Enduring story, "Speed 2", the Ring's level as "Ring 2/4", the player's counters ("Poison 3", "Energy 5"), "Dungeons 1" for dungeons completed, "Extra turn" and "Skips a turn". They follow the emblems' row (TRN-053): in portrait at the right of the free room right of the piles (when that room is too narrow, by the life square, past the room chip and the emblems), in landscape right after the piles in the bar row, widening it as far as the hand allows; with emblems or a room chip, on a line above them. Where they don't all fit, the last chip reads "+N". A tap does nothing; holding one shows what it means in the dark rule popup (TBL-132, placed as SYS-045), a "+N" what all it stands for, until the finger lifts. Both players see the same. (r60)
- **TRN-073** Day or night (CR 731) belongs to the game: a chip like TRN-072's, "Day" or "Night", right-aligned just above the Stack, in both orientations, while it is one of them. Holding it says what it means. (r60)
- **TRN-069** A player's dungeon (LOB-117) shows as a small card in a fourth place after Library, Graveyard and Exile (in landscape too, in the bar row, TBL-009), and the room its marker is on as a violet chip "Room: <room>", first among the emblem chips. Tapping it does nothing, holding inspects it, and it can't be dragged. Dungeons to choose from show as cards; a room's ability on the Stack shows its dungeon. (r57)

### Opponent presence

- **TRN-042** The opponent's life square has a small dot in its corner: green when they have this game open and touched it in the last 2 minutes, yellow when open but idle, grey when away. It is hidden while you are disconnected. (r29)
- **TRN-043** The opponent's Life panel has an info line with the same dot: "Online", "Idle 7 min", "Last seen 12 min ago", "Last seen 3 h ago", "Last seen Oct 27" or "Not seen yet". (r29)

### Waiting, assumptions and results

- **TRN-046** Suggest game end opens a chooser My win / Draw game, then a confirm dialog ("Suggest my win?" or "Suggest draw?") with an optional one-line "Reason", at most 200 characters (placeholder "Infinite combo"); both players see the reason in quotes in the suggestion panel (TRN-047). (r45)
- **TRN-047** A waiting suggestion shows as a panel on the table, not a dialog: "<name> proposed their win." (or "a draw"; "You proposed your win." for the author). The opponent gets Decline and Accept (an agreed result, which ends the game at once: the player accepting goes straight to the lobby, the author sees the result dialog, TRN-065), the author Withdraw; made at the newest event. (r60)
- **TRN-064** Concede (a flag icon) in Table actions asks "Concede?" ("You lose this game at once.", Cancel / Concede in red); Concede ends the game at once as your loss (CR 104.3a), also while a suggestion waits: you go straight to the lobby and the other player sees the result dialog (TRN-065). If the other player's latest actions haven't reached you yet, the game first branches at your local head, so the result never depends on what you haven't seen. (r48)
- **TRN-065** Every ending (a loss by the rules, a draw, the loop draw of TRN-060, a concession, TRN-064, an accepted suggestion, TRN-047) shows each player a dialog in the middle of the screen once the end is shown to them: big "You won", "You lost" or "Draw", under it how it ended ("Bob's life reached 0", "You drew from an empty library", "Bob got 10 poison counters", "<card>'s effect", "Bob conceded", "You conceded", "Agreed: <reason>" ("Agreed" without one), "Both players lost at once", "An endless loop"), the eye at its bottom left (hides the dialog to look at what is behind it; only the eye stays, at the same spot) and OK at its right. A result the rules gave isn't final until both have seen it: until then the dialog adds "Waiting for <name> to catch up" with OK greyed, it can't be declined, and Undo (HIS-084) still takes the step back; once both have seen it either player's OK makes it final and recorded (LOB-126) and the other player notified (LOB-111). The dialog stays until that player taps OK, whenever and on whichever device they open the game (once the game is back in its lobby, over the lobby); it is never closed for them in the background. The player whose own action ended the game (the conceder, the player who accepted the suggestion, the player whose OK made the rules' result final) has OK'd it already and goes straight to the lobby. Your OK takes you to the lobby (LOB-046), whose status line then says "<name> hasn't seen the last game's result yet" while the other player hasn't tapped theirs; Ready stays greyed ("Tap OK on the last game's result first.") while you haven't, and the next game starts only once both have (the server keeps each player's OK). (r49)
- **TRN-066** The wait: while the other player must act (online or away), faint words over the battlefield (never taking a touch or covering the board; not while a play is shown, HIS-112) say why: "<name> may respond to <spell>" (an ability as "<source>'s ability", STK-046), "<name> may act · <step>", "<name> is declaring blockers", "<name> is deciding on a mulligan", "<name> is choosing · <card>" (the card only when you can see it), "Waiting for <name>". (r58)
- **TRN-070** Where the other player's answer may be assumed (HIS-096), the main button holds "Assume resolves" (something on the Stack, STK-028), "Assume no response" (nothing on the Stack, as after their blocks), or "Assume no blockers" / "Assume blockers" (CMB-040) while they must act, whether they are online or away, with no timeout, also at points they began, where only their mode decides whether your phone waits (HIS-096); holding "Assume resolves" resolves the whole Stack (TRN-017), holding any other Assume is its tap; greyed where assuming would show you hidden information or a random result (TRN-071). Unless "Assume resolves" is Never, their pause at your end step and right after you attack is never a wait (HIS-096). A tap of Assume and your phone's own assumption are the same press (HIS-109). There is no Assume keep: the other player's opening is always waited for (TRN-027). (r59)
- **TRN-067** Information rule: while your line rests on an unconfirmed assumption, nothing you do or assume may show you a card you couldn't see (the other player's hand or library, face-down cards, random discards; never your own library) or a random result (coin, die, random choice), apart from what the game's settings allow (LOB-135: by default the other player's library cards, not their hand's; with "Allow assumes that reveal hands" their hand cards too, a random discard of theirs included; never face-down cards or other random results). Such an action is refused before it shows: the card shakes and holding it says "Wait for the other player to catch up: this would show you hidden cards or a random result." Where assuming the other player's answer would do so, the wait's Assume shows greyed (TRN-071). The server checks the same for cards, with the game's settings. (r58)
- **TRN-071** Under the information rule (TRN-067, with the game's settings LOB-135: a reveal they allow neither stops nor greys anything), your phone's own passes after an action of yours stop before one that would show you something (you keep priority there), and such an assumption isn't made: the wait reads "Waiting for <name> · going on would show you hidden cards or a random result" (TRN-066) and the main button holds the wait's Assume ("Assume resolves", "Assume no response") greyed: a tap shakes it and holding it says "Assuming this would show you hidden cards or a random result: wait for <name>." (r58)
- **TRN-068** The unconfirmed mark: while actions of yours rest on answers the other player hasn't confirmed yet, a small violet dot (no number) sits at the main button's corner; a tap or hold says "<name> hasn't seen your last moves yet; if <name> answers differently, they'll be taken back". Only you see it. (r50)
- **TRN-048** Decline and Withdraw are their own events, not undos. (r18)
- **TRN-049** While a suggestion waits, every other game action is blocked for a player once the board shown to them includes it; chat, timeline browsing and holding to inspect still work. (r48)
- **TRN-050** A suggestion left behind on a discarded line (an earlier real answer of the other player's branched, HIS-097) no longer locks anything. (r48)
- **TRN-051** The suggestion panel appears once the board shown to you includes its event (in catch-up playback when it is reached, TRN-049), and its buttons work from then on. (r60)

### Combat area

- **CMB-001** Combat happens in the combat band between the two battlefields (CMB-007), which is always there: collapsed, it is as tall as one row of target chips and empty. While you declare attackers, a chip for every target you may attack fades in there (CMB-013), your own view only. The band opens by itself when the first attacker is staged (there is no tap to open it) and Automatum collapses it again itself (SYS-069); in playback it opens only when someone actually attacked. (r55)
- **CMB-002** Attackers and blockers are combat copies of real cards: the real card stays where it is. A card has at most one copy per combat area. (r60)
- **CMB-003** Combat copies are always upright and can't be tapped. A first tap only selects one (CMB-032); tapping the selected copy takes it out of combat (CMB-004) while it is being declared: a staged attacker is unstaged, a placed blocker removed (also one placed to assume, CMB-040); otherwise it deselects it. A copy has no menu (no Combat menu, TBL-116) and there is no "Attack →" (CMB-048). Double-tap does nothing; hold inspects. (r57)
- **CMB-004** Taking a copy out of combat (CMB-003) takes it out of the declaration Automatum waits for (CMB-028); the real card is unchanged. (r56)
- **CMB-006** When the real card leaves the battlefield, all its combat copies disappear; surviving blockers of a removed attacker stay in place. (r60)
- **CMB-007** Rows for the attacking player: the target chips on top (each with its value on it, CMB-013), then blockers, then (after a gap for the block arrows, CMB-031; "Choose blockers" sits in it until the first block, CMB-034) attackers, each lane under its chip, with dashed slots for the next attacker or blocker while they are placed (CMB-028, CMB-034); the defender sees it mirrored (chips at the bottom, by their battlefield). The band is part of the table: a cream area (the old combat area's colour, #f5f1df) across the table's whole width between the battlefields, with no panel of its own; open, it pushes the battlefields apart, its lanes in its middle. It pans and zooms with the battlefields (TBL-001); nothing of it floats over them. The band takes combat drops. (r57)
- **CMB-042** Combat cards are exactly the battlefield cards' size at the same zoom; they zoom with the band (CMB-007), so zooming out (or landscape's opening view, TBL-121) shrinks them with the battlefields. (r55)
- **CMB-008** Pairs are packed groups without dividers between them (only lane separators), centred as a group and re-centred when added; cards sit centred in their slot (one attacker over two blockers is centred), with about two card-border widths of margin. (r40)

### Attack targets

- **CMB-012** While combat is open (and as chips while you declare attackers), every attackable target always has its own lane in the same attacker/blocker rows: the defending player first, then each face-up planeswalker and battle in battlefield order, including your own Sieges (protected by the opponent). An empty lane is as wide as its chip, at least one card slot; while you declare attackers a lane with attackers keeps one card slot more at its end (CMB-028). (r61)
- **CMB-013** Each lane's header is its target's chip, centred over the lane: crossed swords, a round avatar (the defending player's avatar, or their initial in their colour; the card's art; "?" for a face-down card) and the name, at most 12 letters (a longer name is cut with "…"; "Face-down card" for a hidden target), white with an edge in the target side's colour. After the name the chip shows the target's life, loyalty or defense counters (none for a hidden target), collapsed or open; with the damage preview it shows what that would become, "20 → 17" in red, the chip widening about its centre (CMB-029); its lane always keeps room for that, negative results included ("3 → −2"), so nothing moves when the preview shows. Chips keep their own size, zooming with the band, and fade in when they first show. Holding a chip shows the full name over it and highlights the target's card or the player's life square while held. Lanes widen to fit their chips; a too-wide band pans with the table. (r57)
- **CMB-046** A planeswalker or battle that left the battlefield keeps its lane, its chip reading "<name> (gone)", without a value: its attackers stay attacking but attack nothing and deal no combat damage (CR 506.4c). (r55)
- **CMB-014** Lane separators in the open band are 2dp, fairly dark, scaling with card size, run through the chip row, and keep about 2dp of space around cards and chips. (r55)
- **CMB-015** Dropping a staged attacker anywhere into another lane, also an empty one, retargets its whole pair (blockers stay attached); dropped in its own lane it stays. Combat drags do nothing else: no new pairs at dividers, no reordering or swapping, no role changes. (r60)

### Blocks and arrows

- **CMB-031** Every block is an arrow from the blocker to the attacker it blocks, drawn in the gap between the blocker and attacker rows. A blocker sits in the pair of the first attacker it blocks; one blocking several attackers is one card with an arrow to each (no second copy), and an attacker blocked only by an arrow counts as blocked. (r49)
- **CMB-032** Tapping a combat copy selects it: a violet frame, with the copies it fights framed in amber and its arrows amber. Tapping it again unstages or removes it while it is being declared, else deselects it (CMB-003); tapping the empty table clears the selection. The selection is the viewer's own and lasts only for the combat step it was made in. While blockers are declared, tapping a defender's creature picks it as the blocker to place instead (CMB-034). (r56)

### Declaring attackers and blockers

- **CMB-028** While attackers are awaited, one target is active: its chip filled violet and, once the band is open, its lane framed in violet (first the defending player's; tapping a chip makes its target the active one). The chips glow softly in violet as something to tap, more while a finger is down on one (TBL-117). Tapping one of your creatures stages it as an attacker of the active target (its copy appears in that lane), tapping it again unstages it (in a stack a tap stages the next one, CMB-047); one that can't attack that target isn't staged and Automatum's reason shows. Dragging works the same (onto a chip's lane, also in the collapsed band), also to retarget (CMB-015). While attackers are awaited, your creatures that may attack glow red-orange from behind (the cyan glow stays "can activate", TBL-117). Once the band is open the active lane has a dashed slot in the attackers' row, a card's size in red-orange: centred in an empty lane, else after its attackers (every lane with attackers keeps a slot's room while you declare, so choosing another target moves nothing); it is brighter and faintly filled while the creature you are dragging may attack that target, and a drop on it stages the creature there. The collapsed band has no slot: it stays one chip row (CMB-001). (r61)
- **CMB-048** While attackers are declared the quick actions are "Rest → <target>" and "Clear", and the main button reads "Attack (N)" ("No attack" with nothing staged) and declares what the lanes hold as one step. Only the lanes pick targets: no card menu has "Attack →". (r52)
- **CMB-047** While attackers are awaited nobody holds priority: a tap on your creature only stages or unstages it, never activating anything (TBL-122), and card menus show their ability rows greyed ("Nobody holds priority now."); a mana ability an attack cost needs is paid as the main button declares (CMB-044).
