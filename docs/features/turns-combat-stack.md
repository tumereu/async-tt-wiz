# Turns, combat and Stack

Turn and step tracking, whose move it is, the top and bottom game bars, life, player counters, statuses, dice and coins, game start and result suggestions (`TRN`), the combat area (`CMB`) and the Stack (`STK`).

### Turn structure

- **TRN-001** A turn has 12 positions: Untap, Upkeep, Draw, Main 1, Beginning of combat, Declare attackers, Declare blockers, Combat damage, End of combat, Main 2, End step, Cleanup. With Automatum off players move through them by hand and nothing advances by itself; with it on the turn moves through Automatum (TRN-057), which adds a First-strike damage step when a combat needs one (TRN-056). (r37)
- **TRN-056** With Automatum on, a combat in which a creature with first strike or double strike is fighting has two combat damage steps (CR 510.4): First-strike damage (a bolt icon, short name "1st strike"), then Combat damage. The step button shows it like any step; the phase dialog shows its square between Blockers and Damage from the moment that combat reaches it until the combat ends. Played by hand the turn never passes through it: Next step from Declare blockers goes to Combat damage. (r37)
- **TRN-057** With Automatum on, the step button's phase dialog and the step arrow move the turn through Automatum. Next step passes priority for the player holding it (either player may press it, TRN-004, asked first when it acts for the other player, TRN-061); End turn ("End opponent's turn") goes on passing for that player until the turn ends, attacking with nothing; a later step in the phase dialog goes on passing for them until the turn reaches it (attacking with nothing when it comes after combat). In the declare attackers or blockers step, Next step declares what the combat lanes hold. Automatum carries out each step's own actions (untapping, drawing but not on the starting player's first turn, damage wearing off and effects ending at cleanup, discarding to hand size as a choice), puts "at the beginning of …" triggers on the Stack and takes extra turns, then goes on by itself through every step in which nobody can act ("Skip when I have no choice", SYS-063) or has a stop or pause (STK-036) until a player has one or must choose; everything up to there is one step and one Undo. Going back to an earlier step, choosing the step it is in, and changing the step or turn any other way (quick actions and combat tools that change the step) are refused: "With Automatum on, the turn moves on through Automatum: use Next step or End turn." On a board resting in an untap step (a new game after its opening, TRN-062, or a turn changed by hand while Automatum was off) Next step is the active player's: the step ends as if their untapping were done and the upkeep follows. (r42)
- **TRN-061** With Automatum on, Next step, End turn, a later step in the phase dialog and the combat quick actions (Commit attack, No attack, Commit blocks, No blocks, Pass) ask first when they would act for the other player, the one holding priority or declaring attackers or blockers, as Pass for them does (STK-028): "Pass for Alice?" ("Alice has priority and may still want to act. Passing for them lets the game go on."), "End the turn for Alice?" ("… Automatum passes for them until the turn ends, attacking with nothing." in her own turn before her attack), "Declare attackers for Alice?" / "Declare blockers for Bob?" ("… This declares for them what the combat lanes hold: 2 attackers." / "no attack." / "no blocks."), "Declare no blocks for Bob?" (No blocks with blocks in the lanes: "… This clears the blocks in the combat lanes and declares no blocks for them.", nothing cleared before it is confirmed), with Pass for them, End turn for them or Declare for them, and Cancel. Nothing is sent until it is confirmed; confirmed after the table changed, it says "The table changed: try again." (r43)
- **TRN-060** With Automatum on, when the game comes back to the same position with only passes nobody could avoid in between (abilities that keep triggering each other, CR 104.4b), Automatum stops going on by itself, and its "By hand" choice (STK-032) says "The game is a draw: it is in a loop of actions nobody can stop (CR 104.4b). Done ends it as a draw, unless the loop was broken by hand."; Done makes it a draw in Automatum, which then offers no more actions (the game's result is suggested as usual, TRN-046), unless the board was changed by hand meanwhile, when play goes on. Abilities that keep triggering without anyone getting priority stop after 200 rounds with a "By hand" choice "Possible loop: …"; Done goes on. (r39)
- **TRN-002** Turn numbers count both players' turns, so turn 1 is only the starting player's first turn. Next step from Cleanup, or End turn, passes the turn to the other player's Untap. (r33)
- **TRN-052** A turn change Automatum makes names whose turn begins, so an extra turn goes to the same player again (turn number up by one, at Untap); history reads "Turn N → name". (r36)
- **TRN-003** Passing the turn clears every card's damage, until-end-of-turn P/T and until-end-of-turn note at once. (r16)
- **TRN-004** Either player may change the step or end the turn, also on the opponent's turn; the opponent's version reads "End opponent's turn". With Automatum on, doing so for the player who must act asks first (TRN-061). (r40)

### Top bar

- **TRN-005** The top bar's first line reads "Your turn · N" or "<name>'s turn · N" in the turn player's colour; only the name shortens on a narrow screen. (r11)
- **TRN-006** Under it, the move pill: a solid green "Your move" when you hold the move, otherwise an outlined "Waiting · <name>". Tapping the pill passes the move to the other player; anyone can pass, and it always flips to the other player. It does nothing in timeline mode or while disconnected. (r11)
- **TRN-007** The step button in the top bar is a square in the current phase's colour, showing the step's icon and its short name, the same names as in the phase dialog (TRN-008) (no border colour, no turn number). It flashes on a step change and opens the phase dialog. (r35)
- **TRN-008** The phase dialog is a real dialog, not a floating popup. Its title is the step with its phase (e.g. "Combat · Declare attackers"), then the turn player ("Your turn" / "<name>'s turn"); phase labels sit above each row of step squares, and the current step is outlined in the turn player's colour. Step squares use short names that always fit on one line on a phone: Untap, Upkeep, Draw · Main 1 · Begin, Attackers, Blockers, Damage, End · Main 2 · End, Cleanup (and with Automatum "1st strike" between Blockers and Damage while a combat has that step, TRN-056). (r37)
- **TRN-009** The phase dialog has Next step and End turn buttons. Choosing a later step records the steps passed on the way; choosing an earlier step is one single correction event. (r33)
- **TRN-054** The Automatum switch sits in the top bar just left of Table actions and, like it, is hidden while reviewing the timeline. It is a round sigil: off, a dim outlined rune at about 40% opacity; on, the rune on a violet-to-cyan disc with a soft glow that breathes over about 3 s and one small spark circling it. Tapping it first asks "Turn Automatum on?" ("Automatum then plays the game by the rules for both players: it casts, resolves and moves things for you.", Cancel / Turn on) or "Turn Automatum off?" ("Both players then play by hand; nothing is checked or done automatically.", plus "Automatum's unfinished step is taken back first." and "Your unfinished cast is dropped." when they apply; Cancel / Turn off); Cancel, Back or a tap outside does nothing, and only Turn on / Turn off turns Automatum on or off for the whole game (SYS-055). Where turning on is refused, the tap shows the refusal's reason at once, without asking first (SYS-059); switching off during an unfinished Automatum step always goes through and first takes that step back (or closes it where it is, SYS-060), and drops your own open cast, nothing of which was sent (STK-025). History reads "alice turned Automatum on" (HIS-091). (r42)
- **TRN-055** Holding the Automatum switch opens the Automatum panel, a floating sheet like Manage Stack that its ✕ Close, a tap outside it and Back all close. In plain words: "Automatum on" and what it does ("It plays by the rules for both players: casting, abilities, combat and the turn's steps.", or "Automatum off" and "Both players play by hand; nothing is checked or done automatically."); what is done by hand ("By hand: Wild Surge, Storm Spark (2). Automatum can't read these abilities yet; you carry them out yourself.", naming only cards you can see, so it tells nothing of hidden cards); why Automatum can't take over the board when it can't (TBL-112) in plain sentences that name only cards you can see; your own "Skip when I have no choice" switch (SYS-063); "Clear Automatum data" with a sweep icon (enabled while there is any; asks "Clear Automatum data?" first; during an unfinished Automatum step it takes that step back first, SYS-060); and at the bottom, small and grey, "Engine details", closed until tapped, which opens a read-only list of the engine data the game keeps. (r42)
- **TRN-010** The top bar's Table actions button opens the Table actions dialog: Create combat area or Close combat area (only the one that applies), Create token, Roll die…, Flip coin, Timeline, and Suggest game end (or Accept result while the opponent's suggestion is waiting). With Automatum on, Create or Close combat area and Create token aren't there (Automatum opens and closes the combat area itself, SYS-069), except while Automatum waits for something done by hand. (r43)
- **TRN-011** Opening a game shows "Loading table…" until the table loads; "Table unavailable" only when it really fails. (r13)

### Whose turn and whose move

- **TRN-012** Whose TURN shows on the battlefields: only the turn player's battlefield is tinted (your own green-ish, the opponent's red-ish, the land band a little darker); the other battlefield is neutral grey. (r11)
- **TRN-013** Whose MOVE shows on the bars: the top and bottom bars take the light colour of the player holding the move. In timeline mode both bars are grey. (r15)
- **TRN-014** Every step change gives the move to the turn player, except moving to Declare blockers, which gives it to the defender. This change is part of the step event; only a manual pass creates its own event, and there are no other automatic passes. Steps passed on the way inside a multi-step advance leave the move where it is. With Automatum on the move follows who must act instead (TRN-058). (r37)
- **TRN-058** With Automatum on, the move follows who must act: at the end of every Automatum step it is with the player holding priority, the player an open choice belongs to, or the player who declares attackers or blockers, so the move pill, the bar colours and "your move" notifications follow them. That pass of the move is part of the step, not an event of its own. Passing the move by hand still works (TRN-006). (r37)

### Bottom bar and quick actions

- **TRN-015** The bottom bar shows quick actions only on your turn, or on the opponent's turn while you hold the move (that step's actions). Otherwise it shows a faded "Opponent's turn". With Automatum on it shows your Automatum toggles on either turn (TRN-059), the step arrow only when it would otherwise show. (r43)
- **TRN-016** Quick actions are equal-width buttons in the popup style (icon plus small black label, 48dp tall) filling the width left of the step arrow, with press feedback. A greyed quick action stays in place. (r14)
- **TRN-017** The step arrow sits in the bottom-right corner, arrow-shaped, about 72dp wide, with the next step's icon above its short name, the same names as in the phase dialog (TRN-008) ("End turn" when the next step passes the turn). Tap = Next step; hold = End turn, with a small haptic tick. With Automatum on it says what a tap does instead (TRN-063). (r42)
- **TRN-018** Step arrow style (Automatum off; with it on the arrow is neutral, TRN-063): your turn and your move = filled with your colour; opponent's turn and your move = white with an opponent-coloured border; not your move = grey border. (r42)
- **TRN-063** With Automatum on, the step arrow is white with a grey outline whoever's turn and move it is, and says under an icon exactly what a tap does, in MTG Arena's words: "Pass" while the other player of the turn holds priority, for the turn's player "To combat" in their first main phase, "End turn" in their second main phase and end step (passing hands the turn over) and "Next" in any other step; "Attack (2)" / "No attack" and "Block (1)" / "No blocks" while attackers or blockers are declared (the number: the creatures in the lanes); "Resolve" with Automatum's spell or ability on the Stack (a tap passes priority as Let it resolve does, STK-027); "Done" while Automatum waits for something done by hand (a tap is Done, STK-032). While another choice is open (or the Stack holds only entries made by hand) it reads "Next" greyed, and a tap says why. Hold is End turn as before (TRN-017); acting for the other player asks first (TRN-061). (r42)
- **TRN-019** Untap step: "Untap all", "Untap + next" and "Untap, draw → main" (untap, draw in the Draw step, then Main 1). Upkeep: "Draw + main". Draw: "Draw one" and "Draw one + next". (r33)
- **TRN-020** Main 1: "Select attackers" (moves to Declare attackers and turns on multi-select) and "Skip combat" (moves to End step). With Automatum on neither is there (TRN-059): Next step goes on to Declare attackers through Automatum, where taps stage attackers (CMB-028). (r43)
- **TRN-021** Main 2 and End step: a visible "End turn" quick action. (r3)
- **TRN-022** On the starting player's turn 1 no quick action draws: Untap, Upkeep and Draw offer "Go to main" instead. (r25)
- **TRN-023** Each quick action is one Undo step, however many changes it makes. (r15)
- **TRN-024** There are no quick actions for tapping/untapping the selection or for the graveyard, and no automatic combat-damage maths: with Automatum off players deal combat damage by hand (I-D39). (r40)
- **TRN-059** With Automatum on, the quick-actions row is your Automatum toggles, on either player's turn, left of the step arrow: "Full control" (SYS-071), "Skip if no choice" (Skip when I have no choice, SYS-063) and "Autotap mana" (LOB-113), equal-width icon + text toggles, tinted violet when on; holding one says what it does. Full control and Autotap mana change your device's setting (Full control then goes into the game as SYS-071 says), Skip when I have no choice your setting in the game. There are no other quick actions then (no manual ones and none that move the turn: the step arrow and the phase dialog do, TRN-057), except your opening's (Mull N, Mull −1 and Ready, TRN-027, TRN-062) and Automatum's combat ones while it offers them to you (CMB-028, CMB-029, CMB-034). (r43)

### Game start and mulligans

- **TRN-026** The game setup's Starting player box offers Random or either player, plus an "Alternate" switch in its top-right corner, on by default: after each play ends, the player who did NOT start it is set to start the next. You can still pick by hand. (r28)
- **TRN-027** A game begins with both players in the status "Mulliganing". While yours is, your quick actions are "Mull N", "Mull −1" and "Ready"; Ready clears the status. The non-starting player sees only those three, with no step arrow. (r25)
- **TRN-028** The starting player's normal turn-1 quick actions stay hidden while either player's status is exactly "Mulliganing"; any manually set or edited status counts as cleared. (r25)
- **TRN-029** There is no Turn 0: both players mulligan during turn 1, while their opening status is set. (r25)
- **TRN-062** With Automatum on, the opening stays as it is (TRN-027–TRN-029): while your status is "Mulliganing" on turn 1, Mull N, Mull −1 and "Put on bottom" in your own hand cards' menu (a cell after Inspect, the London mulligan's bottom) work, for your own hand and library only; once you are Ready they are refused like every change by hand (SYS-069). The game begins only once both players are Ready: until then the step button and every other way of moving the turn on say "The game begins once both players are Ready." Then the step button begins the starting player's first turn through Automatum from its untap step, without a draw (TRN-057). Switching Automatum on during the opening or after it works the same way. (r43)

### Life and player counters

- **TRN-030** Each player starts at 20 life. Tapping a life square opens a floating Life panel with life and status but no counters: the life row (heart, −, value, +) sits on the panel edge facing the life square, also in landscape, where the panel opens beside the square. (r26)
- **TRN-031** Life and player counters can be changed by either player, by − / + steppers, by tapping the value to type a number, or by dragging the value. With Automatum on, dragging a life square on the table is refused: the life square shakes (TBL-118); the Life panel's life −, + and value are greyed: a tap shakes them, holding one says why ("With Automatum on, life changes only by the rules. Switch Automatum off to change it by hand."), except while Automatum waits for something done by hand (STK-032); the status controls and the coloured counters, which Automatum ignores, still work. (r43)
- **TRN-032** Value drags: the dominant axis counts; right is +, left is −; vertically, toward the other player is −, toward your own edge is +. The first 3 units cost 33.6dp each, then it speeds up (about 30 units at 288dp). A bubble anchored to the value (above it, below near the top edge) shows e.g. "+3 → 23". (r31)
- **TRN-033** Each player has six coloured counters: red, blue, green, yellow, purple and grey. 0 and empty are the same everywhere. (r15)
- **TRN-034** Portrait: each player's row shows packed piles plus an always-visible 3×2 counter grid (a faint outline when a counter is empty). Tapping the grid opens a Counters popup with all six counters and no life. (r15)
- **TRN-035** Landscape: each player's six counters always show in the sidebar as in portrait (a faint outline when empty), in a row under the life square and piles (above them on the opponent's side), with that player's Temp piles at the left of the row before the counters; when they don't fit, the counters move to their own row (ZON-045). Tapping a counter opens the Counters popup. (r35)
- **TRN-036** Set/edit values everywhere use "− [value] +" with one square value field. (r13)

### Status bubbles

- **TRN-037** A player sets their own status in their Life panel: presets OK?, Yes, No, Wait, Thinking…, Go ahead and GG, plus a pen and a Clear button, in a 3×3 grid. Clear and Hide are always shown, greyed when there is no status. (r26)
- **TRN-038** The pen opens a Status dialog prefilled with the current text, selected, keyboard up; up to 512 characters, wrapped, with a "n/512" count; Enter or Save sets it. A preset, Clear or Save closes the panel. (r30)
- **TRN-039** A status shows as a speech bubble by the life square (under the opponent's, above your own), wrapping over up to four lines and never narrower than the life square, so its tail always meets a straight edge; each line is centred in the bubble. The Life panel shows the full status, scrolling after four lines. Statuses stay until cleared. (r42)
- **TRN-040** Setting and clearing a status are game events (in history, undoable) and never change whose move it is. (r25)
- **TRN-041** Each viewer can Hide a player's status bubble (until that player sets a new status) or Mute it for this game (it becomes a small dot, the full text stays in the Life panel). Show and Unmute replace them. These choices are local to the device; they don't silence notifications. (r25)
- **TRN-053** With Automatum, a player's emblems (a planeswalker's ultimate, say) show as violet chips by their life square, each "Emblem: " and the emblem's text, cut after two lines: under the opponent's square, above your own, the oldest nearest, beyond the status bubble or its dot when there is one. Like status bubbles they are drawn only, so taps reach the table beneath. (r36)

### Opponent presence

- **TRN-042** The opponent's life square has a small dot in its corner: green when they have this game open and touched it in the last 2 minutes, yellow when open but idle, grey when away. It is hidden while you are disconnected. (r29)
- **TRN-043** The opponent's Life panel has an info line with the same dot: "Online", "Idle 7 min", "Last seen 12 min ago", "Last seen 3 h ago", "Last seen Oct 27" or "Not seen yet". (r29)

### Dice and coins

- **TRN-044** Roll die… asks for the number of sides (6 by default, any whole number ≥ 1); Flip coin flips at once. Results are random; nobody can pick them. (r12)
- **TRN-045** A result chip appears over the roller's half of the table ("d20 → 14", "Heads / win", "Tails / loss") for about 2.5 s and then fades, for both players, live and in playback but not on jumps or catch-up. In timeline mode it stays while you are stopped on that event group. The timeline icon shows no value badge. (r34)

### Result suggestions

- **TRN-046** Suggest game end opens a chooser My win / My loss / Draw game, then a confirm dialog ("Suggest my win?", "Suggest my loss?" or "Suggest draw?") with an optional one-line "Reason", at most 200 characters (placeholder "Infinite combo"). (r24)
- **TRN-047** A waiting suggestion shows as a panel on the table, not a dialog: "<name> proposed their win." (or loss, or "a draw"; "You proposed your win." for the author) with the reason in quotes. The opponent gets Decline and Accept, the author Withdraw. (r24)
- **TRN-048** Decline and Withdraw are their own events, not undos. (r18)
- **TRN-049** While a suggestion waits, every other game action is blocked for both players; chat, timeline browsing, hold-preview and Inspect still work. (r27)
- **TRN-050** In the timeline the lock applies only when the shown board already includes the suggestion: from an earlier point or another branch you can continue and play on, leaving the suggestion behind on the old branch. (r27)
- **TRN-051** The suggestion panel appears in playback once its event is reached; its buttons work only at the live end, with playback paused. (r18)

### Combat area

- **CMB-001** Combat uses a temporary combat area between the two battlefields. It is created from Table actions, or by itself when the first attacker is declared. It stays open through step changes and closes only on purpose: Close combat area, or a quick action that closes it. (r16)
- **CMB-002** Attackers and blockers are combat copies of real cards: the real card stays where it is, and anything done to a copy's card applies to the real card. A card has at most one copy per combat area. (r1)
- **CMB-003** Combat copies are always upright and can't be tapped. Tapping one opens its own one-page Combat menu, not the normal card menu (with Automatum on a first tap selects it, CMB-032): Inspect, Note, Ping / Unping, Attack →, Remove from combat, Graveyard, Exile, Hand, P/T, Damage, EOT P/T, EOT note, EOT keywords, Link, Links. P/T, Damage and the EOT marks set the real card's values; Graveyard, Exile and Hand move the real card (CMB-004). It has no tap, counter, flip or other battlefield controls (use the real card for those). Double-tap does nothing; hold inspects. (r40)
- **CMB-004** Remove from combat removes only the copy (the real card is not untapped). Graveyard, Exile and Hand move the real card to its owner's zone (Hand is greyed for a token's copy). (r28)
- **CMB-005** Combat copies can have their own notes and pings, separate from the real card. A copy without a note of its own shows the real card's note chip. (r36)
- **CMB-006** When the real card leaves the battlefield, all its combat copies disappear; surviving blockers of a removed attacker stay in place. Closing the area removes all copies but leaves real cards and their state unchanged. (r1)
- **CMB-007** Rows for the attacking player: the target headers on top, then blockers, then, after a gap for the block arrows (CMB-031), attackers. The defender sees it mirrored: attackers on top, their blockers below, the headers at the bottom. The combat area is a white rounded panel sized to its content, floating over the middle of the table: opening it never moves or resizes the battlefields, which show around it (the narrow band between them shows each battlefield's own colour, the upper half the upper battlefield's, the lower half the lower one's). The panel covers the battlefield cards under it, and only the panel takes combat drops: a card dropped beside it lands on the battlefield. A round fold button (a "fold" icon; holding it says "Hide the combat area") just right of the panel's top-right corner folds it away to see the battlefield under it (each viewer's own choice); "Combat ▴" in the middle of the table brings it back, and a new combat area shows again. Its cards are never smaller than 44dp wide, whatever the card size. (r40)
- **CMB-008** Pairs are packed groups without dividers between them (only lane separators), centred as a group and re-centred when added; cards sit centred in their slot (one attacker over two blockers is centred), with about two card-border widths of margin. (r40)
- **CMB-009** Dragging a battlefield card into the combat area makes its copy there. Dropping left or right of all groups makes a new pair at that end; near a divider, a new pair there. While dragging, a faint shorter divider shows where it lands; reordering works the same both ways. Dragging a copy between rows changes its role, except a defending player's blocker dropped in an attacker row, which blocks that attacker (CMB-031). (r40)
- **CMB-010** Dragging a combat copy to a real zone (e.g. graveyard) moves the real card there. (r1)
- **CMB-027** A card dragged into the combat area (from the battlefield or from another combat slot) that covers at least half the width of a card in the row it is dropped in goes beside that card, on the side the dragged card's centre is closer to: it joins that card's pair, or over a card of its own pair it changes places within the pair. With less overlap the gaps of CMB-009 apply. This wins over divider drops and reordering pairs, in both rows, except a card of the defending player dropped in an attacker row, which blocks that pair's attacker (CMB-031, CMB-034). (r40)

### Attack targets

- **CMB-012** While combat is open, every attackable target always has its own lane in the same attacker/blocker rows: the defending player first, then each face-up planeswalker and battle in battlefield order, including your own Sieges (protected by the opponent). An empty lane is one card slot wide. (r27)
- **CMB-013** Lane headers sit in their own row, never covered, as a pill tinted with the colour of the target's side: the player lane shows the defending player's round avatar and name, card lanes tiny art and name; a hidden target shows only "→ Face-down card". At the header's right end the target's life, loyalty or defense (none for a hidden target); with Automatum on, loyalty and defense are the card's loyalty and defense counters. With Automatum on, a planeswalker or battle that left the battlefield keeps its lane as "→ <name> (gone)" without a value: its attackers stay attacking but attack nothing and deal no combat damage (CR 506.4c), also after Automatum is switched off and on. Names are always shown in full at the title size of the area's cards; lanes widen to fit, with room for the damage preview's "16 → 11" (CMB-029), and a too-wide area pans with the table. (r40)
- **CMB-014** Lane separators are 2dp, fairly dark, scaling with card size, run through the header row, and keep about 2dp of space around cards. (r40)
- **CMB-015** Dropping an attacker into another lane, also an empty one, retargets its whole pair (blockers stay attached); dropping into the blocker row makes it a blocker. (r31)
- **CMB-016** "Attack →" opens one row per target, each with a small image (avatar or art) and the full name on an opaque background; the current target is marked and greyed. The icon is a crosshair. On a declared attacker's own card menu, Attack → replaces Declare and retargets its copy. (r34)

### Declaring and blocking

- **CMB-017** Declare attackers with a selection: "Declare + tap" and "Declare" (without tapping). New attackers go to the right of existing ones; the selection clears after declaring. Without a selection: "Select attackers" ("Select more attackers" once attackers exist). With Automatum on it is CMB-028 instead. (r38)
- **CMB-018** Declare attackers with nobody declared: "No attack" closes the empty combat area and goes to Main 2. With attackers declared: "Assume no blocks" goes to Declare blockers and then to Combat damage as two separate steps, so the opponent can go back to Declare blockers and block. With Automatum on it is CMB-028 instead. (r38)
- **CMB-019** Declare + tap puts the taps first, and the step into Declare blockers joins the attack, so the timeline always stops at Declare blockers for the defender. (r29)
- **CMB-020** The defender at Declare blockers gets "No blocks + next". Blockers are placed by dragging own cards into a pair's blocker row, or onto an attacker (CMB-031). With Automatum on it is CMB-034 instead. (r40)
- **CMB-021** End of combat: "Close + next" moves on to Main 2 and closes the combat area. (r16)

### Combat damage

- **CMB-022** At Combat damage, with Automatum off (or a combat declared by hand), the turn player's only quick action is "Close + next" (closes combat, goes to Main 2): the damage itself is dealt by hand (I-D39); with Automatum on a combat Automatum declared gets its damage from Automatum (CMB-029, CMB-033). (r40)

### Blocks and arrows

- **CMB-031** Every block is an arrow from the blocker to the attacker it blocks, drawn in the gap between the blocker and attacker rows. A blocker sits in the pair of the first attacker it blocks; a blocker blocking several attackers is one card with an arrow to each (no second copy), and an attacker's blockers include those arrows (an attacker blocked only by an arrow counts as blocked). With Automatum off, dropping a card of the defending player (from the Battlefield or a blocker copy) onto an attacker blocks it: a card not blocking yet becomes a blocker in that attacker's pair, a blocker already blocking another attacker gets one more arrow, and dropping a blocker on an attacker it blocks by an arrow removes that arrow. Nothing checks the rules then. History reads "Hill Ogre blocks Ironhide Brute and Stout Bear". (r40)
- **CMB-032** With Automatum on, tapping a combat copy selects it: a violet frame, the copies it fights (the attackers it blocks, or an attacker's blockers) framed in amber and its arrows amber. Tapping the selected copy again opens its Combat menu (CMB-003); tapping the empty table clears the selection. The selection is the viewer's own and lasts only for the combat step it was made in. While blockers are being declared, tapping one of the defending player's creatures picks it as the blocker to place instead (CMB-034). (r40)

### Combat with Automatum

- **CMB-028** With Automatum on, while Automatum waits for attackers the lanes are the attacker's staging: one target lane is active, framed in violet (at first the defending player's; tapping another lane's header makes it active). Tapping one of the attacking player's creatures on the Battlefield stages it as an attacker of the active target (its copy appears in that lane), tapping it (or its copy) again unstages it; a creature that can't attack that target isn't staged and Automatum's reason shows ("Stout Bear can't attack …"). Dragging creatures into the lanes works the same way, only as Automatum allows; a copy dragged back onto the Battlefield leaves the staging. The attacking player's quick actions are "Rest → <target>" (stages every creature that may attack the active target and isn't staged yet; greyed when there is none), "Clear" (unstages all; greyed when nothing is staged) and "Commit attack (N)" ("No attack" with nothing staged), greyed with Automatum's reason while the staging can't be declared. Commit and No attack do what Next step does then (TRN-057): they declare what the lanes hold through Automatum as one step (attacking taps the creatures, vigilance doesn't), and Automatum goes on by itself to the next point where someone could act. Declared lanes show Automatum's combat: a pair per attacker, blockers by their arrows (CMB-031). (r40)
- **CMB-034** With Automatum on, while Automatum waits for blockers, tapping one of the defending player's creatures (on the Battlefield or its blocker copy) selects it as the blocker to place (CMB-032; tapping it again deselects it) and the attackers it may block glow violet; tapping an attacker's copy (or its real card) then blocks it (an arrow), tapping an attacker it already blocks removes that block, and tapping an attacker it can't block shows Automatum's reason; a blocker that can block one attacker tapped onto another moves there. A blocker that blocks one attacker is deselected once it blocks; one that "can block an additional creature" stays selected until it blocks as many as it can (tapping the empty table finishes early). Dragging a blocker onto an attacker does the same as tapping it. The defender's quick actions are "No blocks" (greyed with the reason when some creature must block), "Clear" (removes every block; greyed with none) and "Commit blocks" (greyed with Automatum's reason, e.g. menace needs two blockers, while the blocks can't be declared, and with nothing blocking); No blocks and Commit blocks declare through Automatum like Next step. (r40)
- **CMB-029** With Automatum on, in the declare blockers step (also while a division of combat damage is open, and in the first-strike damage step) both players see on the combat area what combat damage would do if nobody does anything more, with the blocks being arranged and the suggested divisions: a red "−3" badge over the middle of each copy that would take damage, with "☠" beside it when it would die (clear of the card's name), a red "5 → Bob" tag over an attacker dealing damage to its target (unblocked or with trample), "16 → 11" at the right end of the target's header, in place of its value, and by each life square the life change. An attacker's tags sit on its side away from the arrows, after its keyword badges. An eye button just right of the area's top-right corner, below its fold button (CMB-007), hides and shows these numbers (each viewer's own choice; holding it says which). When the combat has first-strike damage, the copies of the creatures that deal the next combat damage have a thin red outline (I-D31). When getting to the damage needs a choice (a trigger's target, a discard, a choice between replacement effects), the preview takes the suggested answer and a red "Depends on a choice" tag sits under the area's bottom-left corner. There is no separate prompt: once blockers are declared the player holding priority gets "Pass" in the quick actions (none while their pause panel, STK-037, shows its own Pass), which passes priority as Next step does, and may cast or activate instead as usual; Automatum deals the combat damage when both have passed. (r40)
- **CMB-033** With Automatum on, a division of combat damage Automatum asks for (the attacker's first, then the defender's, CR 510.1) opens as a dialog for the player who divides (or who chose to choose for them, STK-031), one at a time with "1 of 2" when that step has more: the creature's name, P/T and trample, "N damage to divide among what it fights", a switch "In order" / "Free", a row per creature (and the attacked player or planeswalker with trample, always last) with its card drawn small as on the battlefield (holding it inspects it; a face-down one covered), the damage it gets and "lethal ☠" when that is lethal, a hint line, Inspect (hides the dialog to look at the table; "Divide damage" brings it back) and OK. It opens filled in with Automatum's suggestion, so OK alone accepts it. In order (the default): the creature rows are dragged by their handle into another order, and each creature gets lethal damage in turn, the rest going on to the next (to the player or planeswalker with trample, else to the last creature). Free: − and + by each row; OK is enabled only when all the damage is divided and, with trample, every blocker has lethal damage before any goes past them (CR 702.19b); the hint line says why. A division whose in-order result can't depend on the order (one creature, or enough damage for lethal damage to all of them, counting the other combat damage being assigned in that step) isn't shown while the dividing player has "Skip when I have no choice" on: the device of whoever made the latest event answers it with the suggestion at once, as its own step (the dividing player's device only when that hasn't happened within a few seconds, so only one device answers); with it off the dialog opens for it, in Free. (r40)
- **CMB-035** With Automatum on, a real Battlefield card in combat carries a small chip over its top edge: violet "→ <target>" while staged in the declare attackers step; grey "attacking" or "blocking" otherwise, only on cards wide enough for it (larger card sizes). Attackers' copies show violet badges for the keywords that matter in combat (flying, reach, menace, trample, first strike, double strike, deathtouch, lifelink, vigilance), as Automatum works them out, on their side away from the arrows. A card's chips and badges are stacked one per line, none wider than the card (their text made smaller to fit), so neighbours' never overlap. (r40)

### Putting cards on the Stack

- **STK-001** Every way of putting a card on the Stack (from battlefield, hand, graveyard, exile or library) adds a Stack entry pointing at the real card; the real card stays where it is. Several entries may come from the same card. (r17)
- **STK-002** A hand card's entry is shown face up to both players while it is on the Stack. (r2)
- **STK-003** Stack entries are always upright and can't be tapped. A real card is never in the Stack itself; only entries pointing at it are (STK-001). (r35)
- **STK-004** A hand card double-tapped onto the Stack (ZON-004) that is a non-land permanent on all its faces resolves onto the main battlefield row by itself; play and resolve are one Undo. Not with Automatum on (STK-023). (r37)
- **STK-005** "Face down" in the hand menu puts a face-down entry for the card on the Stack (the opponent sees a back), which resolves by itself like a creature (STK-004): the card goes from the hand onto its owner's battlefield face down (2/2). Play and resolve are one Undo. (r35)
- **STK-006** Adding an entry shows a ghost card flying from the card to the Stack; removing one flies back. An entry leaving to the graveyard, battlefield or exile flies to that destination. Undo reverses the flight. (r14)

### Stack display

- **STK-007** Portrait: the Stack is a fixed-size HUD pinned at the right edge, vertically mid-screen. Landscape: it sits centred in the side area. (r17)
- **STK-008** The Stack area has a ~3dp very dark purple border and a soft shadow (floating look), a fixed 12dp inset, and its count on the top-left corner of the border. Only the top entry is shown, at single-card size. (r14)
- **STK-009** Stack entries can have their own notes and pings, separate from the real card, set from Manage Stack (STK-019). An entry without a note of its own shows the real card's note chip. (r36)

- **STK-011** Bubble above: "Put to battlefield". It removes the entry; a real card not on the battlefield goes to its owner's battlefield, the land row if it is a land, otherwise the main row, at the first free spot. If it is already there, only the entry is removed. (r34)

While Automatum waits for something done by hand they are all there. (r43)
- **STK-015** Normal drags may only pull the top entry. Dragging it to a zone moves the real card and removes the entry; dropping it into the card's current zone only removes the entry. With Automatum on such drags are refused (ZON-077). (r38)

### Text marks

- **STK-016** The bubble toward the centre is a "Mark text" handle: dragging up or down cycles through the card's text options, showing the selected text live; releasing sets one shared mark. Options are the rules-text paragraphs; for split, adventure, double-faced and similar cards the part titles come first. Nothing checks which text is legal. (r1)
- **STK-017** A Stack entry shows all its marks as coloured numbered dots in its corner (8 dark colours), also listed on inspect. Marks clear when the entry leaves the Stack and never show on the original card. Hidden cards offer no text options. (r35)

### Manage Stack

- **STK-018** Double-tapping the Stack (its card or count) opens "Manage Stack · N" with every entry, top first ("Top", "2", …), each with art, name and its marks. (r8)
- **STK-019** Each entry has square icon buttons: Remove, Battlefield, Graveyard (same as the bubbles), Copy (a new entry for the same card on top of the Stack), Change controller (of the entry only; the real card's owner is unchanged), Note and Ping (both on that entry only, not its real card). Battlefield and Graveyard are greyed on copies; Note is greyed on a real card lying on the Stack. (r35)
- **STK-020** Each entry lists its text options as toggle chips, so one entry can hold several marks. (r7)

- **STK-022** Automatum puts an ability whose source isn't on the table (a delayed trigger of a spell copy, an emblem's ability, an ability of a token that's gone) on the Stack as an entry of its own: a card showing the source's name and the ability's text, like an entry whose card has left; history reads "<name> ability → Stack". - **STK-023** With Automatum on, casting a hand card (double-tap or the hand menu's Cast) opens the cast panel when the cast has a real choice: a white panel the full width of the table, docked exactly above your hand (over the Stack too) with a violet top edge: the card small as on the battlefield (a tap opens Inspect; holding it shows it large until released) beside its name (an ability's text for an ability, TBL-115), no rules text, then one section per choice in rules order: how to cast it when Automatum offers several ways (faces, both halves of a fuse card as "Reel // Rend (fused)", which also names the spell, alternative costs, as chips), modes (STK-040), X (− value +), optional costs ("Pay the Kicker cost?" A tap on anything else says "That isn't a legal target." Then the cast goes out, paid by Autotap (STK-024); with Autotap off the panel returns with the mana section. When Automatum leaves nothing to choose but the payment (a land, a spell with no choices) it goes out at once without a panel. Changed choices show without a spinner. The cast goes out as one step (shown at once, STK-042): the card stays where it was with a Stack entry as in STK-001, its sources tapped, and then the caster passes priority by itself (STK-026); a choice the cast causes afterwards (the order or targets of the triggers it sets off) is Automatum's open choice (STK-030). When a Stack entry below the top is a legal target, the Stack glows too: tapping it opens Manage Stack as "Choose a target · N", as tall as its entries, each drawn as its card (STK-030), the legal entries outlined in violet and the others dimmed, without its editing buttons, and the target bar hidden while it is open; a tap on a legal entry picks it and closes it. A mode that may be chosen more than once (a Season's pawprint modes) is added again by each tap and shows "×2" and so on, with a "−" icon beside it taking one back and Clear taking back all; each pawprint mode shows its cost ("{P}{P}") and "Pawprints: N of 5" shows the total, red and OK greyed while it is over the limit. Each mode chip has a mark in front, filled once chosen. (r42)
- **STK-024** With "Autotap mana sources" on (LOB-113, the default) a cast's payment is never asked: Automatum pays with its suggestion, mana already in your pool first, then the cheapest sources, and the cast panel has no mana selection. - **STK-025** An open cast is the caster's alone: nothing reaches the other player until Cast, and Cancel or Back drops it without a trace. ### Automatum: priority and resolution

- **STK-026** With Automatum on, priority is passed for a player by itself whenever the rules leave them nothing to do: their "Skip when I have no choice" is on (SYS-063) and they could only pass, or it is off and they also hold no cards only they can see; and also wherever they could act but have neither a stop nor a pause (STK-036). When both players have passed in a row the top of the Stack resolves through Automatum, and whatever follows happens by itself (state-based actions, triggers going on the Stack) up to the next choice or the next player who could act, all within the same step. So a spell nobody could answer resolves as part of its own cast, and on an empty Stack the steps go on by themselves to the next point where someone can act (TRN-057). (r41)
- **STK-036** With Automatum on, a player who could act is stopped for only at a stop or a pause; everywhere else Automatum passes for them by itself (STK-026). Their stops: their own main phases with an empty Stack, where they act in their own time. Their pauses: a spell or ability of the other player on top of the Stack, the other player's attack (the defending player, in Declare attackers once creatures attack; once per attack: when the attacking player's trigger or spell in that step already gave them the respond panel, the empty Stack after it is no second pause) and blocks (both players, in Declare blockers once creatures attack), and the other player's end step (for "at the end of turn" instants). When nobody could act for a whole round of turns, Automatum goes on to the next stop rather than leaving a player holding priority where they have neither. A turn with an additional combat phase (CR 500.8) gets its second combat, with the same stops and pauses. With Full control (SYS-071) every other step in which they could act and their own spells and abilities are pauses too, and after casting a spell or activating an ability they keep priority instead of passing it. None of this looks at cards only they can see; whether they could act at all still follows Skip when I have no choice (SYS-063). Choices and declarations of attackers and blockers always wait for their player. (r41)
- **STK-037** At your pause on the live table, the respond panel (STK-027), or on an empty Stack a panel docked above the hand (STK-044) "<step>: you may act" (on the other player's attack "Alice attacks: you may act") with an eye, Hold and Pass, shows a thin violet bar that empties over your Response time (LOB-113), with Hold beside Respond. If you do nothing, the game goes on when the bar has emptied: your own device passes for you when the step that made the pause was yours or the other player isn't online (no green dot, TRN-042), otherwise the other player's device does (STK-038). Your device never passes for you while your own cast or ability is open, and passes at most once at a pause (an Undo back to it isn't passed again by itself). The table stays on the paused board for your whole Response time even if the other player's device has already gone on, then moves on to the live game by itself. The eye fades the empty-Stack panel away around itself and brings it back (its time runs on). Like every Automatum panel these sit above your hand (STK-044), so the combat and its damage preview (CMB-029) stay in view. (r42)
- **STK-038** At the other player's pause made by your step, their bar above your hand (STK-044; "Bob may respond to Storm Spark", or "Bob may act · Declare blockers" on an empty Stack) shows the same emptying bar while your device waits for them, then your device passes for them and the game goes on: at once when they aren't online (no green dot, TRN-042) or your "When the opponent can react" is Proceed at once; after your Response time with Wait (the default). It passes at most once at a pause: after an Undo back to it nothing is passed by itself; a pass that couldn't go out (the device was busy or the table changed meanwhile) is tried again, a few times at most. Pass for them (STK-028) still works meanwhile. (r41)
- **STK-039** Hold, Respond (with the pause's bar running or already empty) or beginning a cast or ability at your pause (from the hand or a card menu) keeps you at that point however long you like: timeline mode paused there with a continuation armed (HIS-037), the panel reading "Holding — take your time" (an empty Stack's "<step>: take your time"). What you then do continues from that point: as the next step when the game is still there, as a new branch when the other player's device has already gone on (they see "Opponent branched", HIS-038), also when the game has since gone on to another turn or a choice is open on the line you leave (SYS-062). Let it resolve or Pass there returns to the live game without a branch when the game has already gone on from that point (the other player's device passed for you), and passes as usual when it hasn't; on the live table held on a pause after the game went on, they show the live game. (r41)
- **STK-027** The player holding priority with something on the Stack sees a panel docked above the hand (STK-044): the card on top of the Stack drawn as a card (holding it inspects it) beside "Alice cast Storm Spark" ("You cast …", "… activated …", "… triggered" for abilities), an ability's own line (a spell shows no text: its card is there), "Target:" with its targets (cards drawn as cards, players by name, "You"), "N more below it on the Stack" when there are, the eye, Respond and Let it resolve. A spell cast face down (STK-005) reads "Alice cast a face-down spell", its card covered, to a player who may not see it. Let it resolve passes priority, one step whose history shows the pass. Respond shrinks the panel to a bar above the hand ("Respond — cast a card from your hand", Back, Let it resolve) while the player casts a response the usual way (ZON-004, STK-023). (r42)
- **STK-044** Every Automatum panel sits in one place, like the cast panel (STK-023): the respond panel, pauses, open choices, the bars of the other player's response or choice, early divisions, a hidden division's bar and the target bars are the table's full width (in landscape at most 420 dp from its left edge, clear of the combat area and its preview) with their bottom on the top of your hand row, so your hand is never covered; white with a violet top edge. A panel's bottom row has the eye at its left, always at the same spot (holding it says what it does; it fades the rest of the panel away and brings it back), and its buttons at its right, Hold first. (r42)
- **STK-028** The other player sees a slim bar docked above the hand (STK-044), "Bob may respond to Storm Spark", with Pass for them, which first asks "Pass for Bob?" ("Bob may still want to respond to Storm Spark. Passing for them lets it resolve."). (r37)
- **STK-029** With Automatum on, while Automatum's spell or ability is on the Stack the step arrow reads "Resolve" (TRN-063): a tap, or the phase dialog's Next step, passes priority for whoever holds it as Let it resolve does (STK-027; asked first for the other player, TRN-061).
