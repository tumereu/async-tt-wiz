# Turns

How a game moves through its turns: turn structure, the top bar, whose turn and whose move it is, the bottom bar,
the main button and your settings in the quick actions, the opening and mulligans, life, player chips such as
emblems, the opponent's presence, result suggestions, conceding and how a game ends.

## How it works

Automatum moves each turn through its steps; you move the game on only for yourself, with the main button in the
bottom-right corner, which always says what a tap does ("To combat", "Resolve", "End turn"). Holding it ends your
turn, or resolves the whole Stack when it reads Resolve. The top bar shows whose turn it is and whether it is your move;
the turn player's battlefield is tinted, and both bars take the colour of the player whose move it is. Next to the main
button, the quick actions hold your own settings for this game: how often your device stops for you, when it may assume
the other player's answer, and whether it taps your mana. A game opens with both players taking mulligans at the same
time. Life and other player states (emblems, Monarch, poison and the like) show by each player's life square. A game
ends by the rules, by a concession or by an agreed result one player suggested; both players then see a result dialog,
and nobody goes back to the lobby until both have tapped its OK.

## Turn structure

- **TRN-001** A turn has 12 positions: Untap, Upkeep, Draw, Main 1, Beginning of combat, Declare attackers, Declare blockers, Combat damage, End of combat, Main 2, End step, Cleanup. Automatum moves the turn through them (TRN-057) and adds a First-strike damage step when a combat needs one (TRN-056). (r37)
- **TRN-056** A combat in which a first or double striker fights has two combat damage steps (CR 510.4): First-strike damage (a bolt icon, short name "1st strike"), then Combat damage. The step button shows it like any step; the phase dialog shows its square between Blockers and Damage from when the combat reaches it until the combat ends. (r46)
- **TRN-057** The main button moves the turn on for you only: a tap passes your priority (or declares what the combat lanes hold); holding it is End turn, your device passing your priority until the turn ends, attacking with nothing. The other player's points are assumed (HIS-096), automatic or waited for, never passed for them. Each step's own actions happen by themselves; nothing else changes the step. (r60)
- **TRN-002** Turn numbers count both players' turns, so turn 1 is only the starting player's first turn. From Cleanup the main button (the turn player's reads "End turn", the other player's "To next turn"), or End turn, passes the turn to the other player's Untap. (r33)
- **TRN-052** A turn change Automatum makes names whose turn begins, so an extra turn goes to the same player again (turn number up by one, at Untap); the event that begins it reads "Turn N → <name>" ("Turn N → you" for that player, HIS-091). (r53)
- **TRN-060** When the game comes back to the same position with only passes nobody could avoid in between (CR 104.4b; holding only cards that can't be played then, such as lands while something is on the Stack, doesn't count as being able to act), the player holding priority is asked "The game is in a loop of actions nobody can stop, which makes it a draw (CR 104.4b). End it as a draw, or continue?" with Continue and Draw; Draw makes the game a draw (TRN-065). (r48)
- **TRN-101** Abilities that keep triggering with nobody getting priority stop after 200 rounds with the same choice as the loop draw (Continue and Draw), worded "Possible loop: …". (r48)

## Top bar

- **TRN-005** The top bar's first line reads "Your turn · N" or "<name>'s turn · N" in the turn player's colour; only the name shortens on a narrow screen. (r11)
- **TRN-006** Under it, the move pill: a solid green "Your move" when it is your move (TRN-058), otherwise an outlined "Waiting · <name>", also while catching up (HIS-094). Tapping it does nothing: the move isn't passed by hand. (r53)
- **TRN-007** In the phone layout the step button in the top bar is a square in the current phase's colour with the step's icon and its short name, as in the phase dialog (TRN-008), with no border colour or turn number. It flashes on a step change and opens the phase dialog. (r35)
- **TRN-008** The phase dialog is a real dialog titled with the step and its phase ("Combat · Declare attackers"), then "Your turn" / "<name>'s turn"; phase labels sit above rows of step squares, the current one outlined in the turn player's colour. Square names fit on one line on a phone: Untap, Upkeep, Draw · Main 1 · Begin, Attackers, Blockers, Damage, End · Main 2 · End, Cleanup ("1st strike" while a combat has it, TRN-056). (r37)
- **TRN-009** The phase dialog only shows where the turn is: its step squares do nothing and it has no buttons; the game moves on only with the main button (TRN-063). (r47)
- **TRN-010** The top bar's Table actions button opens a dialog with Timeline (the only way into timeline mode, HIS-004), Suggest game end (usable whenever you are at the table, your move or not; TRN-162 while a suggestion waits), Concede (TRN-064), Send problem report (Settings' "Report a problem" with its optional description, sent without leaving the game) and Game settings (LOB-135), each an icon and its name. Nothing in it changes the board by hand (TBL-119). (r58)

## Whose turn and whose move

- **TRN-012** Whose TURN shows on the battlefields: only the turn player's battlefield is tinted (your own green-ish, the opponent's red-ish, the land band a little darker); the other battlefield is neutral grey. (r11)
- **TRN-013** Whose MOVE shows on the bars: the top and bottom bars take the light colour of the player whose move it is (TRN-058), never part of an event. In timeline mode both bars are grey. (r50)
- **TRN-058** The move is the player the game really waits for (the record "Your move" notifications use, LOB-073). While one player plays on assumptions about the other, the acting player has it, even while the other's playback stops at an assumption they may answer. The move passing to you has a cue (TRN-075). (r61)
- **TRN-104** An open undo request (HIS-100) or result suggestion (TRN-047) is the answerer's move; a result the rules gave is the move of whoever hasn't pressed OK on it (TRN-065). When nobody is waited for, the game list says "Move not available". (r61)
- **TRN-105** The move changes only where the game really waits: at a point on the way, where the next step follows by itself (a pass your or their own mode makes, an assumption your phone makes, the other phone's events still arriving one at a time), it stays as it was. (r61)
- **TRN-075** Your move cue: when the move passes to you from the other player (you may act again and their real input came since, or playback stops at their assumption about you, TRN-063), your phone gives one haptic tick, plays your "Your move" sound (LOB-146; none at Off) and the main button pulses once in your colour, once per passing, not repeating. (r64)
- **TRN-103** The move cue never comes for your own chain of steps; in playback only when you actually stop at a decision (not a stop your own pause made); not when you've just acted yourself (their answer within 1.5 s of your own action), not in timeline mode or on opening a game. While a play, a combat or a reveal holds your view, it comes when that is done. (r64)
- **TRN-074** Turn banner: at a turn change "Your turn" or "<name>'s turn" shows at the screen's centre in the turn player's colour and fades out in about 1.2 s, taking no touches, holding nothing and waiting for nothing but a play shown before it (HIS-112), live and in catch-up playback. Never in timeline mode, a take-back or on opening a game. (r61)
- **TRN-102** The turn banner and the plays shown never overlap: a play that went on the Stack before the turn change shows first, one after it waits for the banner. (r61)

## Bottom bar

- **TRN-015** The bottom bar shows your toggles (TRN-059) on either player's turn and the main button with what it holds now (TRN-063, TRN-004); where it holds nothing its place shows the quiet waiting status while you wait on the other player (TRN-004) and otherwise stays empty, in your own turn too. During your opening your quick actions are Mulligan and Keep (TRN-062). (r60)
- **TRN-016** Quick actions are equal-width buttons in the popup style (icon plus small black label, 48dp tall) sharing the width left of the main button (TRN-017) equally, with press feedback. A greyed quick action stays in place. (r56)

## The main button

- **TRN-017** The main button sits in the bottom-right corner, arrow-shaped, 112 dp wide whatever quick actions show beside it (TRN-016; under them in the desktop action area, two thirds of the row beside Undo, WEB-018), with an icon above the words of what a tap does (TRN-063). (r74)
- **TRN-106** A tap of the main button does what it says; holding it is End turn, with a small haptic tick, in every response mode, except when it holds Resolve or an Assume (TRN-107, TRN-109). (r74)
- **TRN-107** Holding Resolve (STK-029) or a Stack wait's "Assume resolves" (TRN-070) resolves the whole Stack: your phone presses Resolve at each of your priority points and Assume at each of the other player's (where the information rule allows it, TRN-071), whatever your response mode, until the Stack is empty, a decision of yours, a reveal stop (HIS-110), a greyed Assume, your own action, or the other player's real input arriving. (r74)
- **TRN-108** The other player's real input is never passed over by a held Resolve: if it reaches the server before the chain's next press, that press isn't made and you get priority with it on top (where your response mode decides, as always). (r74)
- **TRN-109** Holding an Assume with nothing on the Stack ("Assume pass", "Assume no blockers", "Assume blockers") is its tap; holding a greyed Assume says why. (r74)
- **TRN-063** The main button holds whatever goes on now. It says under an icon what a tap does, with room for two lines (wrapped between words; drawn smaller rather than cut where two lines don't fit), an icon per kind. When the move passes to you it pulses once in your colour (TRN-075). (r76)
- **TRN-110** The main button tells an assumption from your own action at a glance: your own actions (every name below, and Cancel request, TRN-004) are white with a grey outline and dark teal words; an assumption, the wait's Assume (TRN-070), is filled with the Assume violet (the Assume setting's, TRN-059) with white words under the Assume setting's icon (⏩); greyed is greyed whichever it is. (r76)
- **TRN-111** Main-button names for moving on in your turn: "To combat" (crossed swords), "End turn" (a turn arrow; not stopping at your own main phases, TRN-057; in your own second main phase only in Normal and Quick, which don't stop at your end step), "To end step" (a moon; in Control, in your own second main phase: a tap goes on to your end step, where Control stops; at your end step it reads "End turn"). (r76)
- **TRN-112** A pass with the Stack empty names where it goes, the next step in turn order: "To upkeep", "To draw step", "To Main 1", "To attackers", "To blockers" ("To end of combat" when nothing attacks), "To damage", "To end of combat", "To Main 2", "To end step", "To next turn" (an arrow in your own turn, a skip mark in the other player's). (r76)
- **TRN-113** In combat and with the Stack, the main button reads "Attack (2)" (crossed swords) / "No attack" (a struck circle), "Block (1)" (a shield) / "No blocks" (a struck shield), "Resolve" (a play mark) with something on the Stack (STK-029), and "OK" (a tick) while your division of combat damage is open (CMB-033); while the other player must act it holds the wait's Assume (TRN-070). (r76)
- **TRN-114** While a panel or bar of yours is open, its go-on action is the main button instead of a button of its own, saying what it does: the cast panel's "Cast" ("Activate", "Play", STK-052), a target bar's "Done" or "None" (STK-047), a choice's "OK" (the legend rule's "Keep", STK-030), "Pay" (STK-049), the Keep / Bottom screen's "OK" (TRN-062) and the reveal panel's "OK" (HIS-110); it is greyed while that can't be done yet (a tap shakes it and holding it says why, "Finish the choice first."). (r76)
- **TRN-115** Panels that keep their own buttons: a choice answered by the button tapped (Yes / No, a player, a colour, an option, "Don't pay"), the game's result dialog (TRN-065: it also shows over the lobby, where there is no main button) and the dialogs over the screen (undo requests, rewinds, conceding, choosing for the other player). Nothing sits under the Stack. (r76)
- **TRN-116** The main button is greyed when it can't act (your cast or another choice open with nothing for it to do, such as a single target to tap, the opening, a declaration Automatum doesn't allow, a division not yet complete): a tap shakes it and holding it says why. (r76)
- **TRN-117** A second tap within 0.3 s of a press the main button took is ignored, whatever the button says by then (a quick double tap on Cast casts once and never also passes). (r76)
- **TRN-118** When the result of your own action (a tap or hold of the main button, or a card tap that makes an action) hasn't shown 0.15 s after the press, a soft violet band sweeps across the main button with a thin violet line along its bottom (white over an Assume) until it shows, at least 0.3 s; a quicker result looks as before, and playback never shows it. (r76)
- **TRN-119** The main button never shows a press your phone is about to make itself: while a play shown or a combat's hold (HIS-112, HIS-113) only holds back a step your phone then makes by itself (your pass by your response mode, an End turn or a Resolve chain going on), it holds nothing meanwhile instead of flashing "Resolve", and after that step it shows what you actually have to press; likewise on your own action's board shown at once (SYS-025) when your phone passes for you right after it by itself (your own cast in Normal or Quick), and on the board after your Assume or Resolve when your phone's own step comes next (Quick passing over your own ability below), until that step is made. (r76)

## Your settings in the quick actions

- **TRN-059** The quick-actions row holds your own settings for this game, on either player's turn. In live play: the response mode (a tap goes on to the next of Control, Normal and Quick; its name shows), "Assume resolves" (a tap goes on to the next of Never, Normal, When they have no mana and Always, HIS-096; it reads "Assume: Never", "Assume: Normal", "Assume: No mana" or "Assume: Always") and "Autotap mana". (r57)
- **TRN-120** The response mode and Assume resolves cells always fill by how much control you keep: white words and icon on deep blue for Control / Assume: Never, violet for Normal / Assume: Normal, amber for Quick / Assume: No mana, red-orange for Assume: Always. (r57)
- **TRN-121** In catch-up playback (HIS-094) the same three cells stay in the same places: the response mode as live play's (its name, colour and tap; playback stops where it stops, HIS-095), "Autotap mana", and Assume resolves greyed (your phone assumes nothing while you catch up; a tap shakes it, holding it says why), so the row never changes between two and three cells. (r57)
- **TRN-122** The settings are equal-width cells with an icon and words; a toggle on is filled violet with a check, off white with an empty ring; holding one says what it does. Other quick actions: your opening's (TRN-062) and combat staging's (CMB-048, CMB-034). (r57)
- **TRN-123** Only you see or change your settings (the other player's phone learns your response mode, STK-036, and your "Assume resolves", never shown on their screen: so it shows its own player's move only where your phone really waits for them, TRN-058). (r57)

## The opening and mulligans

- **TRN-027** A game begins with both players "Mulliganing" at the same time (TRN-062); keeping makes you Ready. Each player's mulligans are their own: every declaration is taken as soon as it is made, in the order the players make them, not in rounds. While both openings are open, each player's own mulligan is their move (LOB-022, LOB-072). (r76)
- **TRN-124** Once the starting player has kept, they may press "Assume keep" (TRN-070, HIS-096) and play turn 1 at once while the other player still decides; each opening move of the other player's after that makes the turn again on top of it, standing as far as it comes out the same (HIS-115, HIS-116), and the other player sees none of it before keeping (HIS-117). (r76)
- **TRN-029** There is no Turn 0: both players mulligan during turn 1, while their opening status is set. (r25)
- **TRN-062** The opening follows the game's rules (opening hand 7, the first mulligan free, London mulligans, CR 103.5), both players declaring at once. While you are "Mulliganing" your quick actions are "Mulligan" (TRN-125) and "Keep" (keeps the hand as it is); nothing else asks about it or covers the board. (r76)
- **TRN-125** "Mulligan" is taken at once, without waiting for the other player's declaration: your hand goes back, your library is shuffled and a new opening hand is drawn. From the second mulligan on, the Keep / Bottom screen (STK-043) then opens at once for the cards it puts on the bottom, one more each time, and Mulligan and Keep are greyed until its OK, the main button (TRN-063), "Put the card on the bottom first." (r76)

## Life and presence

- **TRN-030** Each player starts at 20 life. Tapping a life square opens a floating Life panel with that player's life (only the rules change it) and, for the other player, their presence line (TRN-043), for you the quick messages and Write (LOB-139); its life row sits on the panel edge facing the square (the panel opens under the opponent's square and above your own, in landscape too). (r59)
- **TRN-034** Portrait: each player's row shows the life square and packed piles, the other player's also their hand's fan (ZON-001); the room beside them stays free. Your row has no margin above your life square and no room under it. (r64)
- **TRN-035** Landscape (phone layout): each player's life square and piles sit in their bar row (TBL-009), the opponent's in the top bar and yours in the bottom bar, their emblems right after them. (r57)
- **TRN-042** The opponent's life square has a small dot in its corner: green when they have this game open and touched it in the last 2 minutes, yellow when open but idle, grey when away. It is hidden while you are disconnected. (r29)
- **TRN-043** The opponent's Life panel has an info line with the same dot: "Online", "Idle 7 min", "Last seen 12 min ago", "Last seen 3 h ago", "Last seen Oct 27" or "Not seen yet". (r29)

## Player chips: states, emblems, dungeons

- **TRN-072** What the rules keep for a player and show nowhere else shows as round chips like the emblems' (TRN-053: the same size and violet edge, a pale violet fill), in the same row, before the emblems: Monarch, Initiative, City's blessing, Enduring story, speed, the Ring's level, the player's counters (poison, energy, …), dungeons completed, extra turns and skipped turns. Both players see the same. (r74)
- **TRN-130** Each state chip shows its state's own icon in dark violet, with its current level under it where it has one: a crown for Monarch, a compass for Initiative, a castle for City's blessing, an open book for an enduring story, a speedometer with the speed, a ring with the Ring's level (of 4), a drop with the poison, a bolt with the energy, stars with the experience, the radiation sign with the rad counters, a trophy with the dungeons completed, a redo arrow with the extra turns and a skip arrow with the turns skipped. (r74)
- **TRN-131** Any other player counter's chip shows its first letter and count, or its first two letters where an earlier counter's chip shows that letter ("S1" shield, then "St2"). (r74)
- **TRN-132** State chips stay in the player's row on any width, never over the hand: where they don't all fit, the last reads "+N" (TRN-128). (r74)
- **TRN-133** A tap on a state chip does nothing; holding one shows what it means in the dark rule popup (TBL-132, placed as SYS-045), with its words first where they say more than its name ("Speed 2", "Ring 2/4"), a "+N" what all it stands for, until the finger lifts. (r74)
- **SYS-066** An emblem Automatum makes (CR 114) is kept in the game with whose it is and its text, for its chip by the life square (TRN-053). (r36)
- **TRN-053** A player's emblems show as small round chips (24 dp) with a violet edge in their row of round chips by the life square, after their dungeon's room and their states (TRN-069, TRN-072), oldest first: each with the emblem's art from the card records (the emblem printing with the same rules text), or an emblem icon when there is none; beyond three, the fourth chip reads "+N" for the rest. (r62)
- **TRN-126** The row's round chips are all one size and never overlap. In portrait the row sits at the right of the free room right of the player's piles, inside the player's row and clear of the hands on any width, over two lines when one isn't enough (that room is always at least 96 dp while there are chips: where the piles don't leave it, they scroll under it). (r62)
- **TRN-127** In landscape the row of round chips sits right after the player's piles in their bar row (TBL-009), over two lines only where the bar is tall enough, clear of the hands and of your quick actions (your row gives way to your hand in the bottom bar, TBL-009). (r62)
- **TRN-128** Where the round chips don't fit, they shrink (to 20 dp at least) and then fewer show: the states' last chip reads "+N" for the states left out, and the emblems' last "+N" for theirs, the emblems keeping at least half the chips. (r62)
- **TRN-129** A tap on an emblem chip does nothing; holding any of them inspects all that player's emblems (TBL-104) until the finger lifts: one dark panel each, outlined in its owner's colour, with the emblem's art (or the icon), "Emblem" and its full text. (r62)
- **TRN-073** Day or night (CR 731) belongs to the game: a round chip like TRN-072's, a sun for day or a moon for night, right-aligned just above the Stack, in both orientations, while it is one of them. Holding it says what it means. (r62)
- **TRN-069** A player's dungeon (LOB-117) shows as a small card in a fourth place after Library, Graveyard and Exile (in landscape too, in the bar row, TBL-009), and the room its marker is on as a round chip with a door, first in the player's row of round chips (TRN-072), whose hold names it ("Room: <room>"). Tapping the dungeon does nothing, holding inspects it, and it can't be dragged. Dungeons to choose from show as cards; a room's ability on the Stack shows its dungeon. (r62)

## Result suggestions

- **TRN-046** Suggest game end opens a chooser My win / Draw game, then a confirm dialog ("Suggest my win?" or "Suggest draw?") with an optional one-line "Reason", at most 200 characters (placeholder "Infinite combo"); both players see the reason in quotes in the suggestion panel (TRN-047). (r45)
- **TRN-047** A waiting suggestion shows as a panel on the table, not a dialog: "<name> proposed their win." (or "a draw"; "You proposed your win." for the author); made at the newest event. The opponent gets Decline and Accept, greyed at first as an ending's OK is (TRN-136); the author Withdraw. (r60)
- **TRN-134** Accepting a suggestion makes an agreed result, which ends the game at once: the player accepting waits at the table for the author's OK on the result dialog (TRN-065). (r60)
- **TRN-048** Decline and Withdraw are their own events, not undos. (r18)
- **TRN-051** The suggestion panel appears once the board shown to you includes its event (in catch-up playback when it is reached, TRN-049), and its buttons work from then on. (r60)
- **TRN-049** While a suggestion waits, every other game action is blocked for a player once the board shown to them includes it; chat, timeline browsing, holding to inspect and Table actions (HIS-020) still work. (r48)
- **HIS-020** A pending win/loss/draw suggestion never blocks Table actions: it still opens with every entry usable for either player (Timeline, Suggest game end, Concede, Send problem report, Game settings). It locks play only once the board shown to you (your local head) includes it (TRN-049). (r55)
- **TRN-162** While a suggestion waits, Suggest game end makes a new suggestion that replaces the waiting one, whoever made it: the author changes theirs, the other player answers with their own. (r55)
- **TRN-050** A suggestion left behind on a discarded line (an earlier real answer of the other player's branched, HIS-097) no longer locks anything. (r48)

## Conceding and endings

- **TRN-064** Concede (a flag icon) in Table actions asks "Concede?" ("You lose this game at once.", Cancel / Concede in red); Concede ends the game at once as your loss (CR 104.3a), also while a suggestion waits: you wait at the table for the other player's OK on the result dialog (TRN-065). If the other player's latest actions haven't reached you yet, the game first branches at your local head, so the result never depends on what you haven't seen. (r48)
- **TRN-065** Every ending (a loss by the rules, a draw, the loop draw of TRN-060, a concession, TRN-064, an accepted suggestion, TRN-047) shows each player a dialog in the middle of the screen once the end is shown to them: big "You won", "You lost" or "Draw", under it how it ended (TRN-135), the eye at its bottom left (hides the dialog to look at what is behind it; only the eye stays, at the same spot) and OK at its right. (r49)
- **TRN-135** The result dialog says how the game ended: "Bob's life reached 0", "You drew from an empty library", "Bob got 10 poison counters", "<card>'s effect", "Bob conceded", "You conceded", "Agreed: <reason>" ("Agreed" without one), "Both players lost at once", "An endless loop". (r49)
- **TRN-136** The result dialog's OK always works, but is greyed for the dialog's first 2 seconds, so a misplaced tap can't accept it. (r49)
- **TRN-137** A result the rules gave is final only with both players' OKs, whatever the other player has seen: after your OK you stay at the table, the dialog says "Waiting for <name> to acknowledge" and Take back OK takes OK's place, working until their OK comes; it can't be declined, and Undo (HIS-084) takes the step back until it is final, with the usual approval, and every OK goes with it. (r49)
- **TRN-138** Both OKs make a result final and recorded (LOB-126), the player who pressed first is notified (LOB-111), and only then does each go to the lobby (LOB-046). The game waits for whoever hasn't pressed theirs (TRN-058), with reminders (LOB-075, LOB-076); nothing is recorded until they do, and a player who never does leaves it to the cleanup (LOB-133). (r49)
- **TRN-139** Your OK and its take-back show on your other devices and to the other player at once, and an OK is never played back. (r49)
- **TRN-140** A concession or an accepted suggestion is final and recorded at once and can't be undone; the player whose own action ended the game (the conceder, the player who accepted the suggestion) has given their OK with it. (r49)
- **TRN-141** Whatever the ending, nobody goes to the lobby until both players have pressed OK: after a concession or an accepted suggestion the player whose action ended it stays at the table with the dialog saying "Waiting for <name> to acknowledge" (no button), and the other player stays with the dialog until they tap OK (whenever and on whichever device they open the game), never closed for them in the background; both then go to the lobby. (r49)
- **TRN-142** Either player can leave for other screens as usual; once the game was closed, opening it shows the same dialog over the lobby, which takes nothing until both OKs stand (its Cancel game is gone; behind the dialog, the status line says "<name> hasn't seen the last game's result yet" and Ready is greyed, "Tap OK on the last game's result first."). (r49)
- **TRN-143** Until both OKs stand, the server refuses every lobby action of that game (decks, Ready, starting player, Show deck, settings, Cancel game: "Both players tap OK on the last game's result first."). The next game starts only once both have pressed OK (the server keeps each player's OK). (r49)
