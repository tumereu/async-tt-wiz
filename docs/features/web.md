# Web client

The app in a browser: addresses and Back, loading and updates, sign-in and sound, the table in a browser, the phone and
desktop layouts, the desktop table, the mouse and keys, desktop screens, drafts and card lists, notifications, installing
and the tab's title.

## How it works

The web client is the app in a browser, served by the ATW server beside its API (one address for both). Every other
entry of the catalog applies to the web client too, unless it is marked "(Android)" or an entry here says otherwise.
Each screen has its own address, so refreshing a page and the browser's Back and Forward work as on any web page. The app
draws in one of two layouts: the phone layout is the Android app in a browser; the desktop layout arranges the same
parts for a large window, a mouse and a keyboard, with the game table spread over the window, the chat in a dock at the
right and hotkeys you can change. Card records and your sign-in are kept in the browser. Notifications come through the
browser's push service once you turn them on, and the site can be installed as an app, though there is no offline play.

## Addresses and Back

- **WEB-001** Every screen has its own address: `/` the menu, `/games`, `/games/<id>` (a game), `/pods`, `/pods/<id>` (a pod, on the tab it opens on, DRF-077), `/pods/<id>/<tab>` (draft, build, stats or games), `/decks`, `/decks/import`, `/decks/<id>`, `/decks/<id>/edit`, `/cubes/new`, `/cubes/<id>`, `/contacts`, `/settings` and `/stats`. An address of no screen, loaded directly, gets the server's "Not found" page (404); one reached inside the open app (through the browser's history) opens the menu. (r71)
- **WEB-100** Refreshing a page opens the same screen, and an address opened while signed out opens once you have signed in. (r71)
- **WEB-002** The browser's Back and Forward move between screens, never into dialogs or menus. Opening a screen adds a history entry; returning to the screen you came from (an on-screen Back) goes back an entry instead of adding one, so Back never loops; switching a pod's tabs keeps one entry. Back from a game leaves the table at once, straight to the screen before it, without SYS-050's second Back. (r71)
- **WEB-003** Every screen but the menu has a back arrow at the left of its header: it does what Android's Back does there (closes an open panel or the side menu first, steps back inside a section, such as Games → Choose opponent), and on a screen with nothing to step back from opens the screen it belongs to (a game's Games, a section's menu). (r71)
- **WEB-004** Esc closes the open dialog, popup, panel or side menu, never a screen, except on a game's table and the draft: there Esc with nothing open leaves the screen, as Back does (a game's table also closes the chat and leaves timeline mode first). On Android Chrome the phone's Back closes them too (where the browser supports it); with none open it is the browser's Back. (r72)

## Loading and updates

- **WEB-005** The first visit shows a loading screen (the wizard hat, "ATW", "Loading Async Tabletop Wizards…" and a moving bar) until the app draws; later visits load from the browser's cache. When the app can't load it says "ATW couldn't load. Check your connection and reload the page." (r71)
- **WEB-006** A tab left open on an older version reloads itself when the server has a newer one (checked when the page opens, when it comes back to the foreground and every 10 minutes): at once on the menu, the Games list or the sign-in page, or as a game is opened; never while a dialog, panel or menu is open or text is being typed. Elsewhere it waits until you reach one of those. (r71)
- **WEB-065** After a tab has reloaded itself for a newer version, a short note says "ATW was updated", and What's new (LOB-087) follows its usual rules; a first visit counts as a fresh install. (r71)
- **WEB-060** Several ATW tabs may be open in one browser, also on the same game: an action made in any of them reaches the server and the other player. A tab never goes into "Reconnecting…" because another tab of the same browser opened a game (the browser is one device to the server, and a tab that acts takes the game's connection back on its socket at once, SYS-014). (r75)
- **WEB-056** The web client runs in desktop Chrome as in other browsers, also as a game's table opens: none of its code is too big for Chrome's optimizing compiler, which would end the page with "Aw, Snap! Out of memory" once that code got busy (the build checks it). (r74)
- **WEB-066** When the browser ends a visit anyway (a crash, out of memory, a background tab it discarded) or the app can't start, the next visit's app log, and so a problem report sent then, says so with that visit's last log lines. (r74)
- **WEB-057** While a game's table or the draft shows in a visible tab, the app log (and so a problem report) records how smoothly it draws, whether or not reports are on, beginning as it opens with the window's size and the graphics the browser draws with. (r74)
- **WEB-101** In the drawing log only what the page draws counts: a frame is a picture the page drew, lasting until its next one while it keeps drawing or something animates or is dragged; while nothing changes (and while the tab is hidden) nothing is counted, so pauses are never slow frames. (r74)
- **WEB-067** For each minute of drawing that had slow frames (five or more frames that missed a display refresh, or one of 100 ms or more), the drawing log gets a line with its frame rate, its frame times (median, 95th and 99th percentile, worst) and its slow frames by what was animating or being dragged and why they were slow (the page's drawing, its other work such as the engine's messages, display refreshes it didn't draw while animating, or the browser and graphics card). (r74)
- **WEB-068** Each minute's line also says how long the page's own drawing of a frame took (average and longest) and gives its three worst frames with what was on screen then (an open popup, timeline mode, cards in view, the last action, the milliseconds of drawing and other work). As the table or draft closes, or a problem report is sent from it, the log gets the same for the whole visit with its five worst frames. (r74)

## Sign-in and sound

- **WEB-009** The card records (LOB-091) download into the browser's own storage, which is asked to keep them (persistent storage), and are used from there on later visits; if the browser deletes them (iOS Safari does after 7 days unvisited unless the site is installed), they download again. A browser that can't keep them (its storage full or refused, as in some private windows) uses them for that visit, and Settings → Card records says under the installed line "This browser couldn't keep them, so they download again on your next visit"; pictures it can't keep are still shown and download again next time. Sign-in is kept in the browser too, across reloads, until you log out. (r78)
- **WEB-008** The "Your move" sound (LOB-146, TRN-075) plays in the browser, also in a background tab, once you have tapped or typed on the page after opening it (browsers allow sound only then). (r71)
- **WEB-010** "Import from link" (LOB-061) reads Archidekt decks through the ATW server, since Archidekt answers no browser directly. (r71)
- **WEB-064** Emojis show in colour in the browser wherever players' own words show (chat messages and the chat's draft, names, notes), typed, pasted or picked from the system's emoji picker, and are sent whole, so the other player sees them as written. (r76)
- **WEB-069** The emoji font comes once the app shows (a few seconds on a first visit after an update, at once later); until it has come, emojis other than the pick mark's 🔥 show as empty boxes. The symbols the app itself draws (the cards' type glyphs, the hourglass, the timeline's symbols) keep their plain look. (r76)

## The table in a browser

- **WEB-007** A game opens in the browser as in the app: a started game's table with the hand, panels, combat band, timeline and chat, also from its address (a refresh opens it again); games still being set up open their lobby, and pods their draft, build, stats and games. (r72)
- **WEB-070** In the browser the game itself (your actions and their outcomes, passes, End turn, the other player's moves), what you can play from your hand and with your cards, a raised hand card's cost chips, and the steps of a cast, of a choice or payment while something resolves and of combat (what may attack or block, its costs and damage) are worked out beside the table, so it stays responsive meanwhile and the hand, menus and panels show them as they arrive. (r72)
- **WEB-011** On a game's table and the draft the browser's own gestures stay off: the page doesn't scroll, bounce or pull to refresh, a pinch, double tap, Ctrl+wheel or Ctrl with + or - doesn't zoom the page, and a long press or right-click shows no browser menu, callout or text selection; the table's own pan, pinch, hold and double tap get every touch. Other screens keep the browser's usual behaviour. (r72)
- **WEB-012** The table's haptic ticks (the move passing to you, TRN-075; a held quick action) vibrate the phone where the browser can (Android's Chrome), once the page has had a tap; elsewhere (iOS, desktops) there is none, and nothing fails. (r72)
- **WEB-058** With a mouse, in either layout, the pointer turns into the hand over everything that can be clicked (every control, every card, the table's cards drawn on its canvas too) and into the text cursor over a text field, as web pages show them; elsewhere it stays the plain arrow. (r73)
- **WEB-059** The page's hidden accessibility structure (what screen readers read and the hover pointer goes by, WEB-058) follows what shows: a menu, popup or dialog that closes leaves it at once, its controls with it. (r74)

## Layouts

- **WEB-013** The app draws in one of two layouts: the phone layout (the Android app as it is, portrait or landscape by the window's shape) or the desktop layout (the desktop table, WEB-015). Automatic chooses the desktop layout when the window is at least 1024 × 600 and the main pointer can hover (a mouse or trackpad), otherwise the phone layout, following the window as it changes. The Android app always draws the phone layout. (r73)
- **WEB-014** Settings → Table → "Layout" (in a browser only): Automatic (the default), Phone or Desktop, each with what it means; kept in that browser and applied at once. (r73)

## Mouse and keys

- **WEB-023** On the desktop table the left mouse button is the finger: a click acts at once as a tap does (TBL-041: it opens a card's menu, activates or raises a hand card), a double click is a double tap (TBL-042's timing), and a drag drags a card or pans the table as a finger's drag does; the middle button's drag pans the table from anywhere. The right button never acts on the table or inspects. A finger on a touchscreen works as on the phone. (r73)
- **WEB-026** Resting the pointer on a card for a quarter of a second shows its quick look: its large picture (each face it may be seen with) beside the card (above a card of your hand's fan, clear of the fan and its chips, WEB-015), without dimming anything; it goes when the pointer leaves the card or a button is pressed. Card backs have none. (r73)
- **WEB-027** Hovering a hand card you may use now shows its chips (ZON-080) faded above it where it lies in the fan (it doesn't rise, WEB-015), and the table outlines in amber what Autotap would tap for its regular way; the chip the pointer is on turns solid and its own sources are outlined. A click on a chip casts the card that way at once; a click on the card raises it as a tap does. (r73)
- **WEB-106** Hovering your battlefield card with an ability usable now shows its ability chips (TBL-124) faded the same way, also when it has just that one; a click on a chip activates that ability. The chips stay while the pointer moves from the card onto them. (r73)
- **WEB-028** A right-click on a control does what holding it does: the main button's hold (End turn, or Resolve the whole Stack, TRN-017), Undo's Undo dialog, a quick action's hold; one without a hold action shows its tooltip. Resting the pointer on a control for half a second shows its tooltip. In a draft's pick a right-click burns a card (on a burned one: unmarks it), as a swipe to the left does (DRF-058). (r73)
- **WEB-029** The mouse wheel zooms the battlefields around the pointer, within the battlefield's zoom limits (TBL-010); Shift with the wheel, or a sideways scroll, pans them sideways. A double click on the empty battlefield fits the table (TBL-012). (r73)
- **WEB-024** Holding Alt with the pointer over a card (Shift works the same, for browsers whose Alt menu gets in the way) inspects it while held, until it is let go: the table dims and the card's pictures show with its changes beside them, as pinned Inspect lays them out (TBL-131). There is no pinned Inspect by a tap of Alt (owner playtest 2026-11-15). (r74)
- **WEB-107** In an Alt Inspect each change's source line ("From Ember Blade") and, under a line naming a keyword or a card's state, its explanation (TBL-132) are always shown, since nothing clicks a change here (owner 2026-11-15). (r74)
- **WEB-085** An Alt Inspect of several cards (a Stack entry with what it names, a stack of permanents, TBL-111, any other) lists them one under another, each card a little less tall than the window so the next one shows, and the mouse wheel scrolls them up and down while Alt (or Shift) is held; the wheel with Alt or Shift never moves the browser back or forward or scrolls the page. (r74)
- **WEB-086** Alt (or Shift) inspects the same way over the play shown (HIS-112), and in a draft's pick over a pack card (its pictures as holding it shows them). Shift pressed together with another key is that key's hotkey (Shift+Space, WEB-031), not Inspect: anything it opened closes at once. Alt and Shift don't inspect while a text field has the keyboard. (r74)
- **WEB-025** Holding Alt anywhere on the desktop table is the finger held down: while held it pauses a play shown, a Stack entry's arrows growing, a combat's hold and playback (HIS-021, HIS-112, STK-058, HIS-113), with the pause icon. The left button held still on the table pauses them too, but never inspects a card. (r74)
- **WEB-087** A key-up the page never gets doesn't leave the pause on: Alt (or Shift) is let go when the browser window loses the focus or the page is hidden, when a text field takes the keyboard, and when a later key or a mouse press, move or release comes without it held (so a click, or pressing and letting go of Alt again, always ends a pause left on); the left button held is let go when the window loses the focus. The app log names each such release and why. (r74)
- **WEB-031** On the desktop table, hotkeys (each rebindable, WEB-035; the defaults here): Space does what a tap on the main button does (To …, Resolve, Attack, Cast, Done, OK, Assume …, Keep) and Shift+Space what holding it does (End turn; on Resolve or an Assume, the whole Stack); Ctrl+Z (⌘Z on a Mac) is a tap on Undo. - **WEB-108** More desktop table hotkeys (rebindable, defaults here): F zooms to fit (TBL-012); S opens the Stack list (STK-018); G and X open your graveyard and exile, Shift+G and Shift+X the other player's (ZON-033's search view); M goes to the next response mode as the quick actions' mode cell does (TRN-059). - **WEB-088** On the desktop table holding Ctrl (by default) keeps the response mode Control, as the mode cell shows, until it is let go, and then puts back the mode you had (a mode chosen meanwhile is kept): it acts once Ctrl has been held alone a quarter of a second, so Ctrl pressed with another key is that key's hotkey (Ctrl+Z), never Control mode (owner playtest 2026-11-15). - **WEB-091** The "?" list shows every hotkey with your key for it ("No key" for one you left without) by where it acts (Table, Timeline mode, Pod screens, Draft pick, Deck and cube editor), the fixed keys (Esc, Enter, 1–9, Alt, ?, and WEB-046's with WEB-045's Shift+click under "Build, Prepare and the editor, the card under the pointer") and what the mouse does. ## Notifications

- **WEB-050** Settings → "This browser" (in a browser only) → "Browser notifications", off by default: turning it on shows the browser's own permission prompt, and once allowed this browser gets notifications. Turning it off stops them. The switch belongs to the browser: whoever signs in there gets them. The site never asks for permission by itself. (r73)
- **WEB-098** When the browser's permission for Browser notifications is refused, the switch goes back off with "Blocked in your browser's site settings" (dismissing the prompt just leaves it off); in a browser that can't show them it says "This browser can't show notifications", and on a server that doesn't send them "Notifications aren't set up on this server". - **WEB-051** While Browser notifications is on, three switches under it choose the kinds, each on by default: Invitations, Game activity and Draft activity (LOB-072's three channels). They are kept in that browser and apply at once; a kind switched off is never sent to it. (r73)
